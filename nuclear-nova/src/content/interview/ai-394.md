## Question

Design a visual search system: user uploads an image, you return visually similar in-catalogue items.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: encode catalog images and uploaded queries into a compatible visual embedding space, retrieve approximate nearest neighbors, and rerank with product attributes and availability. Detect or crop the object of interest when the upload contains a scene. Similar appearance is only one dimension of product relevance; category, price, size, and user intent may change the desired ranking.

## Detailed answer

Train or adapt embeddings using product identities, variants, and hard negatives, while preventing near-duplicate leakage across evaluation splits. Maintain a versioned vector index with image quality checks and incremental updates. Filter unavailable items before presenting results and preserve image/catalog provenance.

Handle multiple objects, background clutter, low light, screenshots, and out-of-catalog queries. Use reranking or attribute extraction to distinguish visually similar but functionally different products. Measure recall at candidate depth, user-rated relevance, successful product discovery, and latency. Support abstention or a clarification when no close match exists. Protect uploaded images with a defined retention policy and avoid using them for unrelated training without authorization.

### Retrieve broadly, then verify attributes

Encode catalog images offline and build an approximate-nearest-neighbor index. At query time, detect or let the user select the relevant object, encode its crop, retrieve candidates, and rerank using visual similarity plus requested attributes. This avoids matching a sofa query primarily to the room's wall color or background texture.

Train with product identity positives and hard negatives such as nearly identical items with different functions. Deduplicate images and split by product family when measuring generalization. A visually similar shoe may be unavailable in the user's region or differ in an essential attribute, so catalog filters and attribute checks belong after retrieval and before display. Handle out-of-catalog images by showing uncertainty rather than inventing an exact match. Version the encoder with its index; embeddings from incompatible versions are not interchangeable. Measure candidate recall, final relevance, and discovery success on realistic photographs, screenshots, clutter, and poor lighting, alongside end-to-end latency.
