## Question

How is KL divergence loss different from cross-entropy loss? And from contrastive loss?

## Brief answer

For a fixed target distribution $p$, cross-entropy and forward KL differ only by the target's entropy, so they have the same gradients with respect to predicted distribution $q$. Contrastive losses instead distinguish matching from nonmatching representations or examples; they may use cross-entropy internally, but solve a different training problem.

## Detailed answer

The identities are $H(p,q)=-\sum_x p(x)\log q(x)$ and $D_{KL}(p\|q)=H(p,q)-H(p)$. For a one-hot target, $H(p)=0$. If $p$ changes with learned parameters, the supposedly constant term is no longer necessarily constant. KL is asymmetric; reversing its arguments changes the optimization behavior.

An InfoNCE-style contrastive objective treats a positive pair as the correct class among negatives using similarity scores divided by temperature. Its behavior depends on negative sampling, false negatives, batch composition, and representation normalization. Use cross-entropy for supervised categorical prediction, KL for distribution matching with an explicit direction, and contrastive learning for representation relationships. Numerical implementations require stable log probabilities and careful handling of zero target mass.

### A small distribution example

Let target p=(0.75,0.25) and prediction q=(0.5,0.5), using natural logarithms. Cross-entropy is about 0.6931 nats, target entropy about 0.5623, and forward KL about 0.1308. When optimizing q with fixed p, cross-entropy and forward KL differ only by that constant, so their gradients with respect to q's model parameters are identical.

Contrastive learning instead defines which examples should be close. An image and its matching caption form a positive pair; other captions in the batch act as negatives. Applying cross-entropy over their similarity scores produces an InfoNCE loss, so “contrastive” describes the supervision and candidate construction rather than a completely unrelated mathematical family. False negatives, such as two captions describing the same image content, can push compatible representations apart. Temperature controls the sharpness of similarity comparisons. Choose the objective according to whether you need calibrated class probabilities, teacher-distribution matching, or reusable embeddings.
