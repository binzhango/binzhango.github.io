## Question

Explain GRPO and why dropping the value network matters at scale.

## Brief answer

GRPO estimates relative advantages from a group of sampled responses to the same prompt instead of training a separate value network. Removing the critic reduces memory and training complexity, but rollout generation and reward quality still dominate much of the cost.

## Detailed answer

A basic group advantage subtracts the group’s mean reward and scales by its variability; policy-ratio clipping and a reference constraint can stabilize updates. Groups with identical rewards provide little useful relative signal. Reward normalization, response length and clipping choices affect behavior, and variants differ. Check correctness, length and diversity rather than reward alone.

Suppose four completions for one prompt receive rewards $[0,0,1,1]$. Their mean is 0.5; using the population standard deviation of 0.5 gives relative advantages $[-1,-1,1,1]$. The successful completions are encouraged relative to unsuccessful peers from the same prompt. If every completion receives zero, subtracting the group mean yields no useful distinction, so increasing rollout diversity or changing the task curriculum may be necessary.

A value network normally predicts expected future reward so a policy can estimate whether an outcome was better than expected. GRPO replaces that learned baseline with group comparisons. This avoids storing and training a separate critic, but requires multiple generated responses per prompt. The net savings depend on model size, group size, sequence length and whether rollout generation dominates.

Practical variants differ in token versus sequence weighting, reward normalization, clipping and reference-policy regularization. These details can introduce length or difficulty biases; a higher average reward may coincide with unnecessarily long answers. Inspect reward distributions, zero-variance groups, response lengths, diversity and held-out correctness. The absence of a critic does not make reward design trivial, and it does not establish that every sampled explanation is valid merely because its final answer receives a reward.
