## Question

What are the problems with vector-based context augmentation?

## Brief answer

Vector retrieval can miss exact identifiers, relational constraints, multi-hop connections, and facts distributed across chunks.

## Detailed answer

Semantic similarity does not guarantee answer support. A document about the right topic can concern the wrong date, entity, or exception. Dense indexes also require coordinated updates when embeddings or sources change. Combine lexical search, metadata filters, structured queries, graph traversal, or parent expansion where evaluation shows a gap. These complementary methods have their own costs and should not be added without a demonstrated need.

A dense score captures learned similarity, not a logical predicate. A passage about product A's 2024 policy may be close to a question about product A's 2025 policy while being unusable as the answer. Likewise, embedding “service X depends on Y” and “Y is owned by team Z” separately does not automatically perform the join needed to answer who owns X's dependency.

Exact constraints need explicit handling where possible. Use metadata filters for date and product version, lexical matching for identifiers, and structured queries for numeric ranges or joins. For relational questions, graph traversal can connect evidence; for fragmented explanations, parent or neighboring-context expansion can restore meaning.

These additions do not make vectors unnecessary. Semantic search remains useful for paraphrase and broad candidate recall, while the other signals address distinctions it may blur. Evaluate each against observed errors. Also manage index freshness and representation compatibility: outdated vectors or mixed embedding versions can undermine retrieval independently of model quality. Inspect answer support in the returned passages rather than interpreting a high similarity score as a confidence probability.
