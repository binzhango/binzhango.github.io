## Question

How do LLMs make generated text diverse and non-repetitive?

## Brief answer

Sampling temperature, top-k or top-p filtering, and repetition controls change output diversity, while training quality and prompting influence coherence.

## Detailed answer

Temperature rescales logits before sampling: lower values concentrate probability and higher values flatten it. Top-p retains a high-probability set whose cumulative mass reaches a threshold. Repetition penalties or n-gram constraints can break loops but can also damage names, code, or necessary repeated terms. Greater randomness is not guaranteed to improve usefulness. Tune decoding against task-specific quality and repetition metrics.

With logits $(2,1,0)$, temperature 1 produces probabilities roughly $(0.665,0.245,0.090)$. Temperature 0.5 divides logits by 0.5, making the distribution more concentrated, while temperature 2 flattens it. Temperature zero is commonly implemented as greedy selection rather than literal division by zero. Temperature changes uncertainty in sampling; it does not make the model's beliefs more factually grounded.

Top-k keeps a fixed number of candidates, whereas top-p keeps a variable-size prefix of the sorted distribution. If probabilities are $(0.6,0.25,0.10,0.05)$ and top-p is 0.8, the first two tokens suffice and are renormalized before sampling. This adapts the number of candidates to how concentrated the distribution is.

For creative text, modest randomness may produce useful alternatives. For extraction, unnecessary diversity can violate a schema or alter exact values. Repetition controls can help loops but may break legitimate repetitions in code, quotations, or names. Evaluate several seeded outputs per prompt, track both diversity and correctness, and preserve appropriate stop tokens and maximum lengths.
