## Question

Tell me about a performance optimisation you shipped. Give me the numbers, and tell me why I should believe them.

## Brief answer

Response framework: present a real optimization with a reproducible baseline, a specific bottleneck hypothesis, and measurements under comparable conditions. Give both absolute values and relative improvement, plus quality and resource trade-offs. Explain why the result was caused by the change rather than warm caches, different load, or selective benchmarking.

## Detailed answer

State workload, hardware, software versions, concurrency, input/output sizes, and measurement method. Show the profiler evidence that guided the change and distinguish microbenchmark gains from end-to-end improvement. Use multiple runs, confidence or variability information, and tail latency when users care about it.

Describe correctness checks, rollout, and whether the benefit persisted in production. Include regressions or constraints, such as higher memory use or gains only above a certain batch size. If exact numbers are confidential, use honestly rounded ratios and explain the restriction. Do not invent a before/after result; the credibility comes from experimental discipline and ownership of the trade-off, not an impressive percentage.

### Connect the number to the mechanism

**Illustrative structure:** “The profile showed repeated serialization consuming 40% of request time. Reusing an immutable representation reduced that component, while end-to-end median latency improved less because model execution remained.” Supply your actual measurements rather than adopting these numbers. Amdahl's law limits a change that speeds only one fraction of the path; eliminating 40% entirely cannot exceed a 1/0.6≈1.67× overall speedup.

Describe a controlled before/after comparison with warmup, realistic input sizes, concurrency, and enough repetitions to characterize variance. Include p95/p99 if users experience tails, and check memory or CPU regressions. Explain correctness verification and rollout controls, then state whether the effect persisted under production traffic. If workload changed at the same time, distinguish correlation from causal evidence. Be ready to reproduce the result or show an anonymized trace; credibility comes from a transparent denominator and known limits, not a dramatic isolated benchmark.
