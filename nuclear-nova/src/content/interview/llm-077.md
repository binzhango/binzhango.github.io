## Question

What is the repetition problem (LLM echo problem)? Why does it happen? How can it be mitigated?

## Brief answer

The repetition problem is unwanted looping or excessive reuse of phrases, caused by interactions among the learned distribution, prompt, and decoding procedure.

## Detailed answer

Greedy or poorly tuned decoding can repeatedly enter high-probability continuations. Repeated training data, malformed chat templates, missing stop tokens, or prompt echoing can contribute. Inspect whether repetition appears in the model output itself or in the application's streaming assembly. Try appropriate stop handling, corrected formatting, better data, and measured sampling or penalties. Raising temperature blindly can replace repetition with incoherence.

First locate the layer creating the repetition. If raw generated tokens are correct but the displayed answer repeats prefixes, the streaming client may be appending cumulative chunks instead of deltas. If the raw model output repeats, inspect the serialized prompt, stop conditions, and token probabilities near the start of the loop. Accidentally including the assistant's response multiple times can itself invite repetition.

A model-generated loop can reinforce itself: after emitting a phrase, the repeated context makes another copy a high-probability continuation. Greedy decoding repeatedly chooses that continuation. Sampling or a carefully tuned repetition penalty may escape it, but penalties operate on token history rather than understanding which repetition is useful.

For an exact-copy task, repeated identifiers are legitimate, so a blanket no-repeat rule can corrupt the answer. For dialogue, missing end-of-turn handling may cause the model to continue inventing further turns. Fix format and stopping bugs first, then compare decoding settings on representative prompts. Persistent task-specific loops may indicate poor or repetitive tuning data. Measure loop frequency and completion correctness together instead of accepting more random output as a successful fix.
