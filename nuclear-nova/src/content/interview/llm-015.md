## Question

How does dropout approximate an ensemble, and when is the geometric-mean equivalence exact?

## Brief answer

Dropout trains shared-weight subnetworks and approximates their ensemble at inference. Exact equivalence is limited to special cases; the relevant concept is a geometric mean, not a geometric distribution.

## Detailed answer

Let $m_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(q)$ and let a softmax linear model have logits $z_m=W(m\odot x)+b$. Define its normalized weighted geometric ensemble by:

$$
\widetilde p_k=\frac{\exp(\mathbb E_m[\log p_m(k)])}{\sum_j\exp(\mathbb E_m[\log p_m(j)])}
=\operatorname{softmax}(\mathbb E_m[z_m])_k
=\operatorname{softmax}(W(qx)+b)_k.
$$

The expected log-normalizer cancels across classes. This is not the arithmetic average $\mathbb E_m[p_m]$. In a nonlinear deep network, replacing every random mask by its mean generally does not reproduce the expected logits, so the exact argument fails. Modern inverted dropout scales retained activations by $1/q$ during training and makes dropout an identity at evaluation.

The ensemble interpretation comes from training on many masks while sharing the same weights. With two independently retained units, four subnetworks are possible; with many units, explicitly training each subnetwork separately would be infeasible. Dropout instead samples masks during optimization, encouraging a useful prediction even when some intermediate features are unavailable.

For inverted dropout with keep probability $q=0.8$, a retained activation is multiplied by $1/0.8=1.25$, and a dropped activation becomes zero. Its expectation equals the original activation: $0.8(1.25x)+0.2(0)=x$. That equality concerns the local activation, not the final output after subsequent nonlinear layers.

The geometric-ensemble identity above is exact because a linear logit is linear in the mask and the class-independent normalizer cancels when renormalized. An arithmetic average of probabilities generally gives a different result. At ordinary evaluation, disable dropout; leaving it enabled makes predictions stochastic. Deliberately repeating stochastic evaluations can be an uncertainty heuristic, but variation across masks is not automatically a calibrated probability of being wrong.
