## Question

You're building a web-scale pretraining corpus (FineWeb-style). Walk me through the pipeline and how you decide whether each filter earns its place.

## Brief answer

Build a reproducible pipeline from licensed or otherwise permitted web sources through extraction, language identification, quality filtering, deduplication, privacy handling, and mixture construction. A filter earns its place through controlled training/evaluation ablations and coverage analysis, not because its name sounds like “quality.” Every filter can remove useful minority-language, stylistic, or domain content.

## Detailed answer

Retain source and processing provenance, crawl time, language confidence, and rejection reasons. Remove boilerplate and malformed text, identify exact and near duplicates across documents and snapshots, and audit sensitive information and benchmark overlap. Sample both retained and rejected data for human review to expose systematic mistakes.

Train matched small-scale proxy runs with and without each filter, holding token budget and evaluation protocol constant. Compare loss and downstream tasks by language/domain, plus data diversity and yield. Revalidate at larger scale because proxy rankings may change. Keep test corpora independent and publish enough pipeline detail to reproduce decisions. FineWeb's creators document a concrete sequence of data ablations and curation choices.

### A filter must earn its discarded tokens

The ingestion pipeline extracts text from crawls, removes markup and repeated boilerplate, detects language, applies quality and privacy rules, deduplicates, and packages versioned training shards. Record rejection reasons and inspect samples on both sides of each threshold. A filter that removes unusual punctuation might improve average cleanliness while disproportionately deleting code or a particular language.

Evaluate each filter with controlled ablations: train comparable proxy models on equal token budgets drawn from the retained mixture and a baseline. If a filter changes both quality and domain composition, separate those effects where possible. Measure downstream tasks, heldout loss, diversity, and token yield. Near-duplicate removal reduces repeated exposure but needs thresholds that do not erase legitimately different documents. Decontamination should include evaluation variants where feasible. Keep data lineage through all transformations so a discovered source issue can be traced to affected shards. Large-scale curation is an empirical optimization process with value judgments and trade-offs, not a universal sequence of heuristics that always improves every model.
