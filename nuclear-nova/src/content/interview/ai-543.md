## Question

A contact centre wants to replace its IVR with voice agents. Run the engagement.

## Brief answer

Run the engagement as workflow redesign with measured service outcomes, not a voice-demo installation. Map why callers contact the center, what the current IVR and agents do, and which tasks can be safely automated. Pilot a bounded intent with clear fallback and compare customer resolution and operator workload against the baseline.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** First inventory telephony integration, authentication, languages, peak concurrency, business APIs, and required disclosures. Define policy ownership and success metrics with operations. Build a sandbox with realistic failures and test call routing, barge-in, transfers, and transaction confirmation. Use recorded or simulated examples only under approved handling rules.

Shadow or assist agents before allowing autonomous completion, then canary a limited traffic segment. Monitor resolution, repeat calls, abandonment, handoff completeness, latency, and critical errors. Keep the old routing path available for rollback. Train staff on new escalation reasons and review samples jointly. Expansion depends on sustained quality and support readiness, not a high containment rate that merely prevents callers from reaching help.

### Tie the rollout to resolved customer work

Choose one common intent with a reliable backend API and a reversible outcome, such as checking an existing order's status. Document its identity requirements, exceptional cases, escalation owner, and definition of completion. Build an evaluation set from representative calls, including accents, noisy lines, corrections, and requests outside the intended workflow.

During a limited pilot, compare matched traffic with the existing service. Track first-contact resolution and repeat contacts over an appropriate window, not just whether the agent ended a call without transferring. A caller who gives up and calls again may appear “contained” while experiencing worse service. Measure the quality of transferred context and whether a human can finish without repeating the entire interview.

Make operational readiness a release gate: telephony failover, queue routing, credentials, alerting, incident ownership, rollback, and staff training must be exercised. Include API outages and ambiguous transaction outcomes in rehearsals. A voice demo rarely reveals these failure paths.

Review costs per resolved task, including model and telephony usage, human rescue, monitoring, and ongoing workflow maintenance. Expand by intent and traffic segment rather than replacing all IVR routes at once. Retain a straightforward human-help route and use recurring error analysis to decide whether the next improvement belongs in conversation design, backend reliability, or policy clarification.
