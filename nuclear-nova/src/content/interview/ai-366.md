## Question

A network confuses pugs and pit bulls and some training labels are wrong. How do you modify the model and the data?

## Brief answer

Investigate the errors before increasing model size. Confusing similar breeds may reflect ambiguous images, label noise, insufficient examples, or shortcuts such as background rather than inadequate capacity. Build a carefully reviewed test set, repair high-impact labels, and train with appropriate augmentation and a loss robust to residual noise.

## Detailed answer

Inspect a stratified sample of false positives and false negatives, including image quality, age, pose, mixed breeds, and annotator disagreement. Define a labeling policy for genuinely ambiguous cases instead of forcing certainty. Use expert review or multiple annotations where needed, and prevent near-duplicate photos from leaking across splits.

Improve cropping or localization if background dominates, add diverse hard examples, and consider a pretrained visual backbone with calibrated outputs. Label smoothing or noise-aware methods can reduce memorization, but cannot replace correct labels. Allow abstention or a broader category when visual evidence is insufficient. Evaluate per-class recall and calibration on independent sources; do not treat a model's disagreement with a label as automatic proof the label is wrong.

### Diagnose whether the evidence is visual or spurious

If most pug images were taken indoors and pit bull images outdoors, a model may learn background rather than breed-related appearance. Evaluate cropped subjects and changed backgrounds to test that hypothesis. If errors concentrate in puppies or mixed breeds, the class definition may be ambiguous rather than the network too small. Introduce an explicit uncertain or mixed category only if it matches the product's actual needs.

Use a trusted adjudicated test set before relabeling training data. Review disagreement clusters with annotators and maintain correction history. Hard-example mining should include genuinely confusing correct labels and mislabeled examples separately; blindly training harder on wrong labels reinforces noise. A pretrained backbone with adequate image resolution and balanced sampling may outperform a new architecture trained on poor labels. Evaluate precision, recall, and calibration by pose, source, age, and image quality. For consequential use, allow abstention and explain the limits of visual breed classification instead of treating every photo as uniquely identifiable.
