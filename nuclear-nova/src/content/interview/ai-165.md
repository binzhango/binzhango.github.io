## Question

If the accuracy of a classifier is 1, what is the lower/upper bound on the loss function for a single training example?

## Brief answer

Perfect classification accuracy does not determine the loss without specifying the loss function and decision rule. For unregularized K-class cross-entropy on a correctly predicted example, the true-class probability is at least 1/K, so loss lies between 0 and log K, with tie conventions affecting whether the upper bound is attained. Other losses have different bounds.

## Detailed answer

Cross-entropy for true class y is $L=-\log p_y$. If argmax predicts y, no other probability exceeds $p_y$, hence $1=\sum_k p_k\le Kp_y$. Therefore $p_y\ge1/K$ and $L\le\log K$. A probability of one gives zero loss; finite softmax logits usually approach rather than attain it.

For binary classification with a 0.5 threshold, the analogous range is zero to $\log 2$. With a nonstandard threshold, label smoothing, sample weights or regularization, these bounds change. A model can predict correctly with low confidence and still incur substantial loss.

For zero-one loss, correct classification gives exactly zero. For a custom loss on unbounded scores, accuracy may impose no useful finite upper bound. Also distinguish perfect empirical accuracy from accuracy over the entire data distribution; the former says nothing about unseen examples.

Take a three-class prediction `[0.34,0.33,0.33]` with the first class correct. Accuracy for that example is one, but cross-entropy is $-\log0.34\approx1.079$, close to $\log3\approx1.099$. A much more confident correct prediction `[0.98,0.01,0.01]` has loss about 0.0202. Accuracy collapses both to the same binary result, while cross-entropy distinguishes confidence.

The upper bound follows only when the decision is an argmax over a normalized K-class distribution and the loss is the unsmoothed negative log-probability of the true class. For binary thresholding at a different cutoff, a positive prediction can be correct with probability below 0.5, changing the bound. Adding weight decay contributes a parameter-dependent term unrelated to that example's accuracy.

Clarify whether “accuracy is 1” means this one prediction or all examples in a finite training set. Even perfect training accuracy does not bound held-out loss or establish generalization. State the assumptions before giving a numerical bound rather than treating accuracy and loss as interchangeable metrics.
