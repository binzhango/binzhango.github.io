## Question

AutoGLM and CogAgent operate real GUIs from screenshots over tens of steps. Design the agent: perception, action space, and error recovery for a 50-step task.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: Use a closed-loop agent: observe the screen, choose one bounded action, execute it, and verify the resulting state before continuing. A 50-step task needs explicit subgoals, durable progress, permission checks, and recovery from changed screens. A confident-looking click sequence without feedback will compound small errors into failure.

## Detailed answer

Convert screenshots and available accessibility information into elements with coordinates, labels, and confidence. Expose a narrow action space such as click, type, scroll, back, and wait. Tie coordinates to a screenshot version and reacquire after layout changes. Prefer semantic elements when reliable, with visual grounding as a fallback.

Track subgoal completion using observed evidence: an order receipt, saved setting, or newly created item. Detect loops and no-op actions, set retry and time budgets, and recover to a known screen. Require explicit authorization for purchases or messages and pause for credentials, CAPTCHA, or ambiguous recipients. Test interruptions, pop-ups, loading states, and localization. Evaluate complete-task success and unintended side effects, not per-click accuracy alone.

### Reliability compounds over fifty steps

If each independent action succeeds with probability 0.98 and no recovery exists, fifty actions succeed together with probability $0.98^{50}\approx0.364$. Real errors are correlated, but the calculation shows why improving individual clicks is insufficient. Break the task into verified milestones: navigate to settings, locate the target account, change one value, then read back the saved value.

Persist a task state containing the current subgoal, last observation identifier, actions already attempted, and confirmed side effects. After a timeout, observe before retrying; the click may have succeeded even if its acknowledgment was lost. Use bounded recovery paths such as returning to a known screen, closing an obstructing dialog, or asking about an ambiguous choice. Avoid replaying the entire task when only its last step failed. Evaluate recovery success and duplicate-action rate using injected slow loads and layout changes, because a clean fifty-step demo says little about routine operation.
