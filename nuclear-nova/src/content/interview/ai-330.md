## Question

What are the differences between L1 and L2 regularization in logistic regression?

## Brief answer

L1 adds a penalty proportional to absolute coefficient values and can drive coefficients exactly to zero. L2 penalizes squared magnitude and usually shrinks correlated coefficients without selecting a sparse subset. Both can reduce overfitting in logistic regression, but their behavior depends on feature scaling, penalty strength, and optimization method.

## Detailed answer

For logistic negative log likelihood $L(\beta)$, common objectives are $L+\lambda\|\beta\|_1$ and $L+\frac\lambda2\|\beta\|_2^2$. L1 is nondifferentiable at zero, so proximal or coordinate methods handle its thresholding behavior. L2 has a smooth gradient and improves conditioning.

With highly correlated predictors, L1 may arbitrarily retain one, making selected features unstable across samples; L2 tends to distribute weight among them. Elastic net combines both when sparse grouped behavior is useful. Standardize numerical features using training data only, choose strength with appropriate cross-validation, and evaluate calibration as well as discrimination. Do not interpret a zeroed coefficient as proof that a variable is causally irrelevant. Avoid penalizing the intercept unless there is an explicit reason.

### The update reveals the difference

For an L2 penalty, the gradient adds $\lambda\beta$, continuously shrinking coefficients toward zero. For L1, a proximal step applies soft thresholding to a gradient-updated coefficient u: $\operatorname{sign}(u)\max(|u|-\eta\lambda,0)$. That interval mapping to exactly zero explains why L1 performs feature selection while ordinary L2 usually leaves small nonzero weights.

If income is measured in dollars and age in years, identical penalty strength has different effects unless scaling is handled. Standardize using training statistics and apply the same transformation during serving. With two nearly identical predictors, sparse selection can switch between them across samples even though prediction is stable; do not overinterpret which one survives. Tune the penalty against heldout log loss or the decision metric, not training fit. Under complete separation, regularization can also prevent coefficients from growing without bound, but existence and uniqueness depend on which parameters are penalized.
