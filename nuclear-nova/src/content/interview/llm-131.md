## Question

How to optimize generator adaptation to inputs?

## Brief answer

Adapt the generator to use supplied evidence through clear prompting, relevant demonstrations, or grounded SFT, and evaluate its behavior under imperfect retrieval.

## Detailed answer

Train or test on sufficient evidence, missing evidence, distractors, contradictions, and unanswerable questions. Require citations where appropriate and teach calibrated abstention rather than guessing. Avoid datasets where the model can ignore retrieval and answer from memorized shortcuts. Generator adaptation cannot repair an absent source reliably; use error analysis to decide whether the bottleneck is evidence availability or evidence use.

Construct examples that force the model to use evidence. If every training question is answerable from common knowledge, a generator can ignore retrieved text and still receive good supervision. Include fictional or newly supplied facts, version-specific information, and questions whose answer changes with the provided passage. This tests whether the model follows the context rather than a memorized response.

Teach three distinct behaviors: answer when support is sufficient, identify conflicts when sources disagree, and explain the missing information when support is insufficient. Include distracting but related passages and negative statements so the model learns to preserve qualifications. Citation IDs should refer to supplied evidence, and support checks should verify the claim rather than merely the existence of the ID.

Start with prompting and demonstrations before training. If grounded SFT is justified, keep source documents separated across splits and evaluate on unfamiliar wording. Compare with an oracle-context baseline to ensure retrieval is not the main issue. Generator adaptation cannot infer an absent private fact reliably; its value is better use of available information and calibrated behavior when that information is incomplete.
