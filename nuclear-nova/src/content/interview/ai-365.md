## Question

Summarize the differences and benefits of the Adam optimizer compared with other methods for neural-network image classification.

## Brief answer

Adam maintains moving averages of gradients and squared gradients, then uses bias-corrected estimates to adapt each parameter's step size. It often trains quickly with little tuning, but uses extra optimizer memory and does not universally generalize better than momentum SGD. AdamW separates weight decay from the adaptive gradient update, making regularization behavior easier to control.

## Detailed answer

The moments are $m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t$ and $v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2$. Bias correction compensates for initialization at zero; the update divides the corrected first moment by the square root of the corrected second moment plus a stabilizer.

For image classification, compare optimizers at matched training budgets with suitable learning-rate schedules, warmup, augmentation, and regularization. SGD with momentum may perform competitively and uses less state; Adam can help heterogeneous or noisy gradients. Do not change optimizer while leaving all hyperparameters fixed and call that a fair comparison. Check validation accuracy, calibration, convergence speed, and memory.

### Adaptive steps are useful but not free

Adam divides each parameter's smoothed gradient by a smoothed estimate of its recent magnitude. Parameters with consistently large gradients receive smaller normalized steps, while sparse or differently scaled coordinates can still progress. Bias correction uses $\hat m_t=m_t/(1-\beta_1^t)$ and $\hat v_t=v_t/(1-\beta_2^t)$ so the initial zero state does not systematically shrink early estimates.

SGD with momentum stores one velocity buffer and follows a shared learning-rate scale; Adam typically stores two moment buffers, increasing memory. AdamW applies weight decay separately from the adaptive gradient update, avoiding the coupling between an L2 penalty and per-coordinate scaling. For image classification, either family can work well with appropriate schedules and augmentation. Compare time to a target validation accuracy, final generalization, calibration, and peak memory. A faster initial loss drop does not establish a better final model, and using SGD's learning rate unchanged for Adam is not a meaningful comparison.
