## Question

What if the exponential term in softmax overflows the float limit?

## Brief answer

Subtract the maximum logit before exponentiating, and compute log probabilities with a stable log-softmax or log-sum-exp operation.

## Detailed answer

Let $a=\max_jz_j$. Then:

$$
\log\sum_je^{z_j}=a+\log\sum_je^{z_j-a},\qquad
p_i=\frac{e^{z_i-a}}{\sum_je^{z_j-a}}.
$$

Every shifted exponent is at most 1, preventing overflow for finite logits. This does not repair logits that already contain infinity or NaN; inspect the earlier computation. Very negative terms can underflow harmlessly in the sum, whereas taking `log(softmax(...))` can create unwanted negative infinities. Fused loss functions avoid that unstable path.

Take logits $(1000,999)$. Direct exponentiation overflows common floating-point types, but subtracting the maximum gives $(0,-1)$. The exponentials become $(1,e^{-1})$, so softmax is approximately $(0.7311,0.2689)$. Adding or subtracting the same finite constant from all logits does not change the normalized probabilities because the common exponential factor cancels.

If the target is the second class, compute loss as $\log(1+e^{-1})+1\approx1.3133$ rather than constructing a potentially rounded probability and taking its logarithm afterward. Log-softmax and fused cross-entropy preserve this stable path.

Masking needs care. A row whose entries are all negative infinity has no valid attention target; subtracting its maximum does not solve the problem. Similarly, an infinity created earlier by an overflowing matrix multiplication is not repaired by the softmax trick. Validate finite activations, accumulation precision, and mask construction upstream. Very small finite probabilities may underflow when explicitly materialized, while their log probabilities remain representable, which is another reason to keep computations in the log domain for losses.
