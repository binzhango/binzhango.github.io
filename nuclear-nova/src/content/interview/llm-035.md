## Question

What’s the Swish formula?

## Brief answer

Swish is $\operatorname{Swish}_\beta(x)=x\,\sigma(\beta x)$; the common $\beta=1$ case is SiLU.

## Detailed answer

Here $\sigma(u)=1/(1+e^{-u})$ is the logistic sigmoid. Swish is smooth and non-monotonic: small negative inputs can produce negative outputs, while large positive inputs approach the identity. Some definitions allow a learned or fixed $\beta$; many Transformer implementations simply use SiLU with $\beta=1$. A SwiGLU FFN combines that activation with a separate multiplicative projection.

With $\beta=1$, evaluate SiLU at three simple points: $\mathrm{SiLU}(0)=0$, $\mathrm{SiLU}(1)\approx0.7311$, and $\mathrm{SiLU}(-1)\approx-0.2689$. The sigmoid factor suppresses strongly negative values and leaves strongly positive values almost unchanged. Unlike sigmoid itself, the resulting activation is not bounded above.

The derivative is $\sigma(\beta x)+\beta x\sigma(\beta x)(1-\sigma(\beta x))$. At zero it equals one half. For some negative inputs it becomes negative, explaining the non-monotonic region. As a positive beta grows very large, the function approaches ReLU away from zero; at beta zero it is simply $x/2$.

A model using SiLU in an ordinary two-projection FFN is different from a SwiGLU model. SwiGLU multiplies the activated branch by another learned projection, creating feature-dependent interactions before the output projection. Preserve the checkpoint's definition and surrounding block structure. Smoothness can help optimization in a particular setting, but it does not guarantee a superior model or remove the need for normalization and suitable initialization.
