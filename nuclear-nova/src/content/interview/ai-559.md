## Question

Design the teleoperation data pipeline. Why is data collection the bottleneck in robotics rather than compute?

## Brief answer

Build a synchronized, calibrated, versioned data pipeline that connects operator intent, robot observations, commanded actions, actual state, and outcomes. Collection is often the bottleneck because each useful episode needs physical setup, reset, maintenance, and quality control. Compute cannot recover missing timestamps, unsafe demonstrations, or absent task diversity.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** At the station, record clocks, camera calibration, joint conventions, embodiment/firmware, task instruction, operator actions, and safety interventions. Validate dropped frames, clock drift, sensor health, and action bounds before accepting an episode. Upload immutable artifacts with manifests and checksums, then annotate outcome and failure cause.

Schedule data collection around policy failure clusters, not just convenient tasks. Track accepted episodes per robot-hour, reset time, operator fatigue, and performance gain per new data tranche. Separate evaluation sites and objects from training. Retain failed and recovered attempts with accurate labels. The best operational improvement may be a faster safe reset procedure or clearer task specification rather than more GPUs.

### Measure useful data yield, not recording duration

An eight-hour collection shift might spend time on task setup, resets, calibration, operator breaks, and failed uploads. Suppose four hours contain demonstrations and 75% of those pass quality checks; only three hours become accepted training data. This hypothetical yield calculation explains why another GPU cannot recover the lost collection time.

Validate episodes close to the station so operators can correct a loose camera or clock drift before an entire day is recorded. Preserve both commanded and measured actions: a saturated actuator may not execute the operator's command, and training against the wrong interpretation creates inconsistent targets. Keep intervention and reset boundaries explicit rather than joining unrelated motions into one episode.

Use a manifest to bind sensor files, calibration, timestamps, instructions, outcomes, and hardware versions. Upload resumably, verify checksums, and publish an episode only when its required artifacts are complete. Version subsequent annotations instead of overwriting the raw record.

Drive the collection queue from policy failures and missing coverage. Track accepted episodes per robot-hour and improvement on held-out tasks per collection tranche. Operator skill, reproducible setup, and safe reset efficiency can dominate progress. Compute becomes valuable once the pipeline supplies diverse, synchronized, correctly labeled experience; scaling corrupted or repetitive data simply trains the same limitations faster.
