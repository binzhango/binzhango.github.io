## Question

How would you improve product search results, focusing on the fraction of relevant documents retrieved (recall)?

## Brief answer

Improve recall by measuring which relevant products never reach the candidate set, then broaden retrieval without overwhelming ranking. Combine lexical, semantic and structured retrieval, fix indexing and query understanding errors, and tune ANN search depth. A reranker cannot recover a product that candidate generation never retrieved.

## Detailed answer

Build a judged query set covering exact identifiers, synonyms, misspellings, attributes and long-tail intents. Inspect failures by stage: absent inventory, bad metadata, tokenization, filters, lexical mismatch or approximate-vector misses. Compare approximate search with exact search on a manageable sample to isolate index loss.

Add complementary candidate sources and deduplicate their union. Tune synonyms and query expansion cautiously; increasing candidate count can introduce irrelevant products and latency. Enforce hard user constraints such as size or availability at the appropriate stage rather than treating every mismatch as a recall failure.

Measure recall at the candidate budget and final ranking metrics separately. Include head and tail queries, categories and languages. Run online tests on successful product discovery, not only clicks, and monitor zero-result rates and latency. The best fix may be catalog quality rather than a new embedding model.

Build a stage-by-stage recall ledger. Is the relevant product in the catalog? Is its metadata correct? Does it survive availability and compatibility filters? Does lexical or dense retrieval return it? Does the approximate index lose it compared with exact search? Each “no” points to a different intervention, and a larger reranker cannot repair any product absent from its candidate set.

Use complementary query handling: exact matching for SKUs, spelling correction for typos, curated synonyms for domain terms and embeddings for descriptive intent. Query expansion should preserve hard constraints; broadening “USB-C charger” to every charging accessory can inflate recall while destroying usefulness.

Measure candidate recall at fixed budgets, then final ranking and successful discovery. Increase ANN search depth or candidate counts only while the marginal gain justifies latency and reranking cost. Inspect long-tail and multilingual slices separately from popular queries. If missing attributes or stale inventory dominate failures, invest in catalog ingestion and validation before replacing the retrieval model.
