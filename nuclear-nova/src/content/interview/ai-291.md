## Question

Why does long-horizon agentic RL need a disaggregated, asynchronous design (as in the slime framework) rather than colocated-synchronous?

## Brief answer

Asynchronous, disaggregated RL can keep trainers busy while variable-length agents wait for tools and environments. Rollout and optimization workers have different resource needs, and synchronous rounds otherwise wait for the slowest trajectory. The trade-off is stale policies, more complex bookkeeping, and harder reproducibility; colocated synchronous training remains reasonable for small, predictable workloads.

## Detailed answer

Separate environment orchestration, inference workers, reward computation, and training. Queue completed trajectories with model version, log probabilities, tool observations, and verifier version. Bound policy lag and use an algorithm appropriate for the resulting off-policy data; merely calling it asynchronous does not make stale samples valid.

Apply backpressure so rollout queues cannot grow without limit. Treat failed tools and timeouts consistently in reward computation, and checkpoint both training state and queue provenance. Measure trainer utilization, rollout tail latency, accepted-sample age, and reward per unit compute. Slime supports flexible rollout/training arrangements; it does not prove that every long-horizon workload requires one configuration.

### Where synchronous execution wastes time

Suppose most agent trajectories finish in ten seconds, but a few wait sixty seconds for tests or external tools. A synchronous batch cannot update until its slowest required trajectory completes, leaving otherwise available resources idle. Disaggregated workers can keep generating other trajectories while trainers consume completed work; this decouples environment latency from training cadence.

The cost is policy staleness. A trajectory generated by an older policy may no longer represent the current action distribution. Store the generating policy version and the log probabilities required by the chosen objective, cap accepted age, and discard or appropriately handle samples outside that contract. Separate queues by workload when long tasks starve short ones, and bound queue memory. Colocation can still be efficient for short homogeneous tasks because it reduces weight-transfer and orchestration overhead. Choose asynchronous execution after measuring idle time and acceptable lag, rather than treating it as an architectural requirement for every RL job.
