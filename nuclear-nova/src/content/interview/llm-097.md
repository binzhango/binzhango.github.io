## Question

During which stage — pretraining or fine-tuning — is knowledge injected?

## Brief answer

Both pretraining and fine-tuning can update factual knowledge in model parameters, while retrieval supplies information at inference without updating those parameters.

## Detailed answer

Pretraining usually provides the broadest exposure to facts and language. Continued pretraining and SFT can add or modify knowledge, though reliable recall and compositional use are not guaranteed. A model answering from a retrieved document is using contextual evidence, not necessarily permanently learning it. Choose among these mechanisms based on how often information changes, how it must be attributed, and how errors will be corrected.

There are three mechanisms to distinguish. Pretraining or continued pretraining updates weights through prediction on broad text. SFT updates weights through desired input-response examples. In-context evidence changes the current computation without updating weights, unless a separate learning pipeline later trains on that interaction.

Suppose a product's warranty changes from one year to two. Continued training or SFT can encourage the new answer, but testing must check old phrasing, paraphrases, and conflicting contexts to determine whether the update is reliable. Retrieval can instead supply the current policy and its effective date on each request, making the answer easier to update and attribute. The model must still read the evidence correctly.

Knowledge and behavior are not perfectly separable in parameters. A demonstration can teach both a new fact and a pattern for answering, while pretraining can teach procedural behavior from text. Avoid claims that one stage “only teaches format” or that another permanently stores every fact it sees. Choose the update mechanism according to freshness, provenance, cost, and observed generalization rather than treating a training-stage label as a guarantee.
