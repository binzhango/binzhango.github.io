## Question

Tell me about a technical misjudgement that delayed a project.

## Brief answer

Use a real technical decision that caused a delay, explain the mistaken assumption and show how you detected and corrected it. Own your role without turning the story into either self-exoneration or exaggerated blame. The strongest evidence of learning is a concrete change to how you validate similar decisions now.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: describe the intended plan and why it initially seemed reasonable. Identify the specific misjudgment—underestimated migration complexity, an untested dependency, unrealistic data quality or a misleading benchmark—and separate it from unrelated events.

Explain the signal that revealed the problem, when you communicated it and how you revised scope or sequencing. Include the cost of the correction and how you protected the team's most important commitments. If other people warned you, acknowledge that and explain why you did not act sooner.

Close with a practical prevention mechanism such as an early integration test, a risk review or a rollback rehearsal. Avoid a disguised-strength example like “I cared too much about quality.” Use your actual experience; do not borrow an illustrative incident as a personal story.

An illustrative incident would be choosing a new storage component after a throughput benchmark, then discovering that migration and recovery requirements delayed the release. A strong account explains why the initial benchmark seemed relevant, which untested assumption failed, and how the owner changed the plan. It should not imply that such an incident happened to the author unless it did.

In a real answer, be precise about communication: when the risk became visible, who needed to know, and what alternatives were offered. “We worked harder” is weaker than explaining a concrete recovery decision such as reducing scope, adding a compatibility layer or reverting to a known component. State what delay was attributable to the decision rather than claiming every schedule change had one cause.

The lesson should alter future behavior. An early restore drill, integration spike or explicit exit criterion is stronger than “I learned to plan better.” Explain how that change was later used, or honestly say it is the process you would apply next time.
