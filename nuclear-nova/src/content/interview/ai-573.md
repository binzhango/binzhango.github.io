## Question

Design a system that finds driving segments similar to a given one across the entire fleet archive.

## Brief answer

Index driving segments using a combination of semantic embeddings and structured scene descriptors, then rerank candidates with temporal and geometric similarity. A visually similar clip may describe a different driving interaction, so define whether similarity means appearance, actor motion, road topology, or causal scenario.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Partition logs into overlapping windows and attach location, weather, map topology, ego maneuver, actor tracks, and event labels. Compute embeddings from synchronized sensor or scene representations and store links to immutable source logs. Retrieve by approximate nearest neighbors with metadata filters, then rerank using trajectory alignment or interaction features.

Evaluate with engineer-labeled similar/dissimilar pairs and downstream mining tasks. Measure recall of rare scenario variants, diversity, latency, and duplicate rate. Test viewpoint changes, different cities with the same interaction, and identical locations with different behavior. Avoid geographic leakage in evaluation. Access controls and retention must apply to the returned raw video and location traces as well as the index.

### Define similarity at the level of the engineering question

For “find more unprotected left turns with an oncoming cyclist,” road topology, relative actor motion, and interaction timing matter more than building appearance. For diagnosing glare, visual conditions may matter more than the maneuver. Offer query controls or separate embedding channels so one generic vector does not have to encode every notion of similarity equally.

Use a two-stage search. First, retrieve a broad candidate pool from embeddings and structured filters. Then rerank using aligned tracks, relative geometry, map context, and temporal event order. Normalize frames where appropriate so the same interaction remains comparable across camera viewpoints and cities, while retaining absolute information needed for geographic constraints.

Store immutable segment identifiers and synchronized source references. Deduplicate heavily overlapping windows and return a diverse result set, otherwise a single repeated commute can dominate the top results. Apply authorization before exposing raw video or precise location data, including through previews and cached results.

Evaluate with engineer-labeled relevance sets for distinct query types and measure rare-case recall as well as top-result precision. Include hard negatives: the same intersection with no interaction, or similar-looking traffic with different right-of-way behavior. Measure whether retrieved examples help reproduce a failure or expand a test set, rather than optimizing embedding similarity without a useful downstream task.
