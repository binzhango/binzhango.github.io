## Question

Design an end-to-end pipeline producing RLHF preference data for a frontier lab: 100k prompt-response comparisons a week, with quality guarantees.

## Brief answer

Treat preference data as a versioned measurement process: define the target rubric, stratify prompt sampling, blind response identities, collect independent judgments, and adjudicate disagreement. Guarantee traceable process quality and measured error bounds rather than promising an objective ground truth for every subjective comparison.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Partition 100,000 weekly comparisons by domain, language, difficulty, and risk. At an illustrative three minutes per comparison, one judgment per pair requires 5,000 annotation hours; multiply by overlap and adjudication before staffing. Randomize answer order, conceal model names, and record rubric version, annotator eligibility, uncertainty, and reasons. Detect presentation defects before sending tasks to people.

Use training and qualification tasks, expert-reviewed anchors, repeated items, and overlapping judgments to estimate reliability. Route high-impact disagreement to specialists, not automatic majority voting. Audit selection bias, annotator fatigue, positional bias, and turnaround distributions. Deliver immutable dataset versions with sampling weights, provenance, and exclusion reasons. Separate customer-specific instructions from shared rubrics and keep evaluation prompts out of training exports. Release a batch only after coverage and audit thresholds agreed with the customer are met.

### Capacity and release controls

If every pair receives two independent judgments, the illustrative three-minute assumption becomes 10,000 direct annotation hours per week, before training, breaks, and adjudication. If 10% of pairs need a further six-minute expert review, that adds 1,000 hours. These calculations make overlap and rubric difficulty explicit staffing decisions instead of hidden quality costs. Measure actual completion-time distributions during a pilot before committing to the production roster.

A comparison moves through sampled, validated, assigned, judged, adjudicated, and released states. The released record contains the prompt version, both exact responses, randomized presentation order, labels, rubric version, and the decision that reconciled conflicting judgments. A correction creates a new dataset version and an affected-record list; silently modifying an exported label makes later model regressions difficult to explain.

Acceptance gates should distinguish task validity, coverage, and judgment reliability. A batch can have high annotator agreement yet fail because it contains only easy English prompts. Audit a stratified sample independently and report uncertainty rather than representing a small clean sample as proof that all 100,000 labels are correct.
