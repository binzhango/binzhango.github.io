## Question

You are opening in a new city. Structure the safety case.

## Brief answer

Define the new city's operating domain, identify what differs from validated deployments, and assemble evidence for each resulting hazard and capability claim. A safety case is a structured argument supported by tests, analysis, and operational controls, not a checklist of aggregate miles. Expansion should be staged with explicit limits and stop criteria.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Inventory road geometry, traffic behavior, weather, construction patterns, emergency interactions, mapping, and local operating requirements. Link each gap to scenario tests, simulation validation, closed-course trials, supervised field evidence, and fallback procedures. Verify remote assistance and incident response capacity before scale-up.

Use independent review of the argument and unresolved uncertainty, then restrict deployment to conditions supported by evidence. Monitor new scenario clusters and update the case after changes. Release authority should consider residual risk and operational capability, not only a favorable average driving metric.

### Connect each expansion claim to evidence and limits

Start with a claim such as “the system can operate within these mapped areas, weather conditions, hours, and road types under the defined fallback policy.” Decompose it into capabilities: detecting relevant actors, handling local road geometry, responding to emergency situations, maintaining localization, and entering a safe fallback when assumptions fail. Each subclaim needs evidence appropriate to its consequence.

Compare the new city with the existing validated domain. A new type of intersection, different construction practice, or heavier precipitation is a specific evidence gap, not something automatically covered by more miles elsewhere. Use simulation to vary scenarios, closed-course tests for controlled hazards, and supervised field trials for interactions that cannot be reproduced adequately offline. State the limitations of each evidence source.

Operational readiness belongs in the argument. Verify mapping updates, remote assistance capacity, incident response, maintenance, and the ability to restrict or suspend service. A technically capable vehicle can still be unsupported during a city-wide disruption.

Stage expansion with explicit geography and condition limits, independent review, and stop criteria tied to observed failures or unresolved uncertainty. Revisit the argument after material model, hardware, or operating changes. Aggregate miles can contribute exposure evidence, but they do not demonstrate that every important local scenario has been handled or that rare catastrophic risks are acceptably controlled.
