## Question

Discuss precision, recall and F1: when would you prioritise one over the others?

## Brief answer

Precision asks how many predicted positives are truly positive; recall asks how many actual positives were found. F1 is their harmonic mean and ignores true negatives. Prioritize recall when missed positives are costly and precision when false positives are costly, but choose a threshold from operational costs and capacity rather than treating any metric as universally best.

## Detailed answer

With true positives TP, false positives FP, and false negatives FN, precision is $TP/(TP+FP)$, recall $TP/(TP+FN)$, and F1 $2TP/(2TP+FP+FN)$. Define behavior when denominators are zero. For rare positives, inspect precision-recall curves and performance at realistic prevalence; a seemingly strong ROC curve can conceal poor precision.

A safety alert may favor recall but still needs a tolerable false-alarm load. A manual fraud-review queue may optimize recall subject to a precision or review-capacity constraint. Report class-specific metrics and macro/micro aggregation rules for multiclass problems. Calibrate probabilities when decisions require expected costs, and validate thresholds on temporally separate data to account for distribution shift.

### Choose a threshold using consequences

Suppose a detector finds 80 of 100 true cases and raises 20 false alarms. Precision and recall are both 0.8, so F1 is 0.8. Lowering the threshold might find 95 cases but raise 95 false alarms: recall becomes 0.95, precision 0.5, and F1 about 0.655. Whether this is better depends on the cost of misses and the capacity to investigate alerts, not the F1 number alone.

F1 ignores true negatives and gives equal harmonic emphasis to precision and recall. It is useful for some retrieval tasks but does not encode every operational objective. A support review team may require at least 90% precision and maximize recall subject to that limit. A medical screening workflow has different downstream costs and requires domain-specific validation. Select thresholds on a heldout set, then monitor changing prevalence: even unchanged sensitivity and specificity can yield very different precision when positives become rarer.
