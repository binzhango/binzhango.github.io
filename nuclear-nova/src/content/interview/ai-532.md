## Question

Engagement metrics and wellbeing metrics disagree. How do you build a system that resolves that?

## Brief answer

Treat wellbeing and safety as explicit constraints on optimization, not metrics to trade away invisibly for engagement. Define the disputed outcome with domain specialists and affected users, then examine causal evidence and subgroup effects. Longer sessions can reflect value, distress, confusion, or compulsive use; they are not inherently success.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Establish a metric hierarchy with hard safety gates, user-reported benefit, healthy task completion, and engagement as a supporting signal. Review experiments that improve engagement but worsen predefined harm indicators before expansion. Use privacy-conscious surveys and carefully designed longitudinal studies rather than inferring wellbeing from chat length alone.

Provide user controls, clear boundaries, and escalation paths for concerning interactions. Audit incentives in ranking and model rewards so the system is not trained to discourage leaving or exploit vulnerability. Document who can approve tradeoffs and which harms are unacceptable. When evidence is uncertain, limit rollout and gather better data. Report heterogeneous effects; a small average gain can conceal serious harm in a vulnerable group.

### Turn the disagreement into a decision rule

Suppose a ranking change increases session length but also increases reports that users feel pressured to continue. First verify sample selection, survey wording, and whether the effects concentrate in particular groups. Then apply the pre-agreed product constraints: an engagement gain cannot compensate for crossing a defined harm threshold. This prevents the team from inventing a favorable weighting after seeing the experiment.

Separate short-term proxy metrics from the outcome of interest. More messages may reflect a useful learning session, repeated misunderstanding, or difficulty disengaging. Use task completion, voluntary return, direct feedback, and appropriately designed longitudinal evidence rather than labeling one behavioral metric “wellbeing.” Collect only the data required for that evaluation under the reviewed privacy policy.

The system needs an accountable owner for stopping or narrowing an experiment and a way to revert ranking or model behavior quickly. Record uncertainty and heterogeneous effects. If the evidence cannot distinguish benefit from harm, continue only within a bounded evaluation scope rather than treating lack of statistical certainty as proof that the change is acceptable.
