## Question

Write the loss function for logistic regression and prove it has a global minimum.

## Brief answer

Binary logistic negative log likelihood is convex, but convexity does not guarantee a finite global minimizer. With perfectly separable data, coefficients can diverge while loss approaches zero, so the infimum is not attained. A positive L2 penalty on all coefficients makes the objective coercive and strictly convex, ensuring a unique finite minimizer.

## Detailed answer

For labels $y_i\in\{0,1\}$ and $z_i=x_i^T\beta$,

$$L(\beta)=\sum_i\left[\log(1+e^{z_i})-y_i z_i\right].$$

Compute this with a stable softplus implementation rather than directly exponentiating large logits. The gradient is $X^T(p-y)$ and Hessian is $X^TWX$, where $W_{ii}=p_i(1-p_i)\ge0$. Thus every Hessian quadratic form is nonnegative and the loss is convex; any attained stationary point is globally optimal.

Counterexample: one sample $x=1,y=1$ has loss $\log(1+e^{-\beta})$, which decreases toward zero as $\beta\to\infty$ without reaching it. Adding $\lambda\|\beta\|^2/2$ with $\lambda>0$ supplies a positive-definite Hessian and forces loss to grow as norm grows. If the intercept is unpenalized, check existence separately, especially when all labels have one class.

### Convex does not guarantee an attained minimum

For any vector v, $v^TX^TWXv=\sum_i p_i(1-p_i)(x_i^Tv)^2\ge0$, which proves convexity. If all labels are separable by a hyperplane, scaling its coefficients can push predicted probabilities toward 0 or 1 and drive the loss toward its infimum without attaining it at finite coefficients. Therefore the question's unqualified request to prove a global minimum exists is false.

For a simple nonseparable intercept-only example with one positive and one negative observation, the optimum is probability 1/2, coefficient 0, and total loss $2\log2$. The gradient vanishes there, and convexity makes it global. Adding a positive L2 penalty on every coefficient makes the objective coercive and strongly convex: it must attain one unique minimum. If the intercept is exempt, check class coverage and separation rather than carrying over that proof unchanged. Stable softplus and finite-value checks remain necessary even for a mathematically well-behaved objective.
