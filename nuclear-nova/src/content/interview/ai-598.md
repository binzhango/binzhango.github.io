## Question

How do you think about engineering work for defence and intelligence customers, and what would you do if asked to build something you were uncomfortable with?

## Brief answer

Explain your own values and decision process concretely, including the use cases you support, the harms you consider, and the boundaries you would not cross. Evaluate the specific system, end users, data, and foreseeable uses rather than relying only on an organization's sector. Do not invent a personal position or past incident to satisfy an interview prompt.

## Detailed answer

**Response framework.** Ask what the requested capability enables, who is affected, what authority and oversight apply, and how misuse or error could occur. Review the organization's policy and available escalation channels, then raise concerns early with a concrete explanation and safer alternatives where possible. Seek qualified ethical, legal, or security review when the issue requires it.

If the work remains outside your boundaries, communicate that clearly and use the appropriate reassignment or refusal process. Explain how you would preserve confidentiality and avoid unilateral harmful actions while escalating. A thoughtful answer can acknowledge uncertainty and competing public interests without surrendering responsibility for the systems you help build.

### Use a specific capability to reason about boundaries

Distinguish a maintenance-planning system, an intelligence-search tool, and a system influencing consequential operational decisions; their users, failure consequences, oversight, and misuse risks differ. Ask what the capability enables, which data it uses, who may act on its output, and whether the intended constraints are enforceable. A sector label alone does not answer those questions.

**Illustrative response structure:** “I would clarify the use case and accountability, document the concern, and raise it through the designated review channel before implementing the contested capability. I would propose a narrower design if it materially reduces the risk. If it remains outside my boundaries, I would seek reassignment or decline through the appropriate process.” Adapt this to your actual values rather than adopting a fabricated stance. Explain how you preserve confidentiality and avoid unilateral harmful actions while escalating. A thoughtful answer can acknowledge uncertainty and competing interests while still making concrete choices about what work you will own.
