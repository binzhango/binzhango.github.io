## Question

A customer insists on fine-tuning an open model on their support tickets because “we want our own model.” You think RAG solves it. What do you do?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: Reframe the conversation around the customer's requirements rather than defending a technique. “Our own model” may mean data control, differentiation, lower cost, or predictable behavior. Agree on measurable tasks and constraints, then compare a RAG baseline, fine-tuning, and a combined solution using the same heldout cases and total operating cost.

## Detailed answer

Explain that support tickets contain both reusable behavior and changing facts. Retrieval is well suited to current policies and account-specific evidence with access controls; fine-tuning may help tone, classification, structured resolution steps, or domain language. Neither automatically solves permissions, data quality, or hallucination.

Audit ticket quality, redaction, consent, and duplicate conversations before either approach. Run a small experiment with clear pass criteria and show concrete failure examples. If fine-tuning adds value, select a manageable adapter approach first unless evidence supports full tuning. If it does not, document that result respectfully and preserve a path to revisit it. The goal is an accountable decision the customer understands, not winning an architectural argument.

### Propose a comparison with a shared success criterion

Ask which outcome “our own model” is meant to achieve: data control, brand voice, lower cost, better resolution, or independence from a provider. Those objectives lead to different technical choices. Offer a small experiment comparing a prompt baseline, RAG over approved policies, and an adapter trained on suitable ticket examples, all judged on the same heldout cases.

Include tickets whose correct resolution changed after a policy update. RAG can retrieve the new policy without retraining; a tuned model may reproduce the old response. Include recurring structured tasks where tuning may help, such as classification or consistent extraction. Protect train/test separation by conversation and customer, and remove private data that should not be memorized. Present failure examples and operating costs, not only an average score. A hybrid may meet the customer's goals best. If evidence favors RAG, explain how it still provides a controlled branded service without dismissing the underlying ownership concern.
