## Question

Training a trillion-parameter model, attention logits can blow up and destabilise the run. What is going on, and how does something like MuonClip address it?

## Brief answer

Large query/key norms can produce extreme attention logits, saturating softmax and making optimization unstable. MuonClip combines Muon-style updates with a query/key clipping mechanism aimed at controlling those logits. It addresses a specific instability; it does not eliminate the need to monitor loss, gradients, numerical precision, and bad training batches.

## Detailed answer

Attention scores are $q^\top k/\sqrt{d}$. Even with the scaling factor, growing norms or aligned outliers can create enormous scores. Softmax then concentrates almost entirely on a few positions, while low-precision arithmetic may overflow or lose useful resolution. Check maximum logits per layer/head, norm distributions, non-finite values, and whether the event follows a data or optimizer change.

The Kimi K2 report introduces QK clipping within MuonClip to control excessive attention scores by adjusting relevant projections. This is more targeted than clipping every gradient with the same threshold. In another architecture, reproduce the failure and test the intervention before copying thresholds. Preserve optimizer state consistently on recovery and compare quality as well as the absence of loss spikes.

### Saturation is a feedback problem

Softmax probabilities depend on score differences. Scores of 2 and 3 yield probabilities near 0.27 and 0.73; scores of 20 and 30 yield roughly 0.000045 and 0.999955. Large query/key norms can therefore turn moderately different directions into nearly one-hot attention. Subtracting the maximum stabilizes exponentiation, but does not undo saturation or fix an overflow that occurred while forming the dot product.

MuonClip combines an optimizer with a control aimed at the query/key projections responsible for excessive logits. Do not confuse this with clipping the loss or truncating attention probabilities afterward: those change different parts of the computation. During diagnosis, replay a failing batch from a known checkpoint, identify the first layer whose statistics diverge, and separate numerical overflow from genuinely extreme finite values. Test the smallest targeted intervention and verify subsequent learning, since a perfectly flat loss curve obtained by suppressing useful updates is not successful stabilization.
