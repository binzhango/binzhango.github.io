## Question

Some annotators are pasting your tasks into ChatGPT and submitting the output. How do you detect and handle it?

## Brief answer

Use multiple behavioral and quality signals to identify suspicious work, then investigate with fair, documented review. Text style or an AI-detector score cannot reliably prove outsourcing. Clarify the annotation policy, protect task data, and distinguish permitted assistance from prohibited external disclosure or replacement of human judgment.

## Detailed answer

Compare timing, identical explanations across workers, pasted-content events where transparently collected, unusual answer-position patterns, and performance on controlled reasoning checks. A fast expert or assistive-technology user can trigger these signals legitimately, so combine them with sample review and a chance to explain. Do not add invasive monitoring simply to obtain a stronger classifier.

For sensitive tasks, use access controls, minimal data exposure, approved tools, and contractual handling rules. Pause suspect batches from training export while reviewing; do not silently relabel them as correct. If misuse is substantiated, remove affected data with provenance and retrain or reevaluate downstream artifacts where necessary. Improve pay, task design, and clear examples: a process that rewards throughput while hiding expectations creates incentives for low-quality shortcuts.

### Investigate batches, not writing style alone

Start with an explicit rule: whether outside assistance is forbidden because human preference is the target measurement, because the data is confidential, or both. Those are different violations. Collect the minimum evidence needed to investigate the rule. An unusually polished explanation might come from a skilled worker; a paste event might be a legitimate prepared note. Neither is a sufficient basis for discipline.

A stronger case combines independent evidence: repeated rare wording across unrelated workers, implausible timing, explanations inconsistent with the selected label, and failures on a supervised follow-up using the same rubric. Have a reviewer examine examples without seeing the initial suspicion score, then let the worker respond through a documented process.

If a batch is contaminated, trace its record IDs through exports and downstream experiments. Quarantine or replace those labels and compare affected model results before assuming retraining is required. At prevention time, reduce sensitive task context, supply an approved reasoning workspace, and pay for careful review. The objective is reliable human judgments and controlled data handling, not an unreliable classifier that guesses who used an LLM.
