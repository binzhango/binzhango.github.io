## Question

Qwen3 unifies a thinking mode and a non-thinking mode in one model with a caller-settable thinking budget. How would you train that, and how would you serve it?

## Brief answer

Train explicit thinking and direct-response behaviors, then serve them with a clear mode and token-budget contract. A thinking budget controls compute spent before the final answer; it is not a guarantee of correctness or an unlimited ability to interrupt any checkpoint cleanly. Evaluate accuracy, latency, and instruction following separately at each supported budget.

## Detailed answer

The original Qwen3 release describes hybrid reasoning and strong-to-weak distillation; later Qwen variants can have different mode behavior. Pin the checkpoint and chat template.

For a general recipe, mix supervised direct answers with verified reasoning examples and use RL on reliably scored tasks. Include examples of transitioning to a final response under budget constraints. At serving time, maintain separate budgets for reasoning and final output where the model protocol supports that distinction, reserve context space, and handle limit exhaustion explicitly. Do not insert invented control tokens. Route simple tasks to direct mode and compare harder tasks at matched total compute. Log token use and final-task success without treating hidden reasoning text as privileged evidence of correctness.

### Budget the final answer separately

An application may afford 1,000 generated tokens in total. Spending all of them on intermediate reasoning leaves no usable result. Where the checkpoint's protocol supports it, reserve a final-answer allowance and trigger its documented transition before the budget expires. If the protocol lacks a safe transition, use a shorter generation policy or choose a supported model rather than injecting arbitrary delimiters.

Construct training examples for both easy direct responses and hard verified reasoning, including cases where concise reasoning succeeds. A teacher can provide useful supervision, but filter solutions by correctness and task coverage instead of copying every long trace. At serving time, compare direct mode, a small thinking budget, and a larger budget on the same heldout tasks. Plot success against total latency and tokens to find where extra reasoning is worthwhile. Tool availability and authorization should remain identical across modes; thinking is a compute choice, not expanded permission.
