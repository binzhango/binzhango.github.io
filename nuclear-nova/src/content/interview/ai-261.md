## Question

A bank wants the whole stack (model, RAG, agents) deployed air-gapped on their own GPUs. What actually changes versus your SaaS?

## Brief answer

An air-gapped deployment must package every operational dependency inside the boundary, including model artifacts, embedding/reranking services, observability, authentication integrations and update tooling. The customer also becomes part of the capacity, incident and upgrade operating model.

## Detailed answer

Produce a dependency inventory and a repeatable signed release bundle with checksums, software/model licenses and offline installation tests. Replace external telemetry, DNS assumptions and package/model downloads with approved internal services. Size GPU memory for total weights, concurrent KV caches and failover, then benchmark representative workloads on the actual hardware. Integrate document ACLs and deletion propagation across all derived indexes. Plan controlled import/export, patching, evaluation, backup restoration and rollback without internet access. Compared with SaaS, fleet variability and slower updates increase support cost. Demonstrate a complete disconnected recovery drill before promising the same operational experience as a managed cloud service.

### A release is a complete bundle

SaaS can hide model downloads, package updates, centralized telemetry, and rapid fleet-wide fixes behind its service. An air-gapped installation must bring a compatible model, tokenizer, embeddings, index software, tool adapters, runtime, drivers, and operational documentation through a controlled import process. A missing dependency discovered only during an outage may be impossible to fetch promptly.

Test a clean installation and full restoration without external DNS or network access. Mirror the bank's identity and document-access model into retrieval and action execution, and retain audit information locally. Define the update cadence and how vulnerabilities or model deprecations reach disconnected sites. Benchmark actual hardware because memory, kernels, and interconnects vary; include failover capacity and long-context cache. Support must use approved diagnostics that do not export sensitive prompts by default. A successful deployment demonstration should include restarting services, restoring indexes/checkpoints, validating a release, and rolling it back while the environment remains disconnected.
