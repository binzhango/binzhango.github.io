## Question

Describe a time you dealt with conflicting priorities or stakeholder feedback. What would your current manager say about you?

## Brief answer

Response framework: describe a real conflict between priorities, identify the decision criteria, and explain how you made trade-offs visible to stakeholders. Then answer the manager question with observed feedback and concrete behaviors, including a credible development area. Do not invent a flattering quotation or imply that every stakeholder ended satisfied.

## Detailed answer

For the conflict, state who needed what, the deadline and resource constraints, and your role. Show how you separated must-have requirements from preferences, quantified consequences where possible, and obtained an accountable decision. Explain how you communicated the plan and handled new information afterward.

For your manager's view, use feedback you actually received: reliability, technical depth, collaboration, speed, or a tendency you are improving. Support each claim with a brief example rather than a list of adjectives. Report the outcome of the priority decision and what you would improve next time. A strong answer demonstrates transparent judgment and follow-through, not simply working longer hours to avoid choosing.

### Distinguish prioritization from appeasement

**Illustrative scenario:** a reliability owner asks for an outage fix while a product owner asks for a launch feature. A useful response quantifies the outage risk, identifies which feature scope can move, and gets one accountable decision rather than promising both on an impossible schedule. It then explains how the trade-off and revised expectations were communicated.

Use actual feedback when answering what your manager would say. Pair one strength with a specific example and one growth area with a changed behavior. “My manager said my designs were thorough but slow to converge; I began time-boxing alternatives and recording decision criteria” is a structure, not a biography to adopt. Describe how the change affected later work using real evidence. The two parts can connect: transparent prioritization demonstrates judgment, while feedback shows self-awareness. Avoid claims that your manager would call you exceptional unless you can support them with genuine feedback.
