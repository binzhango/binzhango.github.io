## Question

What are the benefits of using RAG?

## Brief answer

RAG can improve freshness, domain coverage, source traceability, and updateability without retraining the generator for every document change.

## Detailed answer

It also enables access-controlled information use and can reduce unsupported answers when retrieval is good. Costs include ingestion, indexing, retrieval latency, context tokens, and additional failure modes. Benefits are workload-dependent: irrelevant retrieval can worsen an answer. Measure grounded task success and total operating cost, not just whether the architecture contains a vector database.

The strongest operational benefit is separable updates. A policy document can be corrected or replaced in the knowledge source, reindexed, and used by the same generator. This is often easier to audit than trying to determine whether a parameter update overwrote every old formulation. Source IDs and versions also let reviewers inspect what information was available for an answer.

Access control is another application benefit when enforced correctly: two users can ask the same question and receive evidence from different authorized collections. That requires filters and cache scoping outside the model. It is not enough to retrieve everything and ask the model not to reveal restricted content.

The costs include parsing, embeddings, storage, retrieval, reranking, longer prompts, and maintenance of freshness. A document-heavy task may gain substantially, while rewriting a paragraph already supplied by the user may gain nothing. Evaluate total cost per supported successful answer, including latency and failure recovery. RAG adds an inspectable information path, but each stage can omit or distort evidence, so the architecture needs stage-level monitoring rather than a blanket assumption of reduced hallucination.
