## Question

Budget the end-to-end latency for a real-time voice agent. Why is time-to-first-audio a different problem from an LLM's time-to-first-token?

## Brief answer

Budget from the user's end of speech to audible playback: endpointing, ASR stabilization, reasoning/tool work, text chunking, TTS startup, transport, and audio buffering. LLM first-token latency covers only one segment. A token may be punctuation or an incomplete phrase that cannot yet be spoken naturally.

## Detailed answer

An illustrative target is 200 ms endpointing, 100 ms ASR finalization, 300 ms model planning/first speakable phrase, 180 ms TTS startup, and 120 ms network/playback buffer: 900 ms total before tool-dependent delays. These are proposed allocations and must be measured together under real call conditions.

Overlap stable partial transcription with retrieval and planning, but avoid executing consequential actions before final intent and confirmation. Stream phrase-sized text to TTS and support immediate barge-in cancellation. Track end-of-speech detection errors, first intelligible audio, gaps between audio chunks, and time to a useful answer. Test slow speakers, pauses inside sentences, packet loss, and tool calls. Filling every delay with speech can worsen interruption and trust if the filler masks uncertainty or an unfinished operation.

### Optimize the critical path, then verify the conversation

Instrument timestamps for last user speech, endpoint declaration, stable transcript, first speakable text, first audio byte, and actual playback. These distinguish an endpoint detector waiting too long from a fast TTS service hidden behind slow phrase formation. Client playback telemetry matters: receiving audio at the server does not mean the caller heard it.

Some stages overlap. ASR can process speech while the person is talking, and retrieval can begin from a stable partial hypothesis. Consequently, summing independently measured stage percentiles is not a reliable end-to-end percentile; measure the complete distribution as well as the proposed serial budget. Retract speculative retrieval when a later word changes intent, such as “cancel” becoming “don't cancel.” Keep irreversible tool actions behind final intent validation.

Use a turn identifier on generated text and audio. When the caller interrupts, invalidate that turn, cancel outstanding model and TTS work, and discard buffered audio belonging to it. Otherwise the agent may continue speaking an old answer after accepting a new request.

Evaluate both response delay and conversational correctness. An aggressive endpoint detector reduces apparent latency but can repeatedly cut off slow speakers. A useful release test therefore includes pauses, corrections, interruptions, and real network conditions, with human judgments of whether the exchange felt responsive and coherent.
