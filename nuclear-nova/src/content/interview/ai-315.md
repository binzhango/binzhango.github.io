## Question

Bulbul-style TTS has to speak code-mixed, mixed-script text naturally. What are the hard parts of text normalization and prosody for Indian-language TTS?

## Brief answer

Text normalization must decide how mixed scripts, abbreviations, numbers, dates, currency, and names should be spoken. Prosody must preserve phrase boundaries and natural code-switching without abrupt changes in voice or accent. A single transliteration pass loses meaning; pronunciation often depends on local context and the speaker's intended language.

## Detailed answer

Separate normalization from synthesis so ambiguous transformations are inspectable. Expand “12/03” only with locale or user context; distinguish a phone number from a quantity, and preserve product names. Use a pronunciation lexicon and script-aware grapheme-to-phoneme handling, with a fallback for unseen names rather than silently mangling them.

Train and evaluate on natural code-mixed speech, including intra-sentence switches, honorifics, and regional pronunciations. Align pauses with syntax and avoid spelling every English acronym identically in every language. Measure intelligibility of critical entities, native-listener naturalness, speaker consistency, and first-audio latency. “Bulbul-style” describes a product requirement here; it does not establish the private training recipe of a particular TTS system.

### Normalize meaning before pronunciation

The string “₹1,250 on 12/03; call 987...” contains a currency amount, an ambiguous date, and potentially a phone number. These need different verbalizations: an amount is spoken as a quantity, while a phone number generally preserves individual digits. Date interpretation requires locale or context rather than a universal text substitution. Keep the normalized representation available for debugging before synthesizing audio.

Code switches also change stress and rhythm. An English brand within a Hindi sentence should remain recognizable without forcing an abrupt speaker identity change. Use language/span tags or phoneme-level controls where supported, and preserve sentence-level prosody across switches. Evaluate unseen proper nouns, abbreviations, addresses, and units with native listeners. Streaming adds a trade-off: beginning speech early reduces latency but leaves less future context for phrasing and pronunciation. Buffer enough text to avoid repeatedly revising the intended reading, especially around numbers and ambiguous abbreviations.
