## Question

What are your thoughts on AI safety and the risks of advanced AI systems?

## Brief answer

Discuss concrete risks across present systems and more capable future systems, then explain which engineering and governance responses fit each. Relevant categories include misinformation, privacy loss, biased decisions, misuse and unsafe autonomous actions. Treat long-term risks as uncertain but consequential hypotheses, rather than either established inevitabilities or issues that can be dismissed without evidence.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: choose a deployment you understand and trace how a model error or malicious use could cause harm. Distinguish likelihood, severity, exposure and reversibility. A drafting tool with review differs from an agent with broad credentials and no approval boundary.

Describe practical controls: evaluation, limited permissions, evidence checking, monitoring, incident response and staged rollout. Explain what those controls do not solve, including unknown failure modes and incentives to overstate benchmark performance. Safety and usefulness should both be measured; indiscriminate refusal can also harm users.

State where you are uncertain and what research or operational evidence would change your priorities. Avoid pretending that one alignment technique or policy eliminates all risk. The aim is a coherent position linked to responsible technical choices, not a recital of fashionable concerns.

An illustrative position is: “For an assistant with access to private records and external tools, I would first reduce reachable harm through scoped permissions, verified evidence and reversible actions. As its ability to plan grows, I would also test longer-horizon misuse and whether our evaluators can detect subtle failures. I do not assume today's controls will remain sufficient at every capability level.” This expresses priorities without inventing personal experience.

Separate uncertainty about probability from uncertainty about consequence. A risk can justify preparation because its impact is high even when its likelihood is hard to estimate. Conversely, broad concern should not excuse vague mitigations: name the failure mode, the control and how effectiveness would be measured.

A balanced answer also recognizes product harms from unnecessary refusal, inaccessible interfaces or unreliable advice. Discuss how useful assistance and safety are evaluated together, and identify evidence that would change your priorities. The interviewer is looking for coherent technical judgment and intellectual honesty, not agreement with one dramatic forecast.
