## Question

A generated note contains a medication the patient never mentioned. Treat that as a safety incident: how do you detect it before a clinician sees it?

## Brief answer

Require medication assertions to carry evidence and source type, then compare them against transcript spans and explicitly authorized chart context before showing the draft. A medication absent from speech might legitimately come from the chart, but it must never be presented as something the patient said. Quarantine unsupported or contradictory assertions for review.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Extract medication name, status, dose, route, temporality, and negation with provenance. Run checks for source mismatch, low-confidence ASR, copied template content, and similar-name confusion. If the generator introduces a medication without a traceable source, remove or flag it rather than inventing a citation.

Preserve the incident trace and identify whether the cause was retrieval contamination, chart identity, ASR bias, or generation. Audit other notes produced by the same version and deploy a targeted regression test. Measure unsupported medication assertions with clinician-reviewed samples and verifier false negatives. Deterministic evidence requirements reduce risk, but entailment and clinical meaning still require qualified evaluation; do not promise perfect automatic detection.

### Trace the unsupported medication through each stage

Start with the final note assertion and trace it backward to the structured fact, transcript span, audio segment, and any chart context. This locates the failure: ASR may have substituted a drug name, extraction may have lost negation, retrieval may have supplied another encounter's data, or note synthesis may have invented a familiar regimen. These mechanisms require different fixes.

Require medication assertions to carry a source type and an evidence identifier. A chart-derived item must remain distinguishable from a statement made during the visit. If generation creates a medication assertion without corresponding evidence, block that assertion from the draft or mark it unresolved for authorized review. Merely asking the same model whether it hallucinated is a weak independent check.

Create regression cases with similar-sounding names, negation, discontinued drugs, hypothetical treatment discussions, and misleading historical lists. Evaluate insertions as well as recognition accuracy; a system that always fills an expected medication may look accurate on common cases while fabricating it on absent cases.

During an incident, contain the affected output path and identify potentially impacted drafts through versioned traces under appropriate access controls. Validate the fix on both the triggering case and nearby cases. The goal is to remove the causal failure without suppressing correctly supported medication facts or hiding uncertainty from the clinician.
