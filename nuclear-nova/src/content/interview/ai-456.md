## Question

You retrieved 50 candidate passages but the model's useful context budget is ~10. How do you choose, and how do you know your choices are good?

## Brief answer

Select passages for collective coverage under a token budget, not just the ten highest independent scores. Rerank for relevance, remove near-duplicates, preserve source diversity where useful, and include definitions or neighboring context needed to interpret evidence. Validate selection with labeled evidence coverage and downstream supported-answer quality.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Identify query facets, score candidate passages for relevance and authority, and greedily add the passage with the largest marginal coverage gain per token. Penalize redundant passages and reserve space for instructions and the answer. Ten passages is only a proxy: count actual tokens, and avoid splitting a passage so its qualifications disappear.

For a comparison query, ensure both alternatives and the comparison criteria are represented. For a contract question, retrieving the operative clause without its referenced definition may be insufficient. Evaluate against human-marked supporting spans using recall, contradiction coverage, and citation precision. Run ablations on diversity and neighbor expansion, and compare answer accuracy at equal prompt budgets. Keep a trace of selected and rejected candidates so retrieval failure can be distinguished from generation failure.

### Turn selection into a constrained decision

Suppose the question asks whether product A is cheaper and more reliable than B. The first six retrieved passages all describe A's pricing, while the seventh describes B's pricing and the eighth and ninth provide reliability measurements. Selecting only by individual similarity can spend most of the context on one facet. A coverage-aware selector keeps the strongest A pricing passage, then adds the complementary evidence before another near-duplicate.

A practical greedy score combines relevance, newly covered facets, authority, and contradiction value, minus overlap with already selected passages. Divide marginal benefit by token cost when long passages compete with short ones. This is a heuristic, not a proof of optimality; dependencies such as a clause plus its definition may need to be selected as a bundle.

Evaluate the selector against an oracle set of answer-supporting spans. If the oracle produces a good answer while the selected set does not, selection is implicated. If both fail despite containing the needed evidence, investigate generation or the rubric. Compare at the same total token budget so a larger context does not masquerade as a better selection algorithm.
