## Question

How do you attribute every claim in a generated answer to a specific retrieved span?

## Brief answer

Maintain a mapping from source spans through retrieval and generation, then validate each citation against its cited text. A citation marker alone does not establish that a claim is supported.

## Detailed answer

Assign immutable document-version and span IDs before prompting. Ask the generator for claims with citation IDs drawn from the supplied evidence; reject unknown IDs. Check that the cited span entails the claim, including dates, quantities and qualifiers. Split compound claims when different spans support different clauses. For unsupported statements, revise, retrieve again or abstain. Preserve authorization when displaying the source and report citation precision separately from answer correctness.

Make evidence addressable before generation. A passage identifier should resolve to a specific document revision and character or token span, not merely a mutable URL. Supply compact IDs with the retrieved text and ask the model to associate each answer claim with one or more allowed IDs. Validate that every returned ID belongs to the authorized evidence set.

Then check support, not just identifier syntax. If the source says “up to 30 days for annual plans,” an answer saying “all refunds take 30 days” changes both certainty and scope. Split compound claims so their quantities, dates and qualifiers can be checked independently. A claim that combines two documents may require both citations and an explicit inference rather than pretending one span states the conclusion verbatim.

Useful metrics include citation precision, the fraction of citations that support their attached claims, and claim coverage, the fraction of externally verifiable claims with adequate support. Also measure answer completeness, since a perfectly cited answer can omit the essential point. Unsupported claims should trigger targeted retrieval, revision or an explicit inability to answer. Keep automated entailment judgments calibrated with human review, particularly for tables, negation and numerical comparisons where fluent paraphrases can conceal errors.
