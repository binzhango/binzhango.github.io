## Question

How would you make complex AI research findings accessible to a non-technical audience?

## Brief answer

Start from the audience's decision, explain the finding with a concrete example and distinguish evidence from interpretation. Replace unnecessary terminology with a plain description of the mechanism, while preserving uncertainty and limitations. Accessibility should make the result easier to judge, not make it sound more certain than the research supports.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: state the question the researchers asked, the comparison they ran and the measured result. Use a familiar example only when its correspondence is accurate, and explain where the analogy stops. A chart with a clear denominator often communicates more than a dense list of benchmark names.

Separate “worked in this experiment” from “will work in our product.” Describe dataset scope, error bars or sample limitations in terms relevant to the audience. For executives, connect the result to a decision and the next validation step; for users, connect it to expected behavior and remaining failure cases.

Check understanding by asking the audience to explain the implication in their own words. Avoid jargon replacement that creates a false mechanism, such as describing all model errors as forgotten facts. Use actual evidence from a chosen paper rather than inventing a dramatic business impact.

An illustrative explanation might say: “The researchers compared two ways of finding documents before answering questions. On their test set, the new method found the needed passage more often. That suggests we should test it on our manuals, but it does not yet show that our final answers will be more accurate.” This preserves the causal chain and the limit of the evidence without requiring the audience to know retrieval terminology.

Choose one concrete example that the audience can inspect, such as a question, the retrieved passage and the resulting answer. Explain the failure case too: a method can retrieve related text that does not actually answer the question. For a decision-maker, state the cost of trying the method and what result would justify adoption.

When answering this as a behavioral question, use a real communication event if available: audience, misunderstanding, adaptation and evidence that understanding improved. Do not claim that an illustrative explanation was personally delivered or that it created an unmeasured business outcome.
