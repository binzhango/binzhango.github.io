## Question

Design the safety stack for voice cloning: consent, watermarking and abuse response.

## Brief answer

Treat voice cloning as a privileged capability tied to verified consent, scoped use, and revocable voice assets. Combine enrollment checks, access controls, provenance signals, abuse monitoring, and rapid response. Watermarking can help attribution but should not be assumed to survive every transformation or prove consent on its own.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Record the consent scope, identity verification process, approved users, retention period, and revocation mechanism for each voice. Challenge enrollment with fresh prompted speech rather than accepting arbitrary uploaded clips as proof of authority. Keep raw enrollment audio encrypted with restricted access and prevent public enumeration of voice assets.

Gate synthesis by voice authorization and intended use, log auditable events, and monitor suspicious volume or impersonation patterns. Evaluate watermark detectability under compression, resampling, background noise, and re-recording, reporting false positives as well as misses. Provide reporting, takedown, credential revocation, and evidence preservation. Test account takeover and revoked consent. Policy and legal requirements vary; the engineering system must implement the reviewed policy without claiming that a watermark makes every use legitimate.

### Make consent an enforceable asset lifecycle

Represent a voice as an asset with an owner, authorized callers, allowed applications, a consent version, and an active or revoked state. Every synthesis request checks that state server-side. A long-lived API key must not retain access after consent changes. Revocation should stop new synthesis immediately and define how pending jobs, cached outputs, and retained enrollment material are handled under the applicable agreements.

Fresh enrollment challenges make replay harder, but they do not eliminate coercion, sophisticated spoofing, or account takeover. Combine challenge results with appropriate identity verification and human review for ambiguous cases. Protect the enrollment and recovery paths as carefully as generation; an attacker who can replace a voice's owner can bypass excellent synthesis-time checks.

Treat a watermark as one evidence signal. Measure detector behavior on generated, benign, and transformed audio, and report the decision threshold and false-positive rate. A detected mark may support attribution to a generation process; it does not establish who authorized that generation or whether the surrounding use was acceptable.

Rehearse abuse response with an example: a compromised customer key generates impersonation calls. Operators should identify affected asset and request IDs, disable the key and asset as appropriate, preserve restricted audit evidence, and notify the responsible parties through the established process. Recovery time and revocation propagation are measurable system properties.
