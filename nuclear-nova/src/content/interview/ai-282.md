## Question

Kimi K1.5 scaled RL for reasoning without a process reward model or tree search. Why deliberately keep the RL recipe that simple?

## Brief answer

A simpler outcome-reward loop reduces engineering complexity and avoids errors from a learned step evaluator or expensive search procedure. When final answers are reliably checkable, many sampled trajectories plus policy optimization can provide useful learning signals. The cost is sparse credit assignment and potentially large rollout compute; simplicity is an empirical choice, not a universal rule.

## Detailed answer

A process reward model needs trustworthy intermediate-step labels and can be exploited through convincing but invalid reasoning. Tree search adds branching, state management, and inference expense. Removing these components makes scaling and ablation easier, while allowing the policy to learn different solution paths from terminal rewards.

Track reward accuracy, solution diversity, response length, and held-out success so the model cannot improve its apparent reward by verbosity or exploiting a verifier. Use curriculum and varied tasks when rewards are too sparse. Kimi k1.5 reports a framework without process reward models, value functions, or Monte Carlo tree search; this does not mean there was no sophisticated optimization or infrastructure.

### Credit assignment without grading every step

Imagine sampling eight solutions to a math problem and checking the final answer. Successful trajectories receive a larger learning signal than unsuccessful ones, reinforcing the token choices that produced success. The reward does not reveal which intermediate step was decisive, so several candidate trajectories, a suitable baseline, and varied problem difficulty help provide a usable signal. If all eight fail, the update may contain little information about how to improve.

This simplicity makes verifier quality central. A terminal reward is trustworthy for an executable program only to the extent that tests cover the specification and the sandbox prevents cheating. Monitor independent tasks, not merely reward on repeatedly reused prompts. Longer reasoning should earn its extra computation by solving more problems; otherwise impose appropriate length budgets or compare success per generated token. A simple algorithm can make infrastructure and ablations easier while still needing substantial rollout scheduling, data curation, and evaluation work.
