## Question

Why use LLMs to assist recall?

## Brief answer

LLMs can improve recall by rewriting ambiguous queries, generating alternative phrasings, or decomposing a complex question into searchable subquestions.

## Detailed answer

This helps bridge differences between a user's wording and the source corpus. Keep the original query among the candidates and preserve constraints such as entity names, dates, and negation. Rewrites can drift toward an incorrect interpretation, increasing noise and latency. Measure additional relevant evidence retrieved and downstream answer quality, not simply the number of search calls.

A user's wording may differ from the corpus terminology. “Why does my session disappear?” could need searches for session expiration, cookie lifetime, or token revocation. A model can propose those alternatives, while retaining the original question so a mistaken rewrite does not become the only retrieval path. For a comparison question, separate subqueries can retrieve evidence about each item.

Constrain transformations to preserve important entities, versions, dates, and negation. Rewriting “does not require a license” into “license requirements” may broaden recall but lose the exclusion central to the question. Inspect generated queries for that kind of drift. Deduplicate candidates from multiple searches and rerank against the original information need.

Measure added answer-supporting recall and final correctness against the extra model and retrieval cost. More returned documents are not evidence of better retrieval if they are irrelevant. Use a deterministic fallback when rewriting fails, and avoid allowing generated text to become evidence in the answer. The model is proposing where to search; the actual retrieved sources must establish the facts.
