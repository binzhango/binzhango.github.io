## Question

Why are most modern LLMs decoder-only architectures?

## Brief answer

Decoder-only models pair a simple single-stream architecture with scalable next-token training and flexible prompted generation.

## Detailed answer

Many tasks can be serialized as a prefix followed by a completion. A single stack simplifies training infrastructure, and cached keys and values support incremental decoding. Their prevalence also reflects successful scaling recipes and ecosystem investment. They are not inherently the fastest or best for every task: encoder-decoder models can be competitive for conditional generation, and encoder-only models can be efficient for classification or retrieval. Long-context quality depends on more than the architecture label.

A causal model can learn from ordinary text without constructing a separate input-output pair for every task. Every prefix supplies a context and each next token a target. At inference, tasks such as translation, classification, code completion, and conversation can all be represented as a prompt followed by a completion. This common interface makes data mixing and serving infrastructure convenient.

The causal structure also permits KV caching: after processing a prompt, previous token representations do not need to be recomputed when another token is appended. Training can still evaluate all token positions in parallel because the causal mask prevents future-token leakage.

There are costs. Generating a one-word label through a large autoregressive model may be slower than a small encoder with a classifier head. Bidirectional source representations can be valuable for conditional tasks, and a decoder's full prompt competes with generated text for cache capacity. The prevalence of decoder-only systems reflects a successful combination of objective, scale, tooling, and flexible task serialization; it does not prove a universal architectural superiority or that a separate encoder is obsolete.
