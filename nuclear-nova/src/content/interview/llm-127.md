## Question

How to obtain accurate semantic representations?

## Brief answer

Obtain useful semantic representations by choosing an embedding model trained for the retrieval task and encoding well-formed, appropriately sized content.

## Detailed answer

Preserve titles, relevant structure, and language information; avoid splitting an answer from its qualifier or embedding OCR noise. Follow the model's query/document prefixes, normalization, and length limits. Evaluate held-out retrieval before considering domain contrastive fine-tuning. An embedding can represent semantic relatedness without capturing answer relevance, exact identifiers, dates, or negation reliably, so lexical and metadata signals may still be necessary.

An embedding is useful when distances reflect the distinctions the retrieval task needs. For a support query, the relevant document must answer the problem, not merely discuss the same product. Choose a model trained for query-document relevance, and preserve the contextual cues that distinguish versions, entities, and exceptions.

Encode a heading with its passage when the heading supplies essential meaning. Avoid embedding a number without its row label and units. Follow the model's intended query and document prefixes and length limit; silently truncating the answer-bearing end of a passage can make a good encoder appear ineffective. Normalization must match the intended similarity metric.

Evaluate with judged queries and answer-supporting passages. Inspect errors where near-identical identifiers or negation matter, since dense representations may blur those distinctions. Add lexical or metadata signals where needed. Domain contrastive training can improve alignment if positives and negatives are trustworthy, but training on topical similarity alone can reinforce the wrong notion of relevance. Keep source text and provenance alongside vectors because the vector itself is not interpretable evidence for a final factual claim.
