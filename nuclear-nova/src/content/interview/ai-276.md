## Question

Kimi K2 is a 1T-parameter MoE with ~32B active per token and hundreds of experts. Explain the routing and the systems cost of training it.

## Brief answer

A router selects a small subset of experts for each token, so active computation is far smaller than total parameter count. However, all experts still require storage, optimizer state during training, and a placement strategy. Training cost includes token dispatch, expert imbalance, collective communication, and pipeline coordination, not just the active-parameter FLOPs.

## Detailed answer

Kimi K2's 2025 report describes roughly one trillion total parameters and 32 billion active parameters per token. These are different accounting quantities, not a claim that the entire model fits wherever a 32B dense model fits.

Shard experts across devices, route token activations to their selected experts, run grouped expert matrix multiplications, and combine weighted outputs. Popular experts can become stragglers while others idle. Track expert loads, dispatch bytes, dropped or rerouted tokens, and the critical path. Combine expert parallelism with tensor, pipeline, and data parallelism according to network topology. Checkpointing a trillion parameters and their optimizer states is also an I/O problem. Optimize quality per training dollar, including hardware utilization and recovery overhead, rather than quoting sparsity as an automatic speedup.

### Follow one token through an MoE layer

The attention output enters a router that scores experts. The chosen top-k experts receive that token's hidden vector, apply their own feed-forward networks, and send results back. The layer forms a weighted sum, with any shared-expert path handled according to its architecture. Different tokens choose different subsets, which is how one model offers large conditional capacity without executing every parameter for every token.

If 1T weights were stored at two bytes each, weights alone would occupy about 2 TB. Gradients and optimizer state increase the training requirement substantially even though only part of the network executes for each token. Network traffic grows with routed token assignments and hidden width. Group tokens for the same expert into larger matrix operations, place communicating devices on fast links, and control imbalance so popular experts do not determine step time. Training quality also depends on experts receiving sufficient varied examples; perfect equal loading is not the sole objective.
