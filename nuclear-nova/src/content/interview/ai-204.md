## Question

Design a classifier that predicts the optimal moment to insert a commercial break in a video.

## Brief answer

Treat ad-break placement as a constrained decision over a video timeline, using scene boundaries, speech pauses and engagement context. A classifier can score candidate boundaries, but the final policy must enforce minimum spacing, maximum ad load and content restrictions. Optimize incremental business value subject to viewer retention and interruption quality, not merely boundary-detection accuracy.

## Detailed answer

Suggested pipeline: extract audio pauses, shot changes, transcript structure and temporal features; generate candidate moments; label acceptable versus disruptive placements with human review and historical outcomes. Train a model on candidates rather than every frame, then use a constrained selector to choose a set of breaks.

Avoid leakage from future engagement when making an online decision. Separate model quality from the effects of ad duration and content. Evaluate precision at selected breaks, user abandonment, watch time and revenue per session with confidence intervals.

Test dialogue mid-sentence, live events, music, short clips and long silent scenes. Offline labels may not capture viewer response, so use a small controlled experiment with retention guardrails. If the system operates live, define the allowable lookahead; a boundary visible in a completed video may not be predictable in time.

A boundary score is not yet an ad schedule. Generate candidates from shot changes, pauses and transcript structure, then select a set under spacing and ad-load constraints. Two individually good boundaries may be too close together, so a constrained optimization or dynamic program can be more appropriate than independently thresholding every score.

For prerecorded video, future frames and complete sentence boundaries are available. For live video, only a bounded lookahead can be used; training features that depend on future events would leak information unavailable at serving time. Keep these two deployment settings separate.

Evaluate interruption quality with human labels and viewer outcomes. A pause during a dramatic scene may be acoustically convenient but highly disruptive, while a scene transition may be a natural break. Revenue depends on ad content, duration and auction conditions as well as placement, so randomize and control those factors where possible. Monitor abandonment and long-term engagement, not only immediate ad completion, and provide fallback rules when the model's candidate confidence is poor.
