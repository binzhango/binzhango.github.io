## Question

Behaviour cloning on teleoperation data has a well-known failure mode. What is it, and what do you do about it on a real humanoid?

## Brief answer

Behavior cloning suffers from covariate shift: small policy mistakes lead to states absent from expert demonstrations, where further errors compound. Improve coverage with safe corrective demonstrations, recovery data, and iterative collection on policy-visited states. On a humanoid, exploration must remain inside independent physical safety limits.

## Detailed answer

DAgger addresses the mismatch by iteratively collecting expert guidance on states induced by the learned policy and aggregating that data. On hardware, use a supervised or constrained variant: limit speed and force, keep an operator ready, and stop before unsafe states rather than asking the robot to discover every failure physically.

Add perturbation and recovery examples, validate observation/action timing, and test held-out objects and initial conditions. Use simulation where credible, but verify transfer on real contact dynamics. Measure success, intervention rate, recovery success, and near-hazard events over complete episodes. More nominal demonstrations may help less than a small number of well-chosen recovery trajectories; evaluate the learning curve instead of assuming uniform data value.

### Explain how a small error changes the next input

Suppose demonstrations always approach a cup from the center. A learned controller approaches slightly off-center, nudges the cup, and now sees an arrangement absent from training. Its next prediction is less reliable, so the deviation grows. Low action-prediction error on expert states does not guarantee successful behavior on the states the policy creates itself.

Collect corrections at the edge of the validated operating envelope. An operator can demonstrate how to recover from a safe misalignment, regrasp after a controlled slip, or stop and reset when recovery is inappropriate. Add those state-action pairs to the training distribution and repeat evaluation. This is the practical motivation for aggregating expert guidance on policy-visited states rather than collecting only more flawless nominal demonstrations.

On a humanoid, do not interpret iterative collection as permission for unconstrained exploration. Use reduced speed, suitable support or isolation where appropriate, independent limits, and operator intervention before a hazardous state. Some failures should be investigated in simulation or with controlled fixtures first.

Check timing and representation before attributing every failure to data coverage. Misaligned camera and action timestamps can make demonstrations internally inconsistent. Evaluate complete episodes on held-out conditions, comparing nominal success, recovery success, and interventions. A useful improvement is one that reduces compounding errors without teaching the robot to depend on frequent human rescue.
