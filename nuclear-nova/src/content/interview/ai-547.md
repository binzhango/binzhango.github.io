## Question

Design a service that turns the conversation into draft orders: labs, imaging, referrals, prescriptions, via tool calls against the EHR.

## Brief answer

Generate structured draft orders with evidence and require authorized clinician review before activation. Separate recognizing a proposed order from determining clinical appropriateness and executing it. The EHR integration must enforce patient, encounter, ordering privileges, required fields, and duplicate detection independently of the model.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Extract requested labs, imaging, referrals, and medications into typed proposals with transcript spans and uncertainty. Resolve catalog items through a terminology service; ambiguous drug, dose, route, frequency, or destination remains unresolved. Present a review screen showing the exact proposed order and its source, then submit through the EHR's supported workflow with idempotent identifiers.

Record pending, accepted, rejected, and unknown outcomes and reconcile timeouts before retrying. FHIR R4 MedicationRequest provides a standardized resource model, but actual write support and workflow behavior depend on the EHR deployment. Test wrong-patient context, duplicate proposals, canceled intent, contradictory statements, and partial submission failures. Evaluate unsupported orders and omissions with qualified clinicians rather than relying on valid JSON as evidence of safety.

### Keep proposals, approvals, and execution distinct

Represent each proposed order with a stable proposal ID, patient and encounter identifiers, catalog item, required fields, evidence, and review state. The model may populate a proposal, but only a privileged service can submit the approved version. Bind approval to a hash or version of the displayed fields so a later model edit cannot change a dose or destination under an earlier confirmation.

Conversation context is not always an order. “We considered an MRI but will wait” should not create an active imaging request. Resolve negation, future contingency, and canceled intent before presentation, and still require the clinician to verify the proposal. Missing clinical details remain visibly unresolved; the model should not complete them from typical practice.

For a group of orders, track submission individually. If two labs succeed and a referral times out, report those outcomes separately and reconcile the referral using its operation identifier. Replaying the entire group can create duplicate labs. Preserve the approved payload, server response, and final resource identifier for audit.

A standardized resource schema helps represent data but does not prove that a particular EHR supports creating drafts or executing that workflow. Test against the customer's configured integration, including authorization failures, duplicate checks, revocation, and partial outcomes. Evaluate unsupported proposals and clinically meaningful omissions before judging how many clicks the feature saves.
