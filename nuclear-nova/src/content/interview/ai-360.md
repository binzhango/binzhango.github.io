## Question

Tell me about a time you had to make progress with incomplete information: you couldn't be told the full context of what you were building.

## Brief answer

Response framework: show how you made a bounded, reversible decision despite missing context, while respecting information boundaries. Explain what was known, which assumptions mattered, and how you obtained enough interface-level clarity to move forward. The goal is sound judgment under uncertainty, not a story about bypassing confidentiality or guessing recklessly.

## Detailed answer

Choose a real case and describe the deliverable, constraints, and unavailable information at a level you can share. Identify the smallest questions needed to proceed, such as expected inputs, success criteria, reliability targets, and integration ownership. Use mocks, contracts, or staged experiments where full context is unavailable.

Explain how you documented assumptions, exposed risks to the appropriate owner, and kept changes reversible. Distinguish decisions you could safely make from those that required escalation. Report the eventual outcome and which assumptions changed when more context arrived. A strong answer includes a specific lesson about interfaces, communication, or validation, rather than claiming complete certainty was unnecessary in every situation.

### Show how you reduced uncertainty

**Illustrative response:** a team was asked to build an ingestion interface while the downstream product was confidential. The engineer requested concrete contracts—input size, schema evolution, delivery guarantees, and error handling—without demanding unnecessary product details. They built a reversible prototype against those contracts and flagged assumptions that would make redesign expensive.

In your actual story, distinguish unavailable business context from technical requirements you genuinely needed. Explain how you identified the owner who could answer critical questions, what you documented, and which decisions you postponed. Describe a test or integration milestone that exposed a wrong assumption early. Avoid presenting blind execution as adaptability: progress under uncertainty should include explicit boundaries and a route to resolve blocking unknowns. Conclude with an actual outcome and a specific change in how you now define interfaces or communicate risks, using shareable details and no invented confidential context.
