## Question

FP8 training at 671B scale is hard. What actually breaks in low precision, and how do you make it stable?

## Brief answer

Low precision can overflow, underflow or erase small updates, especially when tensor outliers and long accumulations exceed the format's effective range or resolution. Stable FP8 training uses selective higher precision, appropriate scaling and explicit numerical validation.

## Detailed answer

DeepSeek-V3's FP8 training section describes fine-grained scaling and higher-precision accumulation rather than putting every operation and state tensor in FP8. In an implementation, compare layer outputs and gradients with a BF16/FP32 reference, monitor nonfinite values and saturation, and test rare large activations. Check forward, input-gradient and weight-gradient paths separately: one successful forward pass proves little about training stability. Scale updates, transposed kernels and communication casts must use compatible conventions. Run a matched small-model training ablation before committing a large fleet, and preserve recoverable optimizer checkpoints when experimenting with numeric formats.

### Range, rounding, and accumulation fail differently

An FP8 format has very few exponent and significand bits. A tensor-wide scale chosen to accommodate one extreme outlier can make ordinary small values round to zero or coarse levels. Fine-grained scaling reduces that mismatch by adapting scales to smaller groups or tiles, at the cost of metadata and conversion work. Different forward and backward tensors can need different treatment.

Long dot products accumulate many terms, so low-precision products may still require higher-precision accumulation. Optimizer moments and small parameter updates often need more precision than the main matrix operations. Keep normalization, softmax, reductions, and other sensitive paths at the precision justified by validation rather than imposing FP8 everywhere. Compare forward outputs, input gradients, and weight gradients against a reference with identical data and seeds. Track saturation, nonfinite values, gradient norms, and loss over many steps, including outlier-heavy batches. A single accurate inference pass does not demonstrate stable training. Preserve reproducible checkpoints and recoverable full optimizer state before testing aggressive numerical settings at scale.
