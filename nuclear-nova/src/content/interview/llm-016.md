## Question

What is the curse of dimensionality, and what problem do positional encodings actually solve?

## Brief answer

The curse of dimensionality concerns the data and computation needed to learn in high-dimensional spaces. Positional encodings supply order information; they do not generally solve that curse.

## Detailed answer

Covering a $d$-dimensional grid with $k$ values per coordinate requires $k^d$ points. Distance concentration can also weaken nearest-neighbor intuition, depending on the distribution and metric. Real data may occupy a much lower-dimensional manifold.

Unmasked self-attention without positional information is permutation-equivariant: permuting inputs permutes outputs. Positional embeddings, relative biases, or rotary embeddings make position available. A causal mask already introduces directional structure, but does not make explicit positional methods a dimensionality-reduction technique. Sinusoidal or rotary formulas can be evaluated at new positions; reliable length extrapolation still requires validation.

Take 10 bins along each feature axis. A two-dimensional space has 100 cells; a ten-dimensional space has $10^{10}$. A dataset that seems large in two dimensions becomes sparse in ten. This example assumes a full Cartesian grid; correlated real features may occupy a much smaller effective region. Feature learning and prior structure help by restricting what relationships the model must learn.

Position addresses a different ambiguity. The same token multiset can describe “the dog chased the cat” or “the cat chased the dog.” A model that treats every permutation identically cannot reliably distinguish the roles. Adding a position-dependent representation, or modifying query-key comparisons according to relative positions, gives attention a way to condition on ordering.

A positional representation does not reduce the number of semantic features or supply examples for every possible combination. Likewise, being able to calculate a sinusoid at position one million does not establish useful behavior there: the trained attention patterns may not transfer. Evaluate order-sensitive tasks and long-range evidence use separately from dimensionality reduction or memory efficiency.
