## Question

How to enable an LLM to process longer texts?

## Brief answer

Combine a suitable long-context model with efficient attention, careful positional adaptation, or retrieval and hierarchical processing, depending on the task.

## Detailed answer

Extending a model's native window may require positional changes and long-sequence continued training. FlashAttention and KV compression address resource use but do not teach long-context reasoning by themselves. Retrieval selects evidence; hierarchical summaries compress it but can lose details. Test questions requiring evidence from different locations and multiple documents, not only a single “needle” lookup. More accepted tokens and better use of those tokens are distinct capabilities.

First distinguish a document-processing requirement from a model-window requirement. If a question needs two paragraphs from a thousand-page manual, retrieve those paragraphs and relevant definitions rather than extending the model to read the whole manual. If the task requires a global comparison, use hierarchical processing: extract structured facts from sections, combine them, and retain links to original passages for verification.

When genuinely extending a checkpoint, position handling and training distribution matter. Adjusting rotary position scaling can allow new positions to be represented, but the model may need continued training on long sequences to use them reliably. Memory optimizations such as cache compression and efficient attention solve capacity problems, not missing long-range skill.

Evaluate evidence at the beginning, middle, and end; require multiple distant facts; include distractors and contradictory versions. Measure quality alongside prefill latency, decoding latency, and memory at realistic concurrency. A successful single-needle lookup proves less than accurate synthesis across a long corpus. Choose the smallest effective context and retain a fallback when required evidence cannot fit without losing essential relationships.
