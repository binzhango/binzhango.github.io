## Question

DualPipe overlaps computation and communication in training. Why is that overlap the whole game at this scale, and what is the trade-off?

## Brief answer

At large scale, an idle accelerator still costs money. DualPipe schedules forward and backward work from different microbatches so computation can cover otherwise exposed communication and pipeline bubbles. The trade-off is a more complicated schedule, extra live state and memory, and a need to tune overlap against actual network and kernel contention.

## Detailed answer

Separate a training step into useful matrix computation, expert dispatch/combine, gradient communication, and idle pipeline time. Simply increasing FLOPs does not shorten a step whose critical path is an all-to-all exchange. Independent work can overlap that exchange, reducing elapsed time toward the maximum of the two durations rather than their sum.

DualPipe is a specific bidirectional pipeline schedule; its implementation also matters, because simultaneous communication and compute compete for GPU resources. More in-flight work increases activation lifetimes and complicates checkpointing and failure diagnosis. Measure exposed communication, pipeline bubbles, peak memory, and end-to-end tokens per second against a simpler schedule on the same topology. If bandwidth or memory is exhausted, overlap alone cannot rescue scaling. The official DualPipe implementation documents its scheduling assumptions.

### What overlap changes

Suppose a microbatch needs 80 ms of matrix work and 30 ms of expert communication. A serialized implementation spends 110 ms. Perfect overlap would reduce that portion to 80 ms, a 1.375× improvement, not an unlimited speedup. If overlapping kernels slow matrix work to 95 ms, the gain falls to about 1.16×. These are calculated examples, not DualPipe benchmarks.

Pipeline parallelism adds another source of waste: the first stage fills downstream stages, and the final microbatches drain them. Scheduling forward and backward microbatches from opposing directions can fill otherwise idle slots. The scheduler must respect which activations, gradients, weights, and communication buffers are ready. More microbatches improve scheduling freedom but increase live activations and bookkeeping. Evaluate complete optimizer-step time with the same batch and recomputation policy; a trace that looks busier can still have a longer critical path.
