## Question

Can you give a few examples of real-world areas where machine learning is particularly effective?

## Brief answer

Useful applications include spam detection, recommendation, speech recognition, demand forecasting, and visual defect detection.

## Detailed answer

These tasks contain patterns that are difficult to enumerate as rules. A recommender ranks items from interaction history; a speech model maps audio to text; a forecasting model uses past demand and known covariates. ML works best when the data contains predictive signal and mistakes can be measured. Compare against simple rules or statistical baselines before accepting the added operational complexity.

Consider demand forecasting for a grocery store. Inputs might include past sales, day of week, holidays, price, and planned promotions; the output is next week's demand. A useful baseline predicts the same weekday's recent average. ML becomes worthwhile when it captures interactions the baseline misses, such as a promotion having different effects on weekends. Historical stockouts complicate labels: recorded sales can be lower than actual demand.

For visual inspection, labeled images teach a classifier to distinguish acceptable parts from defects. The evaluation should hold out production batches or cameras, because random image splits can leak nearly identical backgrounds. For recommendations, the model estimates relevance from interactions, but clicks also reflect which items were previously shown. Offline accuracy therefore needs an online test of user outcomes.

Across these examples, the prerequisite is a repeated decision with observable feedback. Use rules when the condition is explicit and stable; use ML when examples contain useful patterns and the benefit exceeds the cost of collecting data, monitoring errors, and maintaining the pipeline.
