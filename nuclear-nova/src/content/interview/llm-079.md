## Question

When should you use BERT vs. LLaMA / ChatGLM models?

## Brief answer

Use BERT-style encoders for efficient representation or classification tasks, and generative Llama/ChatGLM-style models when the output requires open-ended text or interactive instruction following.

## Detailed answer

Examples for encoders include tagging, reranking, and classification with a trained task head. A contrastively trained encoder can serve dense retrieval. Generative models suit explanation, conversation, and flexible structured generation, but need output validation. Benchmark the exact checkpoint and pipeline; decoder models can also classify or embed, and a generic BERT checkpoint may not be the best specialist encoder.

For a ticket-routing service with twenty fixed labels and high request volume, a fine-tuned encoder can produce one prediction in a single forward pass. It avoids generating a sequence and can be easier to calibrate and validate. For an assistant that explains the route, asks a follow-up question, or writes a resolution, a generative model better matches the variable output requirement.

Retrieval presents another distinction. A small contrastively trained encoder can embed documents offline and queries online; a generative model is not necessary for that step. A cross-encoder can rerank a limited candidate set. The generator can then use selected evidence to write the final answer. Combining specialists often gives better cost-quality tradeoffs than using one large model for every stage.

Measure the exact checkpoint, language, context length, and serving configuration. BERT-base is a historical architecture example, not a universal best encoder, and family names such as ChatGLM cover multiple designs. Generative classification also needs parsing and label validation. Choose based on measured task quality, latency, memory, and maintenance rather than assuming newer or larger always fits the requirement better.
