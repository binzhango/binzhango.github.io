## Question

What is a vision-language-action model, and how is it different from an LLM with tools?

## Brief answer

A vision-language-action model maps visual observations and language goals to robot action representations, often continuous poses, joint targets, or action sequences. An LLM with tools usually selects higher-level symbolic API calls whose controllers handle execution. The boundary can blur, but the action space, timing, feedback, and training objective are materially different.

## Detailed answer

A VLA must learn embodiment-specific relationships between perception and movement and operate with closed-loop sensor feedback. Its actions can affect contact and stability immediately, so latency and distribution shift are central. A tool-using LLM might call “grasp object,” leaving trajectory generation and force control to a separate validated subsystem.

Compare systems by the level of control actually learned, not the product label. Test unseen objects, language variations, embodiment transfer, recovery, and safety interventions. Figure's original Helix description is one concrete VLA example with a slower semantic model and faster visuomotor policy. That architecture is an example, not a requirement that every VLA use the same hierarchy or frequencies.

### Compare the action interface and feedback timescale

An LLM-based assistant might issue a structured `pick(object_id)` request. A separate perception and motion stack resolves the object's pose, plans a trajectory, controls the gripper, and reports success. The language model reasons about tasks while a specialized controller owns the physical execution details.

A VLA instead learns a mapping from images, robot state, and language to an action representation, such as a sequence of end-effector targets or joint commands. Training must connect visual observations to the consequences of movement for a particular embodiment. A small prediction error can alter the next camera view or contact state, so closed-loop evaluation matters much more than the plausibility of a verbal explanation.

Neither label determines the entire architecture. A VLA can sit below a symbolic planner, and an LLM can call low-level tools. Inspect what actions the model actually predicts, how often observations refresh, which component enforces limits, and where recovery occurs.

Evaluate the interface on held-out objects, perturbations, ambiguous instructions, and timing variation. A tool-based design can provide clearer execution contracts but inherit the limits of its predefined skills. A learned action model can adapt more flexibly within its training coverage while being harder to diagnose and constrain. Choose the division based on task evidence and the available validated controllers.
