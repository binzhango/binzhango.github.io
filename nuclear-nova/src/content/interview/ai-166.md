## Question

We have two models, 85% and 82% accuracy. Which do you pick?

## Brief answer

Choose neither model from accuracy alone. Compare them on the same representative holdout, quantify uncertainty in the paired difference, inspect error costs and subgroup behavior, and include latency, calibration and operational constraints. An 85% model may be worse for the product if its extra correct predictions are cheap while its remaining errors are costly.

## Detailed answer

Start with the task's decision metric: false negatives and false positives may have different consequences, and class imbalance can make accuracy misleading. Examine the confusion matrices and examples where the models disagree. Use a paired bootstrap or an appropriate paired test rather than treating the two accuracy estimates as independent.

Check whether the 3-percentage-point gap is stable across time, users and important slices. Compare calibration if probabilities drive decisions; adjust thresholds on validation data using the actual cost tradeoff. Measure inference cost and latency under the production workload.

If the higher-accuracy model remains better on the relevant utility metric and meets constraints, select it and canary the release. Otherwise choose the simpler or safer alternative, or route cases selectively. Keep the test set untouched while tuning; repeatedly choosing variants on it turns it into training feedback.

An illustrative cost example shows why the ranking can reverse. Suppose one model makes fewer total errors but most are missed high-cost fraud cases, while the other makes more low-cost false alarms. Expected utility weights those errors by consequence, whereas raw accuracy gives each equal importance. The appropriate threshold may also differ between models if their probability calibration differs.

Compare predictions on the same cases and inspect the disagreement table: how often does A succeed when B fails, and vice versa? The uncertainty in a three-point gap depends on that paired structure and sample size. A gap on 100 examples is less convincing than the same gap on a large representative holdout, and neither establishes future stability under distribution shift.

After offline selection, canary the model with clear rollback criteria and measure actual outcomes, latency and cost. If the models have complementary strengths, an ensemble or routed system is an option only after accounting for extra complexity and leakage-free router evaluation. The answer should be a decision process with evidence, not a reflexive choice of the larger percentage.
