## Question

Explain action chunking. Why predict a sequence of future actions instead of the next one?

## Brief answer

Action chunking predicts several future actions at once, capturing short-horizon coordination and reducing the frequency of expensive policy inference. Execute only a controlled portion before observing again, or combine overlapping predictions carefully. Longer chunks improve continuity but can become stale during unexpected contact or environmental change.

## Detailed answer

Action Chunking with Transformers models action sequences from demonstrations to address compounding errors and nonstationary behavior in manipulation. A chunk might describe a coherent reach-and-grasp segment rather than independently choosing every next motor target. Temporal ensembling can smooth overlapping predictions, but it also affects responsiveness.

Choose chunk horizon relative to control rate, inference latency, and task dynamics. Keep low-level feedback and safety enforcement active between model evaluations. Evaluate task success, action smoothness, recovery latency, and sensitivity to disturbances, not just action prediction loss. Test a moved object or unexpected obstacle midway through a chunk. Predicting a sequence does not justify blindly executing the full sequence open-loop.

### Distinguish prediction horizon from execution horizon

Suppose a policy predicts 20 actions at a 50 Hz control rate. Its prediction horizon is 20/50 = 0.4 seconds. It might execute only the first five actions, observe again after 0.1 seconds, and predict a new chunk. This receding-horizon approach gains short-term coordination without committing to the entire predicted sequence before receiving feedback.

Predicting related actions together can represent a smooth reach or coordinated grasp more coherently than selecting isolated next steps. It can also amortize expensive inference. However, the later actions depend on assumptions about the state that may stop being true if an object moves, contact occurs unexpectedly, or the previous command is not executed accurately.

Overlapping chunks can supply multiple predictions for the same future instant. Combining them can reduce jitter, but excessive averaging may delay a necessary change or blend incompatible plans. Evaluate responsiveness as well as smoothness and ensure the combination respects the controller's action representation.

Choose the executed prefix using measured inference time and task dynamics. A slow free-space reach may tolerate a longer prefix than delicate contact manipulation. Keep feedback control and independent safety limits active throughout. Compare complete episode success and disturbance recovery against a one-step policy, because lower supervised action error alone does not establish better closed-loop behavior.
