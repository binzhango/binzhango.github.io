## Question

Design a fraud-detection system with heavy class imbalance and an adversarial opponent.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: score transactions or events using real-time behavioral features, graph signals, and rules, then route decisions by expected loss and review capacity. Heavy imbalance makes accuracy misleading; adversaries also adapt to the deployed policy. Use delayed-label-aware evaluation, calibrated risk estimates, and human review for uncertain consequential cases.

## Detailed answer

Build point-in-time features for account history, device changes, velocity, counterparties, and unusual relationship patterns. Preserve legitimate rare behavior in the dataset. Train with class weighting or sampling but calibrate on realistic prevalence. Evaluate precision at review capacity, recall of costly events, false-positive burden, and delayed confirmed outcomes.

Combine model scores with policy constraints and step-up verification rather than making every high score an irreversible denial. Monitor adversarial drift, label poisoning, and feedback loops caused by investigating only flagged cases. Keep a randomized or otherwise justified audit sample where appropriate to estimate missed fraud. Version decisions and evidence for review, protect sensitive features, and stage updates with rollback and domain-owner oversight.

### Optimize a review decision under delayed labels

If only 0.1% of transactions are fraudulent, high overall accuracy is nearly meaningless. Suppose analysts can inspect 1,000 cases per day: rank by expected preventable harm subject to review capacity, and report precision and recovered fraud value at that cutoff. Different actions—allow, challenge, review, or block—have different costs and reversibility.

Fraud labels often arrive weeks later and are biased toward cases investigated under the current policy. Maintain appropriate audit samples and account for delayed outcomes before declaring a new model better. Point-in-time features should capture velocity and relationship patterns without future leakage. Attackers adapt, so monitor feature and outcome changes, conduct adversarial scenario tests, and avoid exposing detailed decision thresholds. Preserve a reviewable evidence trail and a route for legitimate users to recover from false positives. Retraining solely on recently flagged cases can amplify the system's existing blind spots rather than discover new attack patterns.
