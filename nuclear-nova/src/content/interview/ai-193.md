## Question

What are the assumptions of linear regression?

## Brief answer

Classical linear regression assumes a linear conditional mean, no perfect multicollinearity and errors with zero conditional mean. Independence and constant error variance support conventional uncertainty estimates; normality is mainly needed for exact small-sample inference, not for computing least-squares coefficients. Prediction can still be useful when some inferential assumptions fail, but interpretation must change.

## Detailed answer

Write $Y=X\beta+\epsilon$. Full column rank makes the coefficient solution identifiable. The exogeneity condition $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon\mid X]=0$ is central to unbiased coefficient estimation; omitted variables correlated with included features can violate it. Homoscedastic, uncorrelated errors give the familiar Gauss–Markov efficiency result among linear unbiased estimators.

Check residual plots for nonlinear patterns and changing variance, inspect time or group structure, and examine feature collinearity. Use robust or clustered standard errors when appropriate, but remember that they do not repair a misspecified conditional mean or causal confounding.

Normal predictors are not required, and independent predictors are not required; only perfect linear dependence prevents a unique ordinary least-squares solution. For forecasting, evaluate on future periods. For causal claims, additional identification assumptions are necessary beyond the regression formula.

Linearity means the conditional mean is linear in the chosen coefficients, not necessarily in raw inputs. A model containing $x$, $x^2$ and an interaction term is still linear regression in its parameters. Choosing those features is part of specifying the mean function. Residual curvature suggests that the chosen representation may miss structure.

Heteroscedasticity mainly affects conventional standard-error formulas and efficiency; robust standard errors can address inference under suitable conditions but do not remove omitted-variable bias. Serially correlated errors in time data require time-aware evaluation and appropriate uncertainty treatment. Strong but imperfect collinearity can make individual coefficients unstable even when predictions remain useful.

Check the assumption relevant to the intended claim. Computing least-squares predictions requires less than making an unbiased coefficient interpretation, and causal interpretation requires still more. Normality of input features is not a requirement. Use residual diagnostics, holdout performance and domain reasoning together, and avoid treating a successful normality test as proof that a regression model answers the underlying scientific or business question.
