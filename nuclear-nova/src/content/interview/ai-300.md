## Question

Qwen3 uses strong-to-weak distillation, bootstrapping smaller models from flagship ones. How does that work and why is it cheaper?

## Brief answer

A stronger teacher generates answers or distributions that supervise a smaller student, transferring some useful behavior without repeating the teacher's full training process. Filter examples for correctness, diversity, and appropriate difficulty. Distillation is cheaper when teacher inference plus student training costs less than obtaining comparable ability through direct large-scale optimization.

## Detailed answer

Use verified tasks to select teacher outputs and include representative easy and hard cases. Sequence-level distillation trains on chosen text; distribution matching can transfer uncertainty when teacher logits are available. For reasoning tasks, compare direct answers, concise verified explanations, and longer traces rather than assuming more generated tokens are better.

The student cannot automatically inherit capabilities beyond its capacity or training coverage. It can also inherit teacher mistakes, stylistic artifacts, and benchmark contamination. Keep independent heldouts, measure short-answer efficiency, and mix original data to prevent narrowing. Strong-to-weak distillation is part of the original Qwen3 training account; the exact teacher/student recipe is release-specific.

### A student learns selected behavior

For sequence distillation, create a task, obtain a teacher response, verify it, then train the student to predict its tokens. For soft-target distillation, minimize a distribution-matching loss between teacher and student token probabilities, often with a temperature that reveals alternatives. Soft targets require compatible vocabularies or a deliberate alignment strategy and access to teacher logits; a text-only API supports sequence supervision instead.

The savings come from amortization: a costly teacher generates a reusable dataset once, while a cheaper student handles many future requests. Include teacher generation and verification in the training budget. An illustrative deployment with millions of repeated classification requests may justify distillation even if the student does not match the teacher on open-ended reasoning. Hold out task families and time periods, test uncommon labels, and preserve an escalation path. A compact model confidently reproducing a teacher's mistake is cheaper failure, not successful transfer.
