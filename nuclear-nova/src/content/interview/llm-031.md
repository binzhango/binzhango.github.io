## Question

What are the differences when applying LayerNorm at different positions in LLMs?

## Brief answer

Pre-norm normalizes before a sublayer; post-norm normalizes after the residual addition; sandwich variants add normalization at both locations around a sublayer.

## Detailed answer

Two common forms are:

$$
\text{Pre-norm: } y=x+F(\operatorname{LN}(x)),\qquad
\text{Post-norm: } y=\operatorname{LN}(x+F(x)).
$$

Pre-norm gives gradients an identity path through the residual stream and often simplifies optimization at depth. It does not by itself bound residual magnitudes. Post-norm can work well with suitable initialization and learning-rate schedules, including deep variants such as DeepNorm. “Sandwich norm” is not a single universally identical implementation; specify the exact equations.

In pre-norm, the derivative of the residual addition contains an identity contribution: a gradient can travel through $y=x+F(\mathrm{LN}(x))$ without passing entirely through the sublayer. This helps explain its optimization behavior. In post-norm, the gradient through $y=\mathrm{LN}(x+F(x))$ also passes through normalization at each block, so initialization and the relative scale of the branches affect propagation differently.

The activation behavior differs too. Pre-norm adds each branch output to an unnormalized residual stream, so its magnitude can grow across layers even though sublayer inputs are normalized. A final normalization is commonly used before prediction. Post-norm explicitly normalizes after each residual addition, but that does not guarantee stable gradients for arbitrary depth and initialization.

For a checkpoint conversion, copying weights while moving the normalization layer is not semantics-preserving. The same matrix now receives a different input. When comparing variants, specify the formula, the final norm, initialization, residual coefficients, and any learned bias. Those details are more useful than assuming “pre” or “post” alone determines accuracy.
