## Question

What is Multi-Token Prediction (MTP), why add an MTP layer, and how does it help at inference time?

## Brief answer

Multi-token prediction trains auxiliary machinery to predict more than one future token, giving additional learning signals beyond the immediate next token. At inference, compatible prediction heads can propose multiple tokens for verification by the main model. This can accelerate decoding when proposals are accepted and verification is efficient, but is not an automatic speedup for every workload.

## Detailed answer

Ordinary causal training predicts $x_{t+1}$ from the prefix through $t$. MTP adds losses for future positions, with the conditioning and module layout depending on the particular design. The extra objectives encourage representations useful across several future steps; their weighting must avoid degrading the main objective.

For speculative use, propose a short continuation, verify it using the target distribution, and accept or correct it with an algorithm that preserves the intended sampling distribution. Naively taking all predicted tokens changes generation semantics. Draft quality, speculation length, verification cost, and batch size determine the gain. Auxiliary heads can also be omitted in an ordinary serving configuration.

### Training and serving are separate decisions

If the prefix ends with “the capital of,” a normal head predicts the next token; an MTP objective also asks representations to support subsequent positions. Training applies additional prediction losses with explicit weights. Depending on the design, later prediction modules condition on earlier future-token information rather than being independent guesses from one unchanged vector. The extra training work is justified only if resulting quality or inference savings compensate for it.

At inference, suppose auxiliary modules propose four tokens. The main model verifies them in a parallel pass; acceptance is high when the proposals agree with its distribution. If only one token survives most rounds, draft computation and cache handling can cost more than ordinary decoding. If several survive, one expensive target invocation advances the sequence by several tokens. Evaluate accepted tokens per round and total milliseconds per output token. Do not report four proposed tokens as a fourfold speedup or assume the auxiliary modules are required for every deployment.
