## Question

A shipped Copilot feature that summarises job applicants for recruiters is accused of working worse for some groups. How do you establish whether that's true, and what do you do about it?

## Brief answer

First determine whether the claimed disparity is real with representative, consented evaluation data and a clear definition of harm. Compare factual accuracy, omission of qualifications, unsupported inferences, and ranking influence across relevant groups and intersections. Investigate the entire pipeline, including input quality and recruiter use, rather than assuming the language model alone caused the problem.

## Detailed answer

Freeze the affected version and preserve reproducible examples under appropriate access controls. Use blinded expert review, consistent rubrics, and confidence intervals; small subgroups may need targeted sampling. Separate differences in available job-relevant evidence from systematic distortion, and never infer protected attributes casually from names or text.

Mitigate severe failures promptly by limiting the feature, showing source evidence, or reverting the change. Audit prompts, data, evaluation coverage, and UI presentation for mechanisms that explain the disparity. Retest proposed fixes on all groups and obtain appropriate employment-domain review before relaunch. Communicate established findings and remaining uncertainty. This is an engineering investigation framework, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific employment or privacy advice.

### Define the disparity before choosing a fix

One measurable harm is omission of relevant qualifications: among applicants whose résumés contain a required certification, how often does the summary omit it? Another is unsupported inference: how often does it add a personality judgment absent from the source? Estimate these rates by appropriately obtained evaluation groups, with uncertainty and intersectional slices where sample size allows. Equal average summary length would not answer either question.

Use matched or carefully constructed counterfactual examples to isolate mechanisms, while recognizing that changing a name alone does not represent every real-world difference. Check OCR quality, document format, language, prompt wording, and recruiter interface before attributing causality to the model. A repair might remove subjective inference fields, extract qualifications into a constrained schema with evidence spans, and require review of missing information. Retest the complete workflow, not only the edited prompt. Keep deployment decisions with responsible domain owners, and communicate what the evidence establishes without overclaiming fairness from one metric.
