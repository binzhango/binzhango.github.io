## Question

GLM-4.5 is an MoE with 355B total but 32B active parameters. Explain the economics: what does that split buy you and what does it cost?

## Brief answer

The active parameter count approximates how much selected model computation a token uses; the total count determines much of storage and deployment capacity. GLM-4.5's 355B-total/32B-active split offers sparse capacity, but not the memory footprint or operational simplicity of a 32B dense model. Expert traffic and utilization determine whether that theoretical advantage becomes a practical saving.

## Detailed answer

Those figures refer to the 2025 GLM-4.5 release, not every model in the family. For a deployment estimate, budget all weights at the chosen precision, cache memory, temporary buffers, and runtime overhead. During training, optimizer state and gradients add substantial memory.

At serving time, distribute experts across devices and account for activation dispatch and combine operations. Small batches may underutilize expert kernels; skewed routes create hotspots. Large batches improve amortization but can worsen tail latency. Benchmark representative prompts and concurrency, including long context, and compare total dollars per successful task with a dense alternative. Do not multiply parameter ratios directly into a claimed latency speedup.

### Separate storage economics from execution economics

At BF16, 355B parameters occupy approximately 710 GB before runtime overhead. The 32B active figure describes which weights participate for a token, not a 64 GB total deployment. An MoE can offer more learned capacity than a similarly expensive dense forward pass, but its memory footprint can force a larger accelerator group even at low traffic.

For a high-concurrency service, many tokens may collectively activate most experts in each scheduling interval. This improves expert matrix sizes but also requires balanced placement and sufficient dispatch bandwidth. At low concurrency, tiny expert batches can waste compute capacity while all experts remain resident. Training adds optimizer state for the whole parameter set and gradients for exercised experts. Compare a dense and sparse candidate by total provisioned hardware hours per correctly completed workload, including standby and failover capacity. Active-parameter count is useful for understanding arithmetic, but insufficient for sizing or pricing.
