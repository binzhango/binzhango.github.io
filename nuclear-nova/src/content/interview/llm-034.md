## Question

What’s the GeLU formula?

## Brief answer

GELU is $\operatorname{GELU}(x)=x\Phi(x)$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

## Detailed answer

Its exact and common approximate forms are:

$$
\operatorname{GELU}(x)=\frac{x}{2}\left[1+\operatorname{erf}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt2}\right)\right],
$$

$$
\operatorname{GELU}(x)\approx\frac{x}{2}\left[1+\tanh\left(\sqrt{2/\pi}(x+0.044715x^3)\right)\right].
$$

Unlike ReLU, GELU smoothly attenuates negative values rather than setting all of them to zero. Exact and approximate implementations have slightly different numerics, so preserve the checkpoint's activation convention.

The factor $\Phi(x)$ acts as a smooth, input-dependent attenuation. At zero, the output is zero and the derivative is one half. At $x=1$, $\Phi(1)\approx0.8413$, so GELU returns about 0.8413; at $x=-1$, it returns about $-0.1587$. A large positive input is almost unchanged, while a sufficiently negative input is pushed toward zero from below.

Its derivative is $\Phi(x)+x\phi(x)$, where $\phi$ is the standard normal density. Consequently, the function is not simply a probabilistic on/off switch executed at runtime, and it is not monotonic everywhere. The smooth transition supplies a different gradient shape from ReLU's hard zeroing of negative values.

In a Transformer FFN, apply GELU to the expanded feature vector before the output projection. Choosing the tanh approximation can change numerical results slightly; using a different activation when loading existing weights changes the model rather than merely optimizing its implementation. Judge speed and accuracy for the actual kernel and model, since a mathematical formula alone does not predict end-to-end throughput.
