## Question

What is the difference between hard and soft attention?

## Brief answer

Soft attention forms a differentiable weighted sum of values; hard attention makes discrete selections whose selection step is generally not differentiable.

## Detailed answer

Standard attention computes weights with softmax and combines all allowed values. Hard attention might sample one location or select a subset; training the selection may require a score-function estimator, relaxation, or another surrogate. A fixed causal or sparse mask does not by itself make attention “hard”: the permitted positions can still receive continuous softmax weights.

Suppose three source values are $v_1,v_2,v_3$ and softmax produces weights $(0.6,0.3,0.1)$. Soft attention returns $0.6v_1+0.3v_2+0.1v_3$. Small changes to the scores change the weights smoothly, allowing ordinary backpropagation through both the scoring function and the values. The result is a mixture, rather than a selected original value.

Hard attention might choose only $v_1$ by an argmax or sample a location according to the weights. Argmax is piecewise constant, so gradients through the discrete choice do not tell the scorer how a different choice would change the loss. Sampling estimators or continuous relaxations address this, each with variance or bias tradeoffs.

Discrete selection can save computation if the implementation avoids processing unselected content, but it can also discard necessary evidence. A top-k retrieval stage followed by soft attention combines hard candidate selection with differentiable mixing inside the selected set. Similarly, setting future-position logits to negative infinity still leaves ordinary soft attention over the allowed past positions.
