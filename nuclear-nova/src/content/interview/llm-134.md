## Question

What are the common PDF parsing methods and their differences?

## Brief answer

PDF processing methods include native text extraction, OCR, layout-aware parsing, and multimodal model-based extraction.

## Detailed answer

Native extraction is often fast for digitally generated PDFs but can lose reading order. OCR recognizes text from page images but can confuse characters. Layout models locate headings, tables, and regions; multimodal models can interpret complex pages but may introduce fabricated content or inconsistent structure. Hybrid pipelines select methods by page type. Evaluate against representative documents and retain visual provenance for uncertain results.

Choose at page level where necessary. A born-digital report with ordinary text can use character extraction and geometric reading-order reconstruction. A scanned appendix needs OCR. A page with complex tables may require table-structure detection even though its text is selectable. A single PDF can contain all three, so one global parser choice can be inefficient or inaccurate.

Native extraction preserves original characters when encoding is valid, but does not automatically recover columns or tables. OCR operates on rendered pixels and can handle scans, but adds recognition errors and resolution-dependent cost. Layout-aware models identify regions and relationships; multimodal models can interpret complex pages but must be checked for omitted or invented content.

Evaluate using task-relevant errors: missing clauses, incorrect numbers, wrong reading order, and broken table headers, not only the percentage of text extracted. Preserve page images and region coordinates for uncertain output. A hybrid pipeline can use cheap extraction first, detect suspicious pages, and escalate selectively. The best method is the one that preserves the evidence needed by downstream questions under an acceptable cost and latency budget.
