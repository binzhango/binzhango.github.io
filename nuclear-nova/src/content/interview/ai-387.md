## Question

Explain position bias in ranking data and how you would debias training.

## Brief answer

Position bias means higher-ranked items receive more attention and clicks even at equal relevance. Training directly on clicks can reinforce the current ranking policy. Estimate examination or exposure propensities and use counterfactual weighting or an appropriate click model, supported by controlled randomization where feasible. Correcting position bias does not remove every other source of selection bias.

## Detailed answer

Log shown items, positions, context, and actual exposure, including non-clicked results. Small randomized swaps or exploration can identify propensity effects under stated assumptions. Inverse propensity weighting upweights feedback from low-exposure positions, but tiny propensities create high variance; clipping trades variance for bias.

Validate propensity estimates and require overlap: items never exposed cannot be evaluated reliably from click logs alone. Account for trust bias, presentation differences, and user intent changes. Use independent relevance judgments and online experiments to check whether debiasing improves outcomes. Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback develops the counterfactual approach and its assumptions.

### A click is conditional on being seen

Under a simplified examination model, click probability is examination probability at the shown position times relevance conditional on examination. A relevant item at position ten may receive fewer clicks than a mediocre item at position one simply because fewer users see it. Training directly on clicks can then reinforce the original ranking.

If a clicked example had exposure propensity 0.1, inverse propensity weighting gives it weight ten, while an example at propensity 0.5 gets weight two. This compensates for unequal observation under the model's assumptions. Very small propensities produce unstable weights, and missing support cannot be fixed: an item never shown has no observed outcome to reweight. Randomized swaps can estimate position effects while limiting user impact. Check whether examination depends on context or presentation, not position alone. Evaluate debiased learning with independent relevance labels and online outcomes; a mathematically corrected estimator can still fail when its propensity model is wrong.
