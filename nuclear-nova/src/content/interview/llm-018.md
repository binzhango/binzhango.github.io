## Question

What is negative log-likelihood? Write the per-token loss for GPT.

## Brief answer

The per-token negative log-likelihood is $\ell_t=-\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})$; GPT-style training averages it over supervised tokens.

## Detailed answer

If $z_{t,v}$ are vocabulary logits predicting token $x_t$, then:

$$
\ell_t=-z_{t,x_t}+\log\sum_{v=1}^{V}e^{z_{t,v}},\qquad
L=\frac{\sum_t m_t\ell_t}{\sum_t m_t}.
$$

Here $m_t$ is 1 for supervised targets and 0 for padding or excluded prompt tokens. The model's output at a sequence position usually predicts the following token, so labels must be shifted correctly. A probability of 0.5 gives about 0.693 nats of loss; 0.01 gives about 4.605 nats. A sequence sum and a per-token mean have different scales.

The log-sum-exp term normalizes logits into a probability distribution. Differentiating the loss with respect to a logit gives $p_v-\mathbf1[v=x_t]$: gradient descent raises the target logit's relative score and lowers competing scores in proportion to their predicted probability. This gives a direct connection between the scalar loss and what learning changes.

Consider targets with predicted probabilities 0.5 and 0.25. Their losses are about 0.693 and 1.386 nats; the mean is 1.040 nats. If the second position is padding and masked out, the correct mean is 0.693, not half that value. The denominator must count supervised targets, and a batch with no supervised targets needs explicit handling instead of division by zero.

For causal training, input tokens at positions 0 through $T-2$ predict targets at positions 1 through $T-1$. Some model implementations perform that shift internally, so shifting again in the caller is a bug. Verify one tiny token sequence manually and inspect loss masks before interpreting full-training curves.
