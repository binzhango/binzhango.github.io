## Question

GLM's original pre-training objective is autoregressive blank infilling. How does it differ from BERT and GPT, and why did the team argue it unifies understanding and generation?

## Brief answer

BERT predicts masked tokens using bidirectional context; GPT predicts the next token from a causal prefix. Original GLM removes spans and generates their contents autoregressively while allowing the visible context to be read bidirectionally. Varying span lengths and using suitable attention and positional encodings lets one objective cover understanding, conditional generation, and unconditional generation.

## Detailed answer

For a sentence with two missing spans, the visible portion supplies context, and each missing span is generated token by token. Unlike predicting independent masked positions, generation can represent variable-length outputs and dependencies within a span. Unlike ordinary left-to-right training, the conditioning text can include words after the blank.

The original GLM design uses two-dimensional positional information to distinguish positions in the original text from positions within generated spans. Its unification claim concerns a training objective and experimental tasks; it is not a claim that all later models bearing the GLM name retain the same objective. Compare architectures at matched data and compute before generalizing benchmark conclusions.

### An example of the three objectives

For “The cat sat on the mat,” BERT-style masking might replace “cat” and predict it using both left and right context. Standard GPT-style training predicts each next token from the preceding tokens and cannot look ahead to “sat” when predicting “cat.” GLM can replace a span such as “cat sat” with a blank, expose the remaining context, then generate the missing span autoregressively.

This combines bidirectional understanding of visible text with sequential generation inside blanks. Attention masks must enforce that distinction: known context can interact appropriately, while a generated span cannot peek at its future target tokens. The second positional coordinate distinguishes successive generated tokens within a blank from their position in the original sequence. Varying blank lengths creates objectives resembling classification-conditioned generation, short completion, and longer text production. The trade-off is more complex input construction and masking than ordinary next-token prediction, and the benefit must be established for the relevant task mixture.
