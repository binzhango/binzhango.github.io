## Question

Your ads CTR model shows a 2% offline AUC gain, but the online A/B is revenue-neutral with worse calibration. What is going on, and what do you do?

## Brief answer

AUC measures ranking across positive and negative examples, not calibrated click probabilities or revenue. An offline AUC gain can coexist with worse calibration, changed auction bids, distribution shift or gains on low-value traffic. Verify the experiment and inspect the decision pipeline before concluding that the model improvement is meaningless.

## Detailed answer

Check randomization, sample-ratio balance, logging and confidence intervals. Determine whether “2%” means relative or absolute AUC change. Compare calibration curves, log loss and expected-versus-observed CTR by segment and score range. A global AUC gain may hide degradation in the high-score candidates that actually enter auctions.

Trace how predicted CTR affects bid ranking, prices, budgets and ad eligibility. Poor probability scale can alter economic decisions even when ordering improves. Delayed conversions, advertiser mix and user response may further disconnect CTR from revenue.

Recalibrate on a representative holdout, rerun auction simulations and test with revenue, user experience and advertiser-value guardrails. Avoid tuning repeatedly on the same experiment. If the gain does not improve the product objective after diagnosis, retain the incumbent and use the failure to improve offline evaluation.

AUC is unchanged by any strictly increasing transformation of scores, but calibration can change dramatically. Squaring probabilities may preserve ranking while making the numerical values wrong. If the auction uses predicted CTR multiplied by bid, those values influence economic decisions, so a ranking gain alone does not establish revenue improvement.

Inspect the region of the score distribution that actually wins impressions. Offline AUC can improve mostly on easy negatives or low-value traffic that never reaches the auction. Training-serving feature differences, delayed labels and changed advertiser budgets can also break the offline-online relationship. Verify sample-ratio balance and confidence intervals before diagnosing a model effect.

Recalibrate using a representative validation set, then evaluate log loss, calibration by segment and simulated auction outcomes without repeatedly tuning on the live experiment. Run another controlled test only after identifying a plausible mechanism. Revenue-neutral results may still hide changes in user experience or advertiser value, so retain those guardrails. If the improvement does not survive the full decision pipeline, keeping the incumbent is a valid outcome.
