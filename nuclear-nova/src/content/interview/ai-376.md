## Question

How would you design the neural network architecture for multi-camera 3D object detection without lidar?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: extract shared visual features from synchronized calibrated cameras, fuse them into a common 3D or bird's-eye-view representation, and predict boxes, classes, velocity, and uncertainty. Temporal fusion helps infer depth and motion, but camera-only depth remains ambiguous. Calibration, timing, and evaluation under occlusion are as important as the network architecture.

## Detailed answer

Use a multiscale image backbone, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and either learned depth lifting or geometry-aware attention to populate a BEV grid. Fuse recent frames after compensating for ego motion, then use detection heads with appropriate matching and losses. BEVFormer's spatial/temporal attention is one concrete example.

Train across weather, illumination, camera exposure, and object distance, with reliable 3D labels. Test calibration drift, dirty lenses, missing cameras, and time skew. Report distance-stratified small-object recall, localization and velocity error, uncertainty calibration, and end-to-end latency. Couple the system to a conservative downstream planner and an explicit operational domain; a benchmark score does not establish safe autonomous operation.

### Geometry turns images into a shared space

Each camera's feature map describes appearance in image coordinates. Intrinsics map pixels to rays, and extrinsics place those rays in the vehicle frame. A learned depth distribution can lift image features along those rays into a bird's-eye-view representation; alternatively, geometry-aware attention samples image features for candidate 3D locations. Multiple cameras contribute overlapping evidence to the same space.

Temporal fusion uses ego-motion estimates to align previous features before combining them. Detection heads then predict 3D centers, sizes, orientation, class, and velocity. Without lidar, depth is especially ambiguous for textureless, distant, or occluded objects, so uncertainty and temporal evidence matter. Train with synchronized calibrated data and audit 3D labels. A small calibration rotation can systematically shift distant detections even when image classification is excellent. Include missing-camera masks and degraded-operation behavior. Judge perception by the planner-relevant errors and operating domain rather than assuming high aggregate detection accuracy establishes safe driving.
