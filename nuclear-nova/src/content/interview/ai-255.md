## Question

A flagship model is a sparse MoE with about ten times more total than active parameters. Why is that architecture a good fit for private enterprise deployment, and where does it hurt?

## Brief answer

A sparse MoE can offer large representational capacity at a lower active arithmetic cost per token, which can be attractive when quality matters and data must stay private. Its full parameter storage and communication requirements can still make private deployments expensive.

## Detailed answer

With ten times more total than active parameters, calculate weight storage from the total, not the active count. A customer's small GPU cluster may lack both memory and fast interconnects, forcing quantization or slow expert offloading. Low-concurrency workloads can underutilize expert kernels; larger batches increase utilization but may exercise many experts and worsen latency. Compare a smaller dense model or distilled specialist at the required quality. Private operation also needs offline updates, observability and access control, none of which MoE routing provides automatically. Make the deployment choice from measured SLO-compliant throughput and total support cost.

### Privacy does not remove the capacity constraint

For an illustrative 300B-total/30B-active model, BF16 weight storage is about 600 GB before cache and buffers, although the active feed-forward computation is much smaller. A private cluster must still store or fetch all experts that traffic may select. Expert offloading to host memory can make capacity feasible while adding unpredictable transfer latency.

Large enterprises with sufficient fast memory and interconnects may benefit from sparse compute at quality levels difficult for a smaller dense model. A small installation with low concurrency may instead pay for many mostly idle experts and obtain worse economics. Measure expert-load skew, batch sizes, cross-device traffic, and SLO-constrained throughput. Quantization and distillation are alternatives, with independent quality checks. Private deployment also includes artifact updates, monitoring, support, security, and failover responsibility. Compare total annual operating cost and required task quality rather than presenting MoE as inherently enterprise-friendly; its value depends on the workload and the infrastructure available inside the boundary.
