## Question

Do different domains require their own domain-specific LLMs? Why?

## Brief answer

No. Start with a capable general model and evaluate whether prompting, retrieval, or task adaptation resolves the domain gap.

## Detailed answer

RAG is useful for changing or private facts. SFT helps with terminology, response style, procedures, and task behavior. Continued pretraining may help when the model lacks broad domain-language competence and sufficient quality data exists. A dedicated model adds training, evaluation, deployment, and maintenance costs. Choose based on observed errors, privacy requirements, and serving constraints rather than the existence of a domain label.

Diagnose what “domain gap” means. If the model lacks a recently changed company policy, retrieval from the authoritative policy is usually easier to update and audit than weight training. If it knows the facts but consistently writes the wrong report format, supervised demonstrations can teach the desired behavior. If it struggles to parse domain terminology and conventions across many tasks, broader domain adaptation may be justified.

For a technical support assistant, start with a fixed evaluation set containing current product facts, diagnostic reasoning, formatting requirements, and unanswerable cases. Compare a general model with a stronger prompt, then with retrieval. Fine-tune only when remaining failures are plausibly learnable from available data, and retain general-capability regression tests.

A separate domain model must be trained, served, updated, and evaluated as facts and workflows change. Limited, repetitive domain data can reduce general ability without teaching robust expertise. A smaller adapted model may still be attractive for privacy, offline operation, or high-volume cost constraints. The decision follows observed failure causes and operating economics; the existence of specialized vocabulary alone does not establish a need for a new model.
