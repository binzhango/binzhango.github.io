## Question

You have dozens of ranking signals and a brand-new tenant with zero interaction data. How do you rank, and how do you improve?

## Brief answer

Start with content relevance, exact matches, source quality, freshness, and explicit organizational metadata. Use broadly trained ranking signals only where they transfer, and avoid pretending sparse tenant data supports personalized models. Collect privacy-appropriate interaction evidence gradually while correcting for exposure bias.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Establish a strong hybrid lexical/dense baseline and a small set of interpretable business features. Ask administrators for authoritative sources and useful evaluation queries rather than requiring a large click history. Use a shared model with tenant-safe features, then calibrate on tenant-specific judgments as evidence grows.

Instrument impressions, result positions, clicks, reformulations, and successful downstream actions, but do not treat clicks as relevance truth. Run controlled exploration with guardrails and offline judged sets before retraining. Segment metrics by query type and source coverage so connector gaps are not blamed on ranking. Evaluate cold-start tenants separately from mature ones. Revert learned personalization if it reduces access diversity or amplifies stale, popular documents at the expense of correct answers.

### Learn from exposure, not clicks alone

At launch, rank using features that exist before any tenant interaction: lexical and semantic match, document type, freshness where relevant, explicit ownership, and administrator-designated authoritative sources. Validate with a small customer-owned judged set. A document with no clicks may simply never have been displayed, so absence of engagement is not evidence of irrelevance.

Log impressions with positions and the eligible candidate set. If a result is always first, its higher click rate may reflect placement rather than quality. Controlled exploration or interleaving can collect comparison evidence, but only within permission and quality constraints. Do not experiment by showing clearly irrelevant or restricted content.

Use learning only where data volume supports it and retain a stable fallback. A tenant with sparse traffic may benefit more from fixing connector coverage or synonym mappings than training a personalized model. Track performance by query type and tenant age so mature customers do not hide poor cold-start behavior. An effective progression is a strong content baseline, explicit judged feedback, then carefully validated behavioral signals as the evidence grows.
