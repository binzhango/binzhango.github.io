## Question

Do 1 million Seattle ride trips suffice to build an accurate ETA prediction model? How would you decide?

## Brief answer

A million trips may be enough for some ETA predictions and insufficient for others. Determine coverage of routes, times, traffic regimes and rare events, then use temporal holdouts and learning curves. Sample count alone does not reveal label quality, distribution shift or the effective number of independent situations represented.

## Detailed answer

Start with the prediction target: pickup ETA, remaining-trip time or total duration require different features and labels. Establish a routing/time-of-day baseline, inspect GPS and timestamp errors, and separate completed-trip selection effects from actual demand. Repeated popular routes may dominate the million examples.

Split chronologically and evaluate by neighborhood, trip length, hour, weather and unusual congestion. Measure absolute error and calibration of prediction intervals, not only average percentage error that can behave poorly on very short trips. Plot performance versus training size to see whether more similar data still helps.

Compare prospective predictions with outcomes during a shadow deployment. New road patterns, events and seasonal conditions may require fresher or more diverse data rather than simply more rows. Define the business tolerance first; “accurate” is not a measurable requirement until acceptable errors and affected users are specified.

One million rows can contain far fewer independent situations if most rides repeat a few popular routes at similar times. A rare snowstorm, stadium event or newly closed road may be almost absent. Coverage of conditions matters more than the headline count, and fresher data can be more valuable than another million near-duplicates.

Define the prediction moment and available features precisely. Using actual trip distance or traffic observed after departure can create leakage when predicting at booking time. Split by time and inspect spatial coverage; random splits can make repeated routes look easier than future deployment. Compare with a route-engine baseline and simple historical averages.

Use learning curves to distinguish data scarcity from model or feature limitations. If error stops improving with more similar data, targeted collection or better real-time traffic features may help more. Evaluate absolute errors and prediction intervals for short and long trips, plus operational tolerance for missed pickup promises. A shadow deployment provides a prospective check before users rely on the estimates.
