## Question

Compare Cross-Attention and Self-Attention — similarities and differences.

## Brief answer

Self-attention and cross-attention use the same query-key-value operation but obtain those tensors from different inputs.

## Detailed answer

Self-attention uses one sequence for Q, K, and V. Cross-attention uses target states for Q and source states for K/V. Attention scores therefore have shape $n\times n$ for self-attention and $n_q\times n_k$ for cross-attention. Causality is a masking decision, not a defining property of cross-attention; a translation decoder usually reads all unpadded source positions while masking future positions in its separate self-attention layer.

The difference is easiest to see through data flow. In self-attention over a sentence, the representation of “bank” can read neighboring words such as “river” to resolve its meaning. In translation cross-attention, the representation of the next target token reads encoded source words to decide what source information to express. Both use dot products, masking, softmax, and weighted value aggregation.

Self-attention can be bidirectional or causal. Cross-attention is often unrestricted over a completed source, but streaming tasks may restrict source positions to information already received. Calling an operation cross-attention does not make it immune to future-information leakage; the source availability and mask must match deployment.

Their costs also follow different lengths. A single-stream self-attention matrix scales with $n^2$, whereas a cross-attention matrix scales with target length times source length. Reusing fixed source K/V projections helps incremental generation, but reading a long source remains expensive. When debugging, verify tensor origins and axis meanings, not only shapes: accidentally passing target states as values can produce a shape-correct computation that no longer retrieves source information.
