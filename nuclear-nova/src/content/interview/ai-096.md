## Question

Design a diarisation system and explain how you attribute roles, not just clusters.

## Brief answer

Diarization answers “who spoke when” by segmenting speech and clustering speaker representations. Assigning roles such as clinician and patient needs additional evidence; an anonymous acoustic cluster is not a verified identity.

## Detailed answer

Detect speech, extract embeddings, cluster or track speakers, and handle overlap explicitly. Align resulting intervals to transcript words. Role assignment can use authenticated meeting metadata or carefully reviewed conversational cues, with an unknown state when evidence is insufficient. Evaluate diarization error and speaker-attributed transcription separately. Test short turns, similar voices, background speakers and changes in microphones.

A typical pipeline detects speech regions, extracts speaker embeddings from segments, groups similar embeddings and refines boundaries. Online diarization must make provisional assignments with limited context, while offline processing can revisit earlier decisions. Overlapping speech is a separate challenge because one interval can contain more than one speaker; forcing a single label can create systematic errors.

Acoustic clusters are anonymous. “Speaker 2” may correspond to the clinician, patient or someone in the background, and a microphone change can split one person into multiple clusters. Role attribution should use authenticated participant metadata, explicit introductions or reviewed conversational evidence. Keep an unknown or uncertain role rather than assigning one from a stereotype or a single sentence.

Align speaker intervals with word timestamps and retain confidence and revision history. If a diarization update changes the owner of an action item, downstream summaries may need review. Evaluate missed speech, false speech and speaker confusion, plus speaker-attributed transcription and role accuracy. Test similar voices, short interjections, overlapping turns, remote participants and device changes. A low clustering error on long clean recordings does not guarantee correct attribution in a real meeting where the most important statement is a two-word interruption.
