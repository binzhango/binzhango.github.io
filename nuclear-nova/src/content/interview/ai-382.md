## Question

Disengagement rate is a weak proxy. How would you actually measure whether an autonomy release is safer than the last one?

## Brief answer

Compare safety-relevant outcomes within matched operational domains and exposure, supported by scenario testing and a documented safety argument. Disengagements depend on driver behavior, test policy, and route difficulty, so their raw rate is not a reliable standalone measure. Rare serious events also require uncertainty bounds rather than declaring safety from a short zero-event period.

## Detailed answer

Use severity-weighted collision and near-collision analysis, rule compliance, time-to-collision where appropriate, and responses to vulnerable road users. Stratify by weather, road type, traffic, and maneuver, and account for changes in exposure. Review root causes rather than treating all events as exchangeable counts.

Combine replay, simulation with validated realism, closed-course tests, fault injection, and controlled on-road evidence. Define acceptance criteria before comparing releases, preserve difficult historical scenarios, and use independent review for severe changes. Simulation supports specific claims under its assumptions; it cannot prove universal safety. Waymo's safety-case framework discusses the relationship between claims, acceptance criteria, and evidence.

### Define the claim and its denominator

“Safer” must identify the operating domain and outcomes: fewer severe conflicts with pedestrians, better response to emergency vehicles, or fewer collisions per comparable exposure. Disengagement depends on operator policy and caution, so a change in that rate may reflect changed intervention behavior rather than improved autonomy.

Compare matched route, weather, traffic, and maneuver distributions. Rare severe events need large exposure and uncertainty bounds; zero observed crashes in a small trial does not establish a zero rate. Use scenario-level evidence for known hazards and system-level analysis for failure containment. A new perception model may improve detection but interact poorly with planning, so evaluate the complete stack. Predefine acceptance criteria and retain difficult historical cases. Independent review should examine why a scenario improved and whether the change creates new hazards. Simulation is valuable when its assumptions are validated, but it cannot replace evidence about conditions it does not model.
