## Question

What is information gain?

## Brief answer

Information gain is the reduction in uncertainty about a target after observing a feature or split.

## Detailed answer

For a decision-tree split $S$ and target $Y$:

$$
\operatorname{IG}(Y;S)=H(Y)-\sum_sP(S=s)H(Y\mid S=s).
$$

A split that separates classes well lowers conditional entropy and produces higher gain. For a balanced binary target, entropy is 1 bit; a perfect split reduces it to zero and gains 1 bit. Estimate probabilities from training data, and constrain tree complexity to avoid fitting accidental purity. Entropy is not the only possible tree criterion; Gini impurity is another.

For a non-perfect example, take eight training records with four positive and four negative labels, so the parent entropy is one bit. A proposed split creates two equal-sized children, one with three positives and one negative, the other with one positive and three negatives. Each child's entropy is

$$
-\tfrac34\log_2\tfrac34-\tfrac14\log_2\tfrac14\approx0.811\ \text{bits}.
$$

The weighted child entropy is also 0.811, so the gain is about 0.189 bits. A split that leaves both children balanced gains zero; a perfect separation gains one bit.

The weights must reflect child sample proportions. Averaging child entropies equally when child sizes differ computes the wrong criterion. A feature with many possible categories can create nearly pure small groups by chance, so unconstrained information gain can favor overfitting. Minimum leaf sizes, depth constraints, pruning, and held-out evaluation help control this.

Information gain measures predictive dependence in the sampled data, not causality. A timestamp or post-outcome field can have excellent gain while being invalid for deployment. Check that the feature is available at the actual prediction time.
