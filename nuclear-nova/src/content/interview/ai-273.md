## Question

DeepSeek reports frontier-class benchmark results at relatively low training-run cost. How can architectural and systems choices make this possible, and how should the cost claim be interpreted?

## Brief answer

Separate algorithmic efficiency, hardware utilization, data efficiency, and accounting. Sparse experts reduce active computation; optimized precision and communication improve utilization; good data and post-training improve quality per training token. The published training-run estimate is not the company's total research cost, and benchmark parity is workload-specific rather than a universal equivalence.

## Detailed answer

First compare equal scopes: hardware type, GPU-hours, precision, context length, tokens, and which training stages are included. DeepSeek-V3 reports 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours; translating that into dollars requires a rental-rate assumption. That is distinct from data acquisition, salaries, exploratory experiments, and unsuccessful runs.

Then explain the mechanisms without claiming any one is magic. MoE spends computation on selected experts but still stores all experts. Precision reduces arithmetic and memory traffic, subject to stability controls. Better scheduling and kernels reduce idle time. Data selection and distillation can improve the learning signal. Finally reproduce representative evaluations with matched inference budgets, prompts, and tool access. Training efficiency, serving cost, and product reliability are separate quantities; a cheaper final training run does not establish the other two.

### An efficiency budget

Write final-run compute cost approximately as required useful operations divided by sustained useful operations per GPU-hour, multiplied by the hourly rate. Architecture can reduce the numerator; kernels, precision, and scheduling can increase the denominator. For an MoE, routed experts reduce feed-forward operations per token, while expert communication and attention remain. MLA chiefly changes attention/cache behavior; it should not be credited with every observed training saving.

As a transparent accounting example, 2.788 million GPU-hours at an assumed $2 per GPU-hour gives $5.576 million. Doubling that assumed rate doubles the dollar estimate without changing the model or experiment. An organization may have incurred much greater total expense discovering the final recipe. Evaluate downstream utility separately: equal benchmark scores can conceal different reasoning budgets, reliability, languages, or agent tool access. The credible claim is a scoped, reproducible efficiency result, not that the whole research program or every competing system has the quoted cost.
