## Question

How do you monitor a deployed ranking model for drift, and what triggers a retrain?

## Brief answer

Monitor feature freshness and distributions, prediction distributions, exposure patterns, and delayed outcome quality. Drift is a diagnostic signal, not an automatic instruction to retrain. Trigger investigation or retraining when a validated performance decline, new regime, or coverage gap warrants it, and distinguish data-pipeline failures from genuine changes in user behavior.

## Detailed answer

Track missing values, stale features, out-of-vocabulary categories, candidate recall, rank-score calibration, and business outcomes by cohort. Compare against seasonal baselines and a stable control where available. Use delayed labels carefully: a temporary lack of conversions may reflect label arrival rather than model failure.

If a schema or serving bug caused the drift, fix or roll back the pipeline first. If the distribution genuinely changed, gather representative labels and train a challenger with historical regression coverage. Validate offline, shadow predictions, and run a staged experiment before promotion. Automating the mechanics is useful, but preserve explicit quality and fairness gates so frequent retraining cannot silently entrench a feedback loop.

### Alert on causes and outcomes separately

A sudden rise in missing features is a data-quality incident even before delayed business labels arrive. A stable feature distribution with worsening conversion calibration suggests a different problem. Compare candidate recall, ranking output, latency/fallback rates, and cohort outcomes so one aggregate drift score does not trigger the same response to every failure.

Use a retraining trigger with evidence: sufficient new representative labels, a persistent quality gap, and a known distribution change. Calendar-based retraining can be useful operationally, but a fresh model should still beat the current one on a fixed regression suite and recent holdout. Preserve label observation windows to avoid training on incomplete negatives. After shadow comparison, canary the challenger and monitor delayed outcomes before full promotion. If a schema change caused the issue, retraining on corrupted features can hide the bug and make recovery harder. Keep model, feature, and candidate-generator versions linked in traces.
