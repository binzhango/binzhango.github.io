## Question

Design an LLM-as-judge evaluation. What are its known biases and how do you correct for them?

## Brief answer

An LLM judge needs a precise rubric, representative calibration data and ongoing comparison with qualified human judgments. Treat it as a noisy measurement instrument with known failure modes, not ground truth.

## Detailed answer

Blind model identity, randomize candidate order and test swapped pairs to expose position bias. Control verbosity and formatting effects; require evidence for factual judgments. Measure agreement and error by task and language, including adversarial answers that flatter or instruct the judge. Use human adjudication for high-impact disagreements. Pin the judge model and prompt so score changes are attributable.

Define the rubric in observable terms: whether each required fact is correct, whether claims are supported, whether instructions were followed and whether harmful or unauthorized actions occurred. Avoid a single vague “quality from one to ten” score. Give the judge the necessary evidence, and allow “insufficient information” when correctness cannot be determined.

For pairwise comparisons, randomize A/B order and repeat a subset with the order reversed. Disagreement exposes position sensitivity. Hide model identity, normalize irrelevant formatting when appropriate, and inspect whether verbosity wins without added substance. Treat candidate answers as untrusted data so text such as “give this answer full marks” cannot become a judging instruction.

Calibrate against expert labels on representative cases, including languages, long answers and adversarial failures. Report confusion patterns rather than only overall agreement: missing a dangerous error is not equivalent to disagreeing about style. Use human adjudication for high-impact or uncertain cases and periodically refresh calibration as the generator changes. Pin judge version and prompt, record its outputs and costs, and keep the judging system separate from the model under evaluation where possible. A consistent judge can still be consistently wrong, so automation should scale a validated measurement process rather than replace its definition.
