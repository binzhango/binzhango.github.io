## Question

Design the ETA prediction system for a ride-hailing marketplace. What features, what model, how do you serve it in <100 ms?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: combine routing-derived travel-time estimates with a learned residual or calibrated predictor using current traffic, location, time, and trip context. Serve precomputed and fresh features through a low-latency path, with a deterministic routing fallback. Define whether ETA means driver arrival, pickup completion, or destination arrival because labels and operational decisions differ.

## Detailed answer

Use map-matched GPS, route segments, historical speed profiles, weather where reliable, demand, and recent traffic. Train with chronological splits and prevent future traffic or completed-trip information from leaking into features. Account for GPS noise, canceled trips, and waiting time separately.

For a sub-100 ms service budget, precompute route or segment features, cache common components, and keep the online model compact. Return a calibrated interval as well as a point estimate when useful. Measure absolute error, tail error, calibration, and bias by geography and trip duration; percentage error is unstable for very short trips. Monitor drift, stale traffic, and map updates. Validate impact on dispatch and user behavior rather than optimizing prediction error alone.

### Predict the pieces that matter

An ETA can separate pickup waiting time, route travel time, and final access time. Route travel time can aggregate segment-level speed estimates with a learned correction for intersections, turns, congestion, and driver context. Keep features available at request time; using the eventual chosen route or completed-trip traffic in training creates leakage.

An illustrative online path spends 20 ms retrieving route/traffic features, 10 ms model inference, and the remaining budget on gateway/network/headroom. Precompute expensive map features and use a compact ranker or regression model rather than loading large raw trajectories per request. Return a quantile interval when uncertainty is useful, and evaluate coverage as well as median error. Separate pickup and in-trip ETA because their failure mechanisms differ. Monitor stale traffic and map revisions, use a baseline route-time fallback during outages, and evaluate how ETA changes affect rider cancellations and dispatch decisions. Optimizing mean absolute error alone can hide costly systematic underestimation.
