## Question

LLMs are non-deterministic, but a refund over \$200 must never be auto-approved. Where's the line between prompting and code?

## Brief answer

Enforce the \$200 auto-approval ceiling in the refund service using authenticated context and exact currency arithmetic. The model may gather facts and propose a refund, but it must not authorize the exception. Evaluate the total relevant refund amount and cumulative behavior so splitting a larger refund into smaller calls cannot bypass the rule.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Convert approved currency amounts to integer minor units and apply a versioned policy before any payment call. For USD, an amount greater than 20,000 cents requires the designated approval path; clarify separately whether exactly \$200 is permitted. Bind the decision to customer, order, currency, previously refunded amount, and actor permissions.

Use a transaction or lock to prevent concurrent requests from exceeding the limit. Return structured outcomes such as approved, approval_required, rejected, or unknown; the model must explain those outcomes without reinterpreting them. Test \$199.99, \$200.00, \$200.01, negative values, alternate currencies, duplicate requests, and concurrent partial refunds. Prompts can improve conversation quality, but only server-side enforcement can provide a dependable invariant across model versions and adversarial inputs.

### Guard the cumulative invariant atomically

Assume the policy applies to the cumulative automatically approved refund for one order. Two concurrent requests for 150 dollars each are individually below the ceiling but together violate it. In one transaction, lock or compare-and-swap the order's refund state, add the proposed amount to the already approved and reserved total, and decide whether the new total remains at or below 20,000 cents. Reserve the amount before calling the payment provider so in-flight requests count too.

Bind retries to an idempotency key. Replaying the same approved request returns its prior decision rather than consuming the allowance again. A timeout after provider submission enters an unknown state until reconciliation; releasing the reservation immediately could allow another refund while the first later succeeds.

Clarify currency and scope with the policy owner: an order-level USD threshold is different from a per-customer daily threshold or a multi-currency equivalent. The service rejects unsupported currency interpretation instead of letting the model improvise an exchange rate. Tests must include concurrent execution and duplicate delivery, because sequential boundary tests alone cannot demonstrate the invariant under real request races.
