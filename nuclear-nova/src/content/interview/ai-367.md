## Question

How do you evaluate a clustering model's effectiveness without pre-labelled groups?

## Brief answer

Without ground-truth groups, evaluate geometry, stability, interpretability, and usefulness for the downstream task. Internal scores such as silhouette or Davies–Bouldin measure properties of the chosen representation and distance metric, not whether clusters reflect meaningful real-world categories. Different cluster shapes and densities can make those metrics misleading.

## Detailed answer

Check how assignments change under resampling, small feature perturbations, and reasonable hyperparameter choices. Inspect cluster sizes, outliers, and representative points, and ask domain experts whether the partitions support a useful action. Compare against simple baselines and a no-clustering workflow.

Select a distance metric and scaling appropriate to the data; Euclidean distance over mixed unscaled features may produce meaningless groups. For density-based clustering, account for noise points rather than rewarding arbitrary exclusion of difficult observations. If clusters drive campaigns or anomaly review, measure downstream outcome improvements through a valid experiment. A stable cluster may still encode an irrelevant artifact, so inspect provenance and potential leakage.

### Internal quality is not the same as usefulness

Silhouette compares within-cluster distance with distance to the nearest other cluster; it favors compact, separated groups under the chosen metric. Density-based or elongated meaningful clusters may score poorly under that geometry. Conversely, clustering images by camera watermark can yield clean separation with no value for the intended task.

Inspect representative examples, boundary points, and clusters dominated by one data source. Refit on bootstrap samples and measure whether similar groups recur; instability suggests the solution depends heavily on sampling or hyperparameters. For customer segmentation, test whether clusters support different effective actions in a controlled experiment rather than merely producing interesting names. Reserve a small human-labeled audit when possible, even if full ground truth is unavailable. Compare several reasonable feature scalings and distance measures. Report noise/unassigned points and tiny clusters honestly; excluding difficult observations can make internal metrics look better while reducing practical coverage.
