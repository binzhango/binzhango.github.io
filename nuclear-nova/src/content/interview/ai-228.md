## Question

Design an end-to-end classification pipeline for Marketplace listings.

## Brief answer

Use a versioned ingestion pipeline that combines listing text, images and structured attributes, predicts a category hierarchy, validates consistency and routes uncertain cases for review. Include duplicate, spam and policy checks as separately measurable decisions.

## Detailed answer

Define labels and handle ambiguous or multi-category items before training. Split by seller and time to prevent near-duplicate leakage, and audit annotation disagreement. Fuse text/image signals while preserving missing-modality behavior; a misleading photo should not silently override authoritative attributes. Measure per-category precision/recall and calibrated abstention, especially for rare categories. Serve a fast classifier with an escalation path for uncertain listings. Monitor feature drift, seller abuse and downstream correction rates, and retain the listing/model version needed to explain or reverse a classification. Deletion must propagate to derived indexes and review queues.

### Classification should produce an actionable result

At ingestion, validate listing fields, normalize text, generate image features, and predict a hierarchical category such as Home → Furniture → Chairs. A hierarchy allows the system to return a broad category when fine-grained confidence is inadequate. Keep seller-provided attributes and model evidence visible for conflict resolution; an image of a box may not identify the product inside it.

Train with audited labels and examples from new sellers and later periods. Duplicate photos or relisted products across splits can make evaluation unrealistically easy. Use per-category thresholds and an abstention/review route for rare or ambiguous items. Separate category prediction from policy enforcement and duplicate detection so each decision has a clear error cost. Log seller corrections as feedback, but audit them because adversarial sellers may manipulate labels. Serve through a versioned model and feature contract, and compare category-specific quality and downstream search success after rollout. A high overall accuracy dominated by common categories can conceal unusable performance in important rare categories.
