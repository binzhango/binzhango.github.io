## Question

Qwen ships open weights that top public leaderboards. As the release engineer, how do you make sure the benchmark numbers are trustworthy and not contaminated?

## Brief answer

Treat a benchmark release as a reproducible experiment: freeze model and harness versions, disclose prompts and budgets, audit overlap with training data, and use fresh private or time-separated tests. Open weights make independent evaluation easier, but do not make contamination impossible. Report uncertainty and failures instead of selecting only favorable benchmark settings.

## Detailed answer

Deduplicate training corpora against question text, answers, and recognizable variants when provenance permits. Search near-duplicates and translations as well as exact matches. Record benchmark access during development; repeated tuning on a public score creates leakage even without literal training copies.

Publish evaluation scripts, dependencies, sampling settings, context limits, tool permissions, and aggregation rules. Distinguish one-shot success from pass-at-k and report inference cost. Reproduce a subset independently and inspect suspiciously memorized solutions. Test newly authored tasks with changed entities and constraints to see whether ability transfers. Contamination checks are evidence, not proof of absence, especially for opaque upstream data. Establish release gates on private product-representative tasks alongside public leaderboards.

### Reproducibility needs a frozen evaluation contract

For each reported score, record the exact task version, split, prompt format, answer extraction, decoding parameters, tool access, retries, and failure handling. A math score can change dramatically when the evaluator accepts multiple attempts or extracts a number differently. Publish the raw per-item outcome where licensing permits, so an aggregate can be recomputed.

Use perturbation tests with care. Replacing names or numbers can reveal memorization, but may also change task difficulty; inspect whether the modified answer remains valid. Temporal holdouts reduce one contamination path but do not guarantee absence of related training examples. Separate benchmark development data from the release gate and avoid selecting checkpoints repeatedly on the final holdout. A trustworthy release includes uncertainty and known limitations, such as inaccessible upstream corpora. Public rank is one signal; private deployment tasks and reproducible evaluation establish whether the claimed capability transfers.
