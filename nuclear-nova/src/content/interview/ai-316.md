## Question

A state agency wants to move a paper-and-call-centre welfare-scheme service onto a multilingual assistant, on-prem for data residency. How do you scope and ship it?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: start with one well-defined welfare workflow and approved policy sources, then add multilingual assistance and human escalation before automating consequential actions. On-premises deployment is a constraint on every data path, not just model weights. Success is accurate service completion and equitable access, not the number of conversations handled automatically.

## Detailed answer

Map current paper forms, call-center decisions, identity checks, document requirements, and appeal paths. Have agency owners resolve conflicting policy documents and set update responsibility. Build retrieval over versioned approved content and use deterministic validation for eligibility fields; the model should explain and collect information, not invent policy.

Inventory logs, telemetry, speech services, backups, and support access for residency requirements. Pilot with staff, then a limited user cohort across languages, accents, accessibility needs, and low-connectivity conditions. Test stale policy, missing documents, ambiguous identity, and service outages. Measure wrong guidance, abandonment, successful applications, and human correction rates. Keep audit trails and a human path for disputed or unsupported cases. Obtain domain-owner review before expanding automated decisions.

### Ship one bounded service journey

Choose a first workflow such as explaining required documents and preparing an application draft. Ingest agency-approved policy versions, identify the applicant's chosen language, retrieve the applicable scheme rules, and collect fields through a validated form. A deterministic rule service can check explicit eligibility conditions; disputed or exceptional cases remain with trained staff. The assistant should never silently convert uncertain conversational information into a final eligibility decision.

Keep speech recognition, retrieval, model serving, logs, backups, and update delivery within the approved deployment boundary. Assign owners for document freshness and model operations before launch. Pilot with call-center staff using representative cases, then release to a limited public cohort with immediate transfer to a person. Test power/network outages, outdated documents, incomplete applications, and repeated submissions. Track completion and wrong-guidance rates by language and accessibility needs. Expand only after the agency can operate, update, and recover the first workflow reliably.
