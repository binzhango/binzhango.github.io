## Question

Tell me about a time you owned an ambiguous problem end-to-end without much direction.

## Brief answer

Choose a real example where the goal or ownership was unclear, then show how you clarified the outcome, identified stakeholders and delivered a bounded result. Emphasize decisions you made independently and the points where seeking input reduced risk.

## Detailed answer

Structure the response around the initial ambiguity, your hypothesis, the first reversible experiment and the feedback that changed the plan. Explain how you established a success measure and communicated tradeoffs rather than waiting for a perfect specification. Name one constraint you preserved and one assumption you revised. Report only measured results you can substantiate, separating your contribution from the team's. A useful reflection identifies how you would shorten the learning cycle next time. Do not invent a dramatic incident; a modest project with clear ownership can demonstrate the same skill.

### Explain how you created a decision boundary

Choose an actual project where a vague request such as “make onboarding easier” lacked a clear metric or owner. Explain how you identified users, observed the workflow, and narrowed the first outcome to something measurable. **Illustrative pattern:** prototype one confusing step, test it with a few users, then revise the scope when the real bottleneck turns out to be missing data rather than interface design.

State what you decided independently, which assumptions you documented, and where you sought input because authority or consequences required it. Describe one reversible experiment and the evidence that changed your plan. Include delivery, validation, and follow-through rather than stopping at a clever proposal. Report actual results and acknowledge contributions; do not adopt the illustrative scenario as biography. The reflection should identify a concrete improvement in your process, such as testing the riskiest assumption earlier or establishing an accountable decision owner sooner. Ownership includes managing uncertainty, not pretending it never existed.
