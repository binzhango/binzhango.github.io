## Question

Whisper transcribes Hinglish poorly, often forcing output into one language or hallucinating. Why, and how would you build an ASR that handles code-mixed speech?

## Brief answer

Code-mixed speech challenges both acoustic recognition and the language model's tendency to prefer one language or script. Names, accents, transliteration, and training-data imbalance can cause substitutions; silence or noise can also produce hallucinations. Diagnose errors by slice before assuming every Whisper checkpoint has the same behavior.

## Detailed answer

Create aligned Hinglish data with an explicit output convention: preserve spoken language, choose scripts deliberately, and standardize names and numerals. Fine-tune or adapt a multilingual ASR with code-mixed speech, telephone noise, and realistic turn boundaries. Avoid forcing a single language token when it suppresses the other language; compare decoding settings empirically.

Use contextual vocabulary for domain names without allowing it to overwhelm acoustics. Gate empty or nonspeech segments with VAD and confidence checks, and ask confirmation for account numbers or consequential entities. Evaluate mixed error rates, named-entity accuracy, script consistency, and hallucinations on silence as well as word error rate. Whisper's weakly supervised training and limitations are described in the original paper; production handling requires target-domain measurements.

### The output convention is part of the target

In code-mixed speech, a speaker may use Hindi syntax, English product names, and digits in one sentence. If training transcripts inconsistently translate English words into Hindi or change scripts, the ASR learns an ambiguous target. Decide whether transcripts preserve the spoken words, which scripts are acceptable, and how names and numbers are normalized; evaluate against that convention.

Separate acoustic errors from language-model bias. Compare recognition of the same words in clean speech, telephone audio, and mixed-language context. If a name is acoustically present but replaced by a common word, add representative vocabulary and training examples; if it is obscured by noise, decoding constraints cannot recover unavailable evidence. Silence hallucinations need nonspeech detection and conservative output handling. Report entity error rates for scheme names, dates, and amounts, because a small overall word error rate can still make a citizen-service workflow unusable. Confirm consequential entities rather than assuming transcription confidence is calibrated.
