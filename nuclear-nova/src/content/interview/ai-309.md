## Question

Sarvam-M ships hybrid think/non-think modes and was post-trained with SFT then RLVR. How would you build that, and why RLVR over vanilla RLHF?

## Brief answer

Build the two modes with explicit mode-conditioned supervised examples, then use verifiable rewards on tasks with dependable checkers. Sarvam-M's model card documents hybrid reasoning, but RLVR is not a universal replacement for human feedback: it addresses automatically verifiable correctness, while helpfulness and culturally appropriate language require other evaluation signals.

## Detailed answer

Sarvam describes SFT followed by a GRPO-based RLVR curriculum in its May 2025 technical article. For a general recipe, begin with multilingual instruction data and preserve both direct-answer and reasoning examples. Use math answer checkers, sandboxed coding tests, and carefully constructed language constraints for RL. Audit verifiers for translation errors, script biases, and exploits, then evaluate on native-language holdouts.

At serving time, apply the checkpoint's documented chat template, bound reasoning tokens, and keep a usable final-answer budget. Track mode-specific latency and quality. The published Sarvam-M card identifies it as a multilingual hybrid-reasoning model built on Mistral-Small; do not extend claims from that checkpoint to all Sarvam models. For subjective conversation quality, combine human review or preference feedback with factual checks rather than pretending every desired behavior has an exact verifier.

### Construct the training stages

SFT establishes the requested language, instruction following, output format, and mode behavior. RLVR then samples responses to tasks with independently checkable outcomes and updates the policy toward higher-reward responses. A group-relative method compares several candidates for the same prompt, reducing reliance on a separately learned value model, but still needs meaningful variation in rewards.

For an Indic arithmetic task, verify the numerical result across accepted numeral scripts and reject answers that change units. For code, execute hidden tests in a sandbox. A verifier that assumes English formatting may penalize a correct regional-language answer and teach the wrong behavior. Keep native-language direct responses in the mixture so reasoning training does not cause needless verbosity or language switching. Evaluate against an SFT-only baseline to determine what RL actually improves. Learned preferences remain useful for tone and usefulness where no exact verifier exists; RLVR is a reward choice suited to particular tasks, not a universal replacement for human judgment.
