## Question

How would you improve an on-device model using signals from user devices without collecting user content?

## Brief answer

Use signals that minimize or avoid raw-content collection: aggregate acceptance/correction rates, privacy-preserving evaluation results, and, where appropriate, federated updates with secure aggregation and differential privacy. None is automatically private merely because it runs on-device. Define the threat model, consent, retention, and privacy budget before deciding which signals are acceptable.

## Detailed answer

Instrument outcomes such as feature completion, cancellation, latency, and whether a suggestion was accepted, while avoiding identifying text or sensitive metadata. These proxies can be biased: acceptance may reflect convenience rather than correctness. Validate them against consented research data and controlled test sets.

Federated learning can keep examples local, but individual gradients may leak information. Secure aggregation reduces server visibility into individual updates; differential privacy bounds a defined disclosure risk at a measurable utility cost. Evaluate subgroup coverage because devices that participate may not represent all users. Maintain red-team tests and staged releases. If useful quality measurement is impossible under the privacy constraints, narrow the feature rather than quietly collecting more content.

### Private measurement still needs a causal question

An increased acceptance rate for reply suggestions could mean better suggestions, more convenient placement, or users clicking accidentally. Design controlled feature experiments and compare several minimized signals, such as acceptance followed by edit or cancellation, rather than treating one counter as correctness. Collect only the aggregate categories needed for the decision and specify retention and access controls.

For federated learning, selected devices compute local updates, which are clipped and combined under a defined privacy protocol. Secure aggregation hides individual contributions from the aggregator, while differential privacy adds calibrated uncertainty to limit information leakage under stated assumptions. Neither automatically prevents every attack or guarantees representative data. Devices with power, connectivity, and eligibility to participate may systematically differ from those excluded. Validate updates on independent consented data, monitor regressions by supported device/language groups where feasible, and maintain a rollback path. If the available signal cannot establish factual quality, acknowledge that measurement limit and keep human evaluation in the process.
