## Question

Design the output representation for a behaviour prediction model. What metrics would you gate it on?

## Brief answer

Represent plausible futures as multiple time-indexed trajectories or occupancy distributions with calibrated probabilities and explicit coordinate/time conventions. Include uncertainty and interactions rather than one averaged path that may pass through an impossible region. Gate on downstream planning usefulness as well as displacement error.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** For each tracked actor, predict K modes with positions, optionally velocity/heading, probability, and validity horizon. Alternatively use occupancy over space and time when identity-specific modes are insufficient. Preserve map and ego-frame alignment and account for occlusion and track uncertainty.

Measure minADE/minFDE or miss rate at fixed K, probability calibration, likelihood under a stated distribution, collision/road-constraint consistency, and performance on rare maneuvers. Test interactive scenarios where another actor's behavior depends on the ego plan. Closed-loop planning should verify whether uncertainty leads to safe, useful decisions. A predictor can lower best-of-K error by adding implausible modes, so evaluate probability mass and mode diversity rather than only the closest candidate.

### Preserve uncertainty the planner can act on

Consider a vehicle approaching an intersection that may turn left or continue straight. A single mean trajectory can run between both lanes and describe a physically implausible future. Multiple modes retain the alternatives, while probabilities tell the planner how much weight to give each. Occupancy distributions offer another representation when uncertain location matters more than maintaining a small set of named trajectories.

Specify the coordinate frame, timestamps, horizon, actor identity, and treatment of missing observations. Misaligned timestamps or ego-motion compensation can make a numerically accurate-looking trajectory operationally wrong. Include uncertainty from tracking and occlusion instead of presenting every prediction with the same confidence.

Best-of-K displacement measures whether at least one candidate is close to the observed future. It does not penalize assigning that candidate negligible probability or filling the remaining modes with implausible paths. Pair it with calibration, miss rate, physical consistency, and downstream planning tests at a fixed K.

Interactive predictions require special care because other actors may respond to the ego vehicle's intended action. Test whether the planner behaves sensibly when plausible futures disagree, rather than rewarding a predictor for an oracle-like mode the planner cannot identify in advance. Gate on useful uncertainty and safe planning behavior, not just the distance of the luckiest hypothesis.
