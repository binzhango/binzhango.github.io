## Question

Explain Constitutional AI. What does it buy you over vanilla RLHF, and what doesn't it solve?

## Brief answer

Constitutional AI uses written principles to guide model critique, revision and preference feedback, reducing reliance on humans labeling every harmfulness example. It makes parts of the desired behavior explicit and scalable. It does not prove alignment, remove human value choices or ensure that model-generated critiques correctly identify every harmful response.

## Detailed answer

The original work combines a supervised stage, in which the model critiques and revises responses using principles, with a reinforcement-learning stage using AI-generated preference labels. Humans still choose the principles, training distribution and evaluation criteria.

Compared with ordinary human-feedback pipelines, the advantage is cheaper and more systematic feedback about specified norms. The risk is correlated error: the critic may share the generator's blind spots, misread a principle or prefer polished refusals over genuinely helpful safe answers. Principles can conflict and may omit entire classes of failure.

Evaluate useful assistance, over-refusal and harmful compliance separately, including adversarial and multilingual cases. Keep independent human review for consequential decisions and emerging failure modes. A constitution is a training specification and governance artifact, not a runtime security boundary; tools still require deterministic authorization and data-access controls.

An illustrative principle might ask the model to acknowledge uncertainty instead of inventing facts. A critique can identify a confident unsupported assertion and produce a revised answer that explains what is known and what needs verification. That creates a reusable training signal without a human rewriting every instance.

The limitation is that the critic must recognize the unsupported assertion. If generator and critic share a misconception, both may endorse it. A principle such as “be helpful” can also conflict with privacy or harm constraints, requiring choices about priority and interpretation. Those choices remain human governance decisions even when feedback generation is automated.

Compare the constitutional pipeline against a human-feedback baseline using independent reviewers and task outcomes, not only the constitutional judge's own scores. Include cases where safe assistance is possible so over-refusal does not masquerade as improvement. Runtime access controls, tool authorization and evidence validation remain necessary because a training principle does not enforce a permission boundary at the moment an external action executes.
