## Question

Explain how you would design and evaluate an A/B test. What is a p-value and how do you interpret it here?

## Brief answer

Randomize the correct unit, predefine a primary metric and guardrails, and size the experiment for a meaningful detectable effect. A p-value is the probability, under the null model and its assumptions, of results at least as extreme as observed. It is not the probability that the null is true or the probability the product change is useful.

## Detailed answer

Choose user-, account-, or cluster-level randomization according to spillovers. Validate exposure logging and run sample-ratio mismatch checks before interpreting results. Set duration to cover important cycles, define exclusions in advance, and estimate effects with confidence intervals. Repeated unplanned peeking inflates false positives; use a valid sequential design if early stopping is required.

Inspect guardrails such as latency, errors, retention, and subgroup harm. Distinguish statistical significance from practical value and account for multiple comparisons. A null result can mean no effect, insufficient power, implementation failure, or dilution from non-exposure. For network or marketplace features, user-level independence may fail; cluster randomization or switchback designs can be more appropriate.

### Interpret the statistical evidence

A p-value is the probability, under the specified null hypothesis and assumptions, of obtaining a test statistic at least as incompatible with the null as the observed one. It is not the probability that the null is true, nor the probability the result will replicate. Report an effect estimate and confidence interval to show magnitude and uncertainty.

For an illustrative binary metric rising from 10.0% to 10.5%, the absolute effect is 0.5 percentage points and the relative change is 5%. Under independent user outcomes, 80% power, and a two-sided 5% significance threshold, a rough normal approximation needs about 58,000 users per arm. Clustering, repeated observations, or multiple testing can increase that requirement. Decide the minimum useful effect before launch. Confirm assignment and exposure logs, then examine guardrails and practical benefit before shipping. A significant tiny change may not justify operational cost, while a wide interval around zero is inconclusive rather than proof of no effect.
