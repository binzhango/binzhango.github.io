## Question

What is the lost-in-the-middle problem in long contexts and how do you address it?

## Brief answer

Lost-in-the-middle describes reduced use of relevant information placed in the middle of a long prompt. Address it through retrieval, context compression and placement experiments rather than simply increasing the context window.

## Detailed answer

Construct a controlled test that moves the same supporting fact among the beginning, middle and end while keeping distractors comparable. Measure answer accuracy and citation support by position. In production, retrieve fewer high-quality passages, preserve local context, and place decisive evidence near the question when evaluations support it. Hierarchical summaries help only if they preserve the needed details. Longer windows increase cost and can introduce more distracting evidence.

The important distinction is between fitting text into the context window and successfully using it. A model may accept 100,000 tokens while attending effectively to only selected portions. Distractors, repeated wording and conflicts between sources can make the problem worse; position is only one variable.

A useful diagnostic builds a document containing one answer-bearing passage and many irrelevant but similarly styled passages. Repeat the query with that passage at 10%, 50% and 90% of the context, preserving its wording and changing only order. Compare with a short prompt containing the evidence alone. If the short prompt works and the middle placement fails, retrieval or context assembly is a better first target than factual fine-tuning.

One intervention is to retrieve at the section level, rerank compact passages and place the most useful evidence near the question. Another is a two-stage process that extracts relevant facts before answering, while retaining source spans so compression can be checked. Both can lose information: aggressive summarization may omit a negation or date, and duplicated evidence wastes context. Validate on multi-document answers and adversarially similar distractors, and measure citation completeness as well as the final answer string.
