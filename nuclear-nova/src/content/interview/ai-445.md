## Question

How would you benchmark an LLM agent's tool use, say, for enterprise workflows composing 10+ APIs?

## Brief answer

Benchmark completed business outcomes and policy compliance in a stateful sandbox, not merely valid tool-call syntax. Include dependencies across more than ten APIs, permission boundaries, partial failures, and recovery. Grade final state, unauthorized side effects, efficiency, and the correctness of the agent's report separately.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Build seeded workflows such as finding an eligible customer, checking a contract, updating a ticket, and drafting a follow-up. Define allowed identities, budgets, irreversible actions, and required confirmations before execution. Simulate rate limits, pagination, stale reads, schema errors, and timeouts after a write commits. Maintain a backend event ledger so evaluators can distinguish a successful action from a fabricated acknowledgment.

Use hidden task variations and multiple random seeds. Report end-to-end success by workflow length, recovery success, permission violations, duplicate writes, latency, and cost. Compare with a deterministic workflow baseline and ablate memory or planning features. Human reviewers assess ambiguous business outcomes, while deterministic invariants catch dangerous failures. Keep production credentials out of the harness; passing a sandbox is a prerequisite for a scoped pilot, not unrestricted authorization.

### Make a ten-step workflow falsifiable

A useful test might require identifying a customer, reading their entitlement, checking outstanding invoices, opening a service case, reserving inventory, and arranging a permitted replacement. Seed a backend with exact initial state and describe the allowed final state. The grader then checks the replacement quantity, customer identity, case linkage, and absence of unauthorized refunds. The agent's final explanation is graded separately from those durable facts.

Inject a timeout after the reservation has committed. A capable agent should inspect the operation status or retry using the same idempotency key; creating a second reservation is a failure even if the user receives a confident success message. Include a read-only identity, an expired entitlement, and an unavailable dependency to exercise legitimate refusal and recovery.

Even if each required step independently succeeds 98% of the time, a ten-step chain succeeds only about 81.7% of the time under that simplified assumption. Real failures are correlated, but the calculation explains why tool-call accuracy is an inadequate headline metric. Report successful final outcomes and failure severity at the workflow level.
