## Question

PHI is in every audio file, transcript and note you touch. How does that shape the architecture, and what can you send to a third-party model API?

## Brief answer

Treat audio, transcripts, notes, derived embeddings, logs, and backups as sensitive clinical data with explicit purpose, access, retention, and deletion controls. A third-party model call is allowed only through the organization's reviewed contractual and security arrangement for that exact service and data use. Encryption alone does not remove those obligations.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Minimize data sent, isolate tenants and encounters, use scoped credentials and audited access, and keep PHI out of ordinary debugging telemetry. Verify service configuration for retention, training use, subprocessors, region, and incident handling with the responsible privacy/security teams. Do not assume an enterprise product name covers every API endpoint or optional feature.

For US HIPAA-regulated workflows, HHS cloud guidance explains that a provider handling ePHI may be a business associate even without the encryption key, and a BAA alone does not replace risk analysis and other obligations. Map data flows and test access revocation, deletion, backups, and vendor outages. This is architecture planning; the organization's qualified owners determine the applicable legal requirements.

### Minimize where sensitive data can travel

Map the data path from microphone to transcription, model processing, draft storage, EHR delivery, logs, support tools, and backups. Each copy needs an identified purpose, access policy, retention rule, and approved processor. Removing patient names alone does not establish that the remaining conversation is safe to send anywhere; clinical narratives can contain identifying details in many forms.

Separate clinical payloads from operational telemetry. A latency dashboard usually needs request IDs, durations, and failure codes, not full transcripts. Use restricted, auditable access for the small set of workflows that require content inspection. Verify that error reporting, tracing, and support exports do not accidentally bypass the primary storage controls.

For a US HIPAA-covered deployment, the responsible organization must assess applicable business-associate arrangements and safeguards for services handling ePHI. A signed agreement alone does not establish that the configured architecture is appropriate, and encryption does not automatically remove the provider's obligations. Keep service eligibility, configuration, access controls, and risk assessment aligned with the actual deployment.

Exercise deletion and recovery workflows, including retained backups and derived artifacts, and document what each operation can guarantee. Test cross-tenant authorization, misrouted encounter identifiers, support access, and accidental payload logging. Review the system with the organization's privacy and security owners using the real data flow rather than a generic claim that a cloud vendor is compliant.
