## Question

Budget the compute and latency for the onboard stack. What breaks when a model gets bigger?

## Brief answer

Budget the complete sensor-to-actuator path, including acquisition, synchronization, preprocessing, model execution, planning, control, communication, and scheduling jitter. Bigger models can consume memory bandwidth, power, and thermal headroom even when average inference fits. Missing a deadline can make otherwise accurate predictions stale.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Define separate deadlines for fast control and slower semantic tasks, then profile p95/p99 and worst observed latency under concurrent load. An illustrative 10 Hz planning loop has 100 ms between updates, but sensing and downstream control consume part of that interval; assigning all 100 ms to inference is invalid. Reserve headroom for fault handling and degraded operation.

Measure accelerator contention, cache/memory pressure, thermal throttling, and sensor backlog on deployed hardware. Use smaller models, mixed precision, scheduling isolation, or asynchronous hierarchy only after quality validation. Test camera bursts, logging load, sensor failures, and high ambient temperature. A throughput benchmark on an idle workstation does not establish a dependable onboard deadline.

### Budget data age as well as execution time

For an illustrative 100 ms planning interval, allocate 15 ms to acquisition and alignment, 10 ms to preprocessing, 35 ms to inference, 20 ms to planning, and 5 ms to command delivery, leaving 15 ms for scheduling variation and handling. These allocations are hypothetical and must be measured on the deployed system. A faster isolated inference benchmark does not guarantee the complete path fits.

Track the age of the sensor observation when its resulting command is applied. Queued frames can make data stale even when each inference finishes quickly. Bound queues, discard obsolete work where appropriate, and retain the latest consistent observation set rather than processing every frame indiscriminately.

Bigger models can increase memory traffic and interfere with other accelerator tasks. They may also change power consumption enough to trigger thermal throttling after sustained operation, so short cold-start benchmarks are misleading. Test concurrent perception, planning, logging, and fault-handling loads across realistic temperatures.

Separate tasks by deadline and consequence. A semantic update can often be skipped or delayed; a stabilization loop cannot simply wait behind it. Use scheduling isolation, bounded interfaces, and validated degraded modes. Optimize model size or precision only while checking behavioral quality, and reject configurations whose tail timing leaves the controller acting on information older than its operating assumptions permit.
