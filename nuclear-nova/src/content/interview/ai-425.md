## Question

Design a serverless inference platform serving 100+ open models on a shared GPU fleet.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: use a model registry and control plane, a gateway with tenant quotas, and a scheduler that packs compatible models onto a shared GPU fleet while managing warm capacity and cold starts. Model diversity creates weight-loading and memory-fragmentation costs; serverless does not mean capacity appears instantly or every model has identical SLOs.

## Detailed answer

Group models by runtime compatibility, size, precision, and demand. Keep popular models warm, cache artifacts near workers, and load less-used models on demand with explicit startup expectations. Admit requests using weight plus KV-memory budgets and isolate untrusted model code. Consider adapter sharing only when base-model compatibility is verified.

Use fair queues, cancellation, timeouts, and overload responses rather than unlimited waiting. Track cold-start latency, artifact cache hit rate, utilization, evictions, tenant fairness, and cost per successful request. Canary runtime/model updates and retain a fallback capacity pool. Prevent eviction thrashing by considering recent demand and reload cost. Offer dedicated endpoints for customers whose latency or isolation needs cannot be met economically by shared placement.

### Model placement is a cache problem with expensive misses

Popular models deserve warm replicas; rare models compete for accelerator memory with reload costs much larger than ordinary data-cache misses. Placement should consider artifact size, load time, recent demand, runtime compatibility, and expected KV usage. A naive least-recently-used policy can thrash between large models during bursts, spending most time loading weights.

The gateway authenticates and queues requests by model and SLO. A controller chooses a warm worker or provisions a compatible group, while admission control limits queued memory and wait time. Artifacts are verified and cached close to workers; untrusted custom code requires isolation. Shared-base adapters can reduce footprint only when the models truly share a compatible base. Expose cold-start expectations honestly and offer dedicated capacity for stringent latency. Test simultaneous cold requests, canceled loads, node loss, and model-update rollback. Track useful completed requests per provisioned dollar, not GPU utilization inflated by repeated loading and abandoned work.
