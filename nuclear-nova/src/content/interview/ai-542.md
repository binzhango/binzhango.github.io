## Question

A hospital group manually schedules and confirms outpatient appointments by phone, with three staff on a rota. Design a system to support this workflow.

## Brief answer

Automate a narrow scheduling and confirmation workflow around the hospital's authoritative appointment system, with reliable human transfer and privacy controls. Keep the agent out of clinical decision-making. Begin by measuring call volume, appointment types, identity checks, and the staff's actual exception handling before replacing any work.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** The voice layer gathers identity and scheduling preferences, while a deterministic service checks eligible slots, visit type, location, and booking rules. Hold a slot briefly, read back the proposed appointment, obtain confirmation, and commit with an idempotency key. Handle voicemail with minimal approved information and reconcile unknown booking outcomes after disconnects.

Pilot one clinic and appointment class with staff supervision. Escalate symptoms, uncertainty about instructions, language/accessibility barriers, and policy exceptions through the hospital's approved process. Measure completed bookings, wrong-patient events, duplicate appointments, no-shows, transfer success, and staff time. Test calendar races, EHR downtime, and a caller changing their mind mid-confirmation. The deployment's privacy and operational requirements must be reviewed by the hospital's responsible teams.

### Model booking as a stateful transaction

Use explicit states such as identify caller, establish scheduling intent, find eligible slots, propose one slot, confirm, commit, and report outcome. The model manages the conversation; a booking service validates the state transition and appointment constraints. A spoken “yes” must refer to the exact current proposal, not a previous slot that the caller already rejected.

Suppose two callers select the same appointment. A hold or conditional booking operation prevents both from receiving a successful confirmation. If the EHR times out after submission, the agent says the outcome is being checked and queries by the operation identifier; it must not blindly submit a second booking. Give a human operator the transaction state and call summary when resolution is uncertain.

Measure the existing three-person rota before choosing staffing changes. Calls may be concentrated at opening time, and a small number of complex cases may consume most staff effort. Capacity planning should include concurrent calls, transfer demand, after-call work, and staff availability for exceptions, not only total weekly minutes.

Start with straightforward confirmations or a clearly defined visit type. Review failed calls jointly with schedulers, then expand only when duplicate prevention, wrong-patient safeguards, accessibility, and handoff reliability meet agreed requirements. Successful automation leaves the caller with a correct, verifiable appointment and a usable path to help.
