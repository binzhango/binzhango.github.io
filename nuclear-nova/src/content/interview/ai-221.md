## Question

You need to serve a Llama-class 70B+ model to hundreds of millions of assistant users. What does the serving stack look like and where does the money go?

## Brief answer

Use regional admission and streaming gateways in front of sharded model replicas, with continuous batching, bounded KV allocation and cache-aware routing. Capacity and cost come from peak token demand and the quality/latency target, rather than the total number of registered users.

## Detailed answer

For a 70B dense model, BF16 weights alone occupy about 140 GB in decimal units; a device with sufficient capacity, multi-device partitioning or evaluated compression is needed, with additional room for cache and workspace. Forecast prompt and output lengths, simultaneous decoding sequences, and cache-hit rates by region. Measure cost per successful conversation including search/tools, retries, safety checks and spare capacity. Prefer smaller evaluated models for suitable tasks, reserve premium capacity for difficult ones, and cancel disconnected streams promptly. Multi-tier routing needs calibrated quality gates: a cheap first attempt that triggers many retries may cost more. Load-test regional failure and overload; shed work deliberately rather than letting queues grow without bounds.

### Follow a request through the fleet

An authenticated regional gateway checks quota, request size, and deadline, then routes to an eligible model pool. A scheduler admits work only when weight, KV, and workspace capacity are available. It chunks expensive prefills, continuously batches active decodes, streams output through the gateway, and propagates cancellation. Cache-aware placement reuses exact prefixes where authorization and model configuration permit.

Forecast demand in tokens per second, separating prompt processing from generated output. For example, ten million daily conversations with two thousand input and two hundred output tokens imply 20 billion input and two billion output tokens per day before retries. The average is not the peak; regional traffic, bursts, failures, and latency headroom determine provisioned capacity. Money goes to accelerator hours, unused reserved capacity, memory-heavy long contexts, retrieval/tools, network, and operations. Measure cost per correctly completed task, including escalations. A smaller model can be economical for simple intent classification while the main model handles synthesis; validate the routing mistakes and fallback cost before claiming savings.
