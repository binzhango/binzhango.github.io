## Question

Compare SFT, RLHF, DPO and RLVR for improving an instruction-tuned model. What data does each need, and when would you pick which?

## Brief answer

SFT learns from desired demonstrations; conventional RLHF learns a reward model from human comparisons and then optimizes sampled outputs; DPO directly trains on preferred versus rejected outputs; RLVR optimizes against verifiable outcome rewards. Choose based on available supervision, task verifiability, exploration needs, and operational complexity, not an assumed universal ranking.

## Detailed answer

SFT needs prompt–target pairs and is a good first step for format, tool schemas, and task coverage. Preference-based RLHF usually needs comparison labels, a reward model, and policy rollouts; it supports exploration but exposes reward exploitation and adds training infrastructure. DPO uses paired preferences and a reference policy without a separate reward-model training stage in its standard formulation.

RLVR needs tasks with reliable automatic checks, such as executable tests or exact mathematical verification. It is less suitable where the checker misses real-world quality. A practical sequence is SFT for basic competence, then DPO for stable offline preference data or online RL when exploration adds value. Reserve held-out tasks, monitor capability regressions and length bias, and manually inspect high-reward failures. Verifiable rewards can still be hacked if tests are incomplete or leaked.

### What the losses actually encourage

For SFT, the loss increases when the model assigns low probability to the demonstrated answer. It teaches a particular trajectory, even if several other answers would also be acceptable. A tool-format task with reliable demonstrations is therefore a strong SFT candidate. It needs no explicit rejected answer.

Standard DPO compares the policy's log-probability ratio for a preferred and rejected answer with the same ratio under a reference model. Increasing the relative probability of the preferred answer reduces the pairwise loss. This is more targeted than imitating every preferred answer independently, but an offline preference dataset cannot expose every failure the updated policy will discover.

Online RL samples from the current policy, scores the outcomes, and updates toward higher reward while controlling drift. RLVR describes where that reward comes from; it can be optimized with several policy-optimization algorithms. A coding model might use tests as outcome rewards, yet a test suite that checks only the final printed number can reward hard-coded shortcuts. For a customer-support model whose quality includes empathy and policy nuance, preference labels may capture more than a brittle automatic checker. Choose supervision by the error being corrected, then verify gains on tasks and prompts excluded from training.
