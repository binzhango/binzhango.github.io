## Question

How would you fuse camera, radar and IMU inputs into a single perception stack, and where would you fuse them?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: synchronize and calibrate sensors, use IMU-derived ego motion for alignment, and fuse camera and radar information in a shared spatial representation with uncertainty. Cameras provide rich semantics, radar contributes range and velocity cues, and IMU supports motion estimation. Fusion location depends on latency, compute, sensor quality, and the need to degrade gracefully.

## Detailed answer

Early fusion combines low-level measurements but requires precise alignment and can be expensive. Feature-level fusion in BEV often balances cross-modal interaction and manageable representation size. Late fusion combines independent detections and is modular, but may lose information that could resolve ambiguous individual observations.

Model timestamp offsets, radar ghosts, rolling shutter, calibration drift, and missing sensors. Train with modality dropout and explicit masks so absence is not mistaken for zero-valued evidence. Propagate uncertainty and maintain consistency across tracks. Evaluate adverse weather, occlusion, and failure cases rather than only clean-data accuracy. Select the architecture using system-level latency and safety requirements; no fusion stage is universally best for every vehicle or sensor package.

### Align before combining

A camera provides rich appearance but uncertain depth; radar supplies range and radial velocity with different ambiguities; an IMU estimates motion at high frequency. Transform measurements into a common coordinate frame and align timestamps before fusion. A small time offset can make a moving object appear in conflicting locations even when each sensor is individually accurate.

A feature-level BEV design encodes each modality, projects it into a shared vehicle-centered representation, includes validity and uncertainty masks, and combines features before tracking or detection heads. The IMU helps compensate ego motion across asynchronous measurements. Late fusion is simpler to modularize and can preserve independent fallback paths, but may lose cross-modal clues. Test radar multipath, camera glare, IMU bias, and missing sensors individually and together. Train with realistic sensor dropout rather than replacing missing inputs with unexplained zeros. The output should communicate uncertainty to downstream consumers instead of averaging disagreeing sensors into an unjustifiably precise estimate.
