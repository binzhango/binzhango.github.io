## Question

What is distillation, and how do you build a strong small model from a large one?

## Brief answer

Distillation trains a smaller student on information from a stronger teacher, such as outputs, logits or intermediate targets. It transfers selected behavior efficiently only when the training distribution and quality controls match deployment needs.

## Detailed answer

Collect representative prompts, generate teacher candidates, filter using verifiable checks or calibrated review, and train the student on the retained targets. Include difficult failures and negative cases, not only easy successful outputs. Compare with training on human data alone and evaluate on prompts absent from generation. Teacher mistakes and stylistic biases can propagate; more synthetic examples can amplify them. Measure student latency and quality at the actual serving precision.

There are two common signals. Sequence distillation trains the student on teacher-generated answers using ordinary supervised loss. Logit distillation uses the teacher's probability distribution, often softened by a temperature, so the student learns which alternatives are plausible rather than only one selected token. The latter requires access to compatible logits or an explicit vocabulary mapping.

A practical pipeline samples deployment-like prompts, generates multiple teacher candidates where useful, checks them, and retains targets with adequate correctness and diversity. Include abstentions, edge cases and recovery behavior. If the teacher is asked only easy questions, the student learns a narrow imitation; if unverified synthetic mistakes are repeated, the errors become a large training signal.

For a domain-specific assistant, mix verified human examples with teacher answers and reserve an untouched evaluation set from different source documents or time periods. Compare the student with the untuned small model and with an equally sized model trained without teacher data. Measure task quality, calibration, latency and cost at the intended precision. Distillation can make a smaller model very effective within a selected distribution, but it does not imply that the student inherits all of the teacher's capabilities or that generated explanations are automatically trustworthy training targets.
