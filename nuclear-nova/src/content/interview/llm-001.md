## Question

How would you describe the concept of machine learning in your own words?

## Brief answer

Machine learning fits models to data so they can make predictions, discover structure, or choose actions on new inputs.

## Detailed answer

A developer specifies a model, objective, and training procedure; an optimizer learns parameters from examples. For instance, a spam classifier learns correlations between message features and labels rather than relying entirely on handwritten rules. Learning does not guarantee improvement on future data: success requires an appropriate objective, representative data, and evaluation on held-out examples.

Training and inference are separate activities. During training, a spam model computes a score from message features, compares it with the known label, and changes its weights to reduce a loss. At inference, the trained weights stay fixed while the model scores a newly arrived message. Updating the model requires another learning step; merely using it does not automatically teach it.

A useful formulation is $\hat y=f_\theta(x)$: $x$ is the available input, $\theta$ the learned parameters, and $\hat y$ the prediction. The designer chooses what information enters $x$ and what mistakes the loss penalizes. A classifier trained on old spam may learn yesterday's vocabulary and fail on a new campaign. Evaluate chronologically held-out messages and choose a decision threshold reflecting the cost of blocking legitimate mail. The central achievement is generalization to new examples, rather than remembering the training set or discovering a causal explanation of every correlation.
