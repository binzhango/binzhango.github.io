## Question

How would you balance performance optimisation with model interpretability?

## Brief answer

Choose the required level of explanation from the decision's consequences and users' needs, then optimize performance within that constraint. Interpretability may mean a transparent model, calibrated uncertainty, faithful local explanations or operational traceability; these are different properties. A fluent explanation generated after a prediction is not necessarily evidence of how the model made it.

## Detailed answer

Suggested approach: establish a simple interpretable baseline, compare it with a stronger model on the same held-out data, and quantify the practical benefit. If the gain is small and decisions need auditability, the simpler model may be preferable. If the gain is material, add feature-quality checks, counterfactual tests and independent validation of explanations.

For an LLM workflow, retrieved citations and tool traces explain evidence and actions but do not expose all internal reasoning. Measure whether explanations change when relevant inputs change and whether they help users detect errors. Avoid presenting attention weights alone as a complete causal explanation.

Use selective automation when confidence or explanation quality is weak: route uncertain cases to review. Monitor performance and explanation failures by subgroup and over time. State the residual limitation openly; transparency controls reduce particular risks rather than proving that every prediction is correct.

For a consequential tabular prediction, a sparse linear model or constrained tree may provide explanations tied directly to its computation. Compare that baseline with a more complex model on the actual decision metric, calibration and important slices. If the improvement is small, added complexity may not justify harder auditing and monitoring. If it is substantial, use validated local explanations and selective review while being clear about their limits.

For a retrieval assistant, source spans and tool receipts provide operational traceability, which is valuable even though they do not reveal the model's internal mechanism. Test whether cited evidence actually supports the output and whether explanations change appropriately under controlled input changes. A generated rationale that remains persuasive when the evidence is removed is a warning sign.

Make the tradeoff measurable: what decisions can be automated, what evidence must accompany them, and what conditions require review? Interpretability is useful when it helps detect or prevent errors, not merely when a dashboard produces attractive feature-importance plots.
