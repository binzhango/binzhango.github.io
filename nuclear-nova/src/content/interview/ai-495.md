## Question

Design the human-handoff path for a customer-service agent. When should it escalate, and what does a good handoff look like?

## Brief answer

Escalate when authority, confidence, safety, or customer preference requires a person, and transfer the work state rather than forcing the customer to repeat the story. A good handoff includes verified identity, intent, relevant evidence, attempted actions, current transaction status, and unresolved decisions. The agent should stop taking conflicting actions once ownership transfers.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Define deterministic triggers for restricted actions and critical failures, plus calibrated signals for repeated misunderstanding or unsupported answers. Route by skill, language, and urgency. Send the human a concise summary with links to source messages and receipts, distinguishing confirmed facts from hypotheses. Tell the customer what happens next and provide an honest wait estimate when available.

Use an ownership token or conversation state to prevent simultaneous human and agent writes. If no person is available, offer a safe asynchronous case rather than pretending escalation succeeded. Measure successful transfer, repeated-information burden, time to resolution, and escalation appropriateness. Test queue outages, a customer changing intent while waiting, and a backend operation that finishes after handoff.

### Transfer authority with the case

A handoff record should state the customer's goal, verified identity, relevant policy, attempted operations, confirmed receipts, unknown outcomes, and the exact decision awaiting the human. Keep transcript links available, but do not force the receiving agent to reconstruct the case from a long conversation. Mark model hypotheses separately from backend-confirmed facts.

When the human accepts ownership, revoke the automated agent's permission to issue conflicting writes. An in-flight operation may still finish, so attach its completion event to the shared case and notify the owner. Without this ownership transition, both agents can independently refund or rebook while trying to help.

For example, a failed refund acknowledgment should transfer with its operation ID and “status unknown,” not a summary saying “refund failed.” The human can reconcile before acting again. If the queue is unavailable, create a durable pending case and explain the expected next step; showing a “transferred” banner without a receiving owner is another form of false confirmation. Evaluate whether the handoff actually reduces repeated questioning and time to verified resolution.
