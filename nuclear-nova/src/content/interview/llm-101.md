## Question

How to ensure multi-task training balance?

## Brief answer

Balance multi-task training through both the probability of sampling each task and the weight of its loss.

## Detailed answer

A large dataset can dominate updates even with equal nominal loss weights. Token-averaged training can also emphasize tasks with longer answers. Adjust sampling ratios, normalization, and weights against per-task validation metrics, and inspect gradient conflicts when one task improves at another's expense. Use a representative mixture or explicit curriculum, then verify that rare but important tasks retain enough training exposure.

The expected influence of task $i$ depends on how often it is sampled and how its loss is scaled. If task A supplies 90% of batches, equal numerical weights do not give task B equal exposure. If batches mix tasks and average over tokens, a long-answer task can contribute many more targets per example. Make those weighting choices explicit before introducing adaptive algorithms.

For example, combine code generation with short classification labels. Sampling equal numbers of examples may still heavily favor code tokens. Options include task-balanced batches, per-task loss normalization, or explicit coefficients chosen on validation tradeoffs. Oversampling a tiny task can help exposure but can also encourage memorization of its few examples.

Monitor each task separately and inspect shared-parameter gradients when improvements conflict. Some tasks benefit from separate heads or adapters, while closely related tasks may improve through shared representations. A curriculum can change the mixture over time, but its final distribution still determines what behavior is reinforced. Define minimum acceptable performance for critical tasks so a better average cannot hide a collapse in one required capability.
