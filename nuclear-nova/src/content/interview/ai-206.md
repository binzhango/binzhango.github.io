## Question

Justify using a neural network for a given problem: what do you need to know about the network, dataset, timeline and business context?

## Brief answer

Justify a neural network by showing that the data and task require useful representation learning and that simpler baselines leave meaningful value on the table. Consider label volume, signal structure, latency, interpretability, maintenance and delivery time. A neural model is appropriate when its measured benefit exceeds its additional operational and development cost.

## Detailed answer

Start with the target decision and a baseline such as rules, linear models or boosted trees. Examine dataset size, label noise, leakage, imbalance and whether pretrained representations can reduce data needs. Images, audio and complex text often benefit from learned representations; small tabular datasets may not need a deep network.

Estimate training and serving resources, data iteration time and the team's ability to monitor and update the system. A tight deadline may favor a pretrained model or a simpler method with clear behavior. Explain the architecture in terms of the input structure rather than choosing a fashionable model family.

Run a bounded experiment with a held-out evaluation and business-relevant metric. Include inference cost and error analysis. If the gain is small or unstable, choose the simpler system and preserve the experiment as evidence rather than treating neural complexity as a goal.

A bounded proof-of-value experiment should compare the neural candidate with a credible simple baseline using the same data splits and business objective. For text or images, pretrained representations can make a small labeled dataset sufficient for adaptation; for a modest tabular dataset, boosted trees or linear models may already meet requirements with less operational complexity.

Estimate the full iteration loop: collecting labels, cleaning data, training, evaluating, deploying and monitoring. A model that takes weeks to retrain may be a poor fit for rapidly changing labels under a short delivery timeline. Serving constraints include latency, memory, hardware availability and whether explanations are needed for consequential decisions.

Define an adoption threshold before experimentation, such as a meaningful improvement in error cost within the latency budget. Inspect which cases improve and whether the gain is stable across time and important groups. If the threshold is not met, keep the simpler solution. Choosing a neural network is justified by demonstrated value and maintainable operation, not by the availability of GPUs or a desire to use a fashionable architecture.
