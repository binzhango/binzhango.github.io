## Question

Why do we need Graph RAG?

## Brief answer

Graph RAG is useful when answering requires connected entities, multi-hop evidence, or corpus-level themes that independent chunk retrieval may miss.

## Detailed answer

A dependency-impact question naturally involves paths, while a broad question such as “What recurring problems appear across these reports?” may benefit from community summaries. Simple fact lookup may work better with cheaper lexical or vector retrieval. Graph extraction, resolution, and maintenance add cost, so compare against a strong baseline on questions that actually require relationships or global synthesis.

There are two common needs. Local relational questions require connecting specific entities, such as a service, its dependency, and the dependency's owner. Global questions require summarizing patterns across many records, such as recurring incident causes. A graph can support traversal for the first and organized summaries of related entities or documents for the second.

A strong baseline is still necessary. A question asking for one exact configuration value may be answered more cheaply by lexical search or a structured database. Graph construction introduces entity extraction, alias resolution, relation validation, and ongoing updates. Incorrect edges can create apparently convincing paths between facts that are not actually connected.

Evaluate on the question types that motivate the graph. For multi-hop questions, require source support for every edge in the answer. For corpus-level synthesis, check coverage and whether summaries overrepresent prominent or densely connected records. Keep original passages available because graph edges and summaries lose nuance. Adopt Graph RAG when that explicit structure improves supported answers enough to justify its indexing and maintenance cost, rather than treating it as the default successor to ordinary retrieval.
