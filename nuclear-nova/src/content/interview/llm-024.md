## Question

What is the training objective of large language models?

## Brief answer

Autoregressive LLMs usually pretrain with next-token prediction, but language models can use masked-token, denoising, or mixed objectives; post-training adds other objectives.

## Detailed answer

BERT predicts selected masked tokens, while T5 reconstructs corrupted text. Decoder-only causal models maximize sequence likelihood. Supervised fine-tuning trains on desired responses, often masking prompt tokens from the loss. Preference learning and reinforcement learning optimize preference or reward objectives. Always specify the model and training stage before saying “the LLM objective.”

The objective determines which conditional distribution receives supervision. In next-token training, “the cat sat” yields targets for later tokens given earlier ones. In masked-token training, the model might reconstruct “cat” from “the [MASK] sat,” using information on both sides. Denoising sequence-to-sequence training can remove spans and train a decoder to reconstruct them. These objectives expose different information and therefore produce different learning signals.

During SFT, an instruction and context often enter the model as input, while the loss is applied only to the desired response. The mathematical token loss can remain cross-entropy even though the data distribution and masking have changed. Preference training instead compares responses or optimizes a reward-related objective; its supervision is not simply another unlabeled document.

A low loss is meaningful only relative to that objective. Predicting common tokens accurately does not establish reliable tool execution, factual answers, or obedience to complex constraints. Evaluate those capabilities explicitly and audit the examples supplying supervision. Architecture names and training objectives are related historically but should not be treated as interchangeable definitions.
