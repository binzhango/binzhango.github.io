## Question

Explain how a Mixtral-style sparse mixture-of-experts model works. Why does a ~47B-parameter model run at roughly the cost of a ~13B one?

## Brief answer

Mixtral 8x7B routes each token to two of eight feed-forward experts per layer. Most expert parameters are therefore inactive for a particular token, so arithmetic is closer to the active parameter count while storage still reflects the full model.

## Detailed answer

The Mixtral paper reports roughly 47B total and 13B active parameters. Shared attention and other components explain why the total is not simply eight copies of a complete 7B model. The active count is an arithmetic guide, not an end-to-end cost guarantee: experts occupy memory, routing creates irregular work, and cross-device dispatch can dominate latency. A batch may exercise most experts even when each individual token uses only two. Compare measured throughput and latency at matched quality, context and concurrency rather than treating this as a dense 13B model with free extra capacity.

### Trace conditional computation

After attention, the router scores eight feed-forward experts and selects two for each token. Those experts process the token's hidden vector, and their outputs are combined with learned gate weights. Other tokens can select different pairs. Attention and other shared layers still execute for every token, so the model is not eight complete independent 7B networks.

At two bytes per weight, 47B total parameters require roughly 94 GB of raw storage even though the active arithmetic resembles a much smaller model. Training also needs gradients and optimizer state for the larger parameter set. At serving time, group token assignments by expert to form efficient matrix operations, then restore token order and combine outputs. Uneven routes create hotspots; cross-device dispatch adds latency. Low-concurrency traffic may produce very small expert batches, reducing the theoretical advantage. Compare quality-equivalent workloads and include full replica memory, interconnects, cache, and utilization when pricing. “Roughly the cost of 13B” is an arithmetic intuition, not a guarantee about hardware count or dollars.
