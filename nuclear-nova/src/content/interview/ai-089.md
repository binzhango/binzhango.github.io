## Question

What changes when you move from images to video?

## Brief answer

Video adds time, motion, long sequences and synchronization with audio or text. Sampling a few frames can support scene understanding but misses events between frames and can destroy temporal ordering.

## Detailed answer

Choose frame rate and clip length from the task: action recognition needs motion, while document-like screenshots may need high spatial resolution. Preserve timestamps and align audio/transcripts, using hierarchical clip summaries or retrieval for long recordings. Evaluate event boundaries, order, causality and cross-shot identity. A model that answers image questions well may still confuse “before” and “after” or hallucinate continuity between unrelated shots.

Video introduces temporal sampling decisions before the model even sees the data. A one-frame-per-second sample may capture a meeting slide but miss a brief hand gesture or a ball crossing a line. A higher frame rate improves temporal coverage but multiplies visual tokens, often forcing a reduction in spatial resolution or clip length.

Preserve timestamps and group frames into clips with temporal position information. Motion-sensitive tasks benefit from representations that compare frames, rather than treating them as unrelated images. Align speech transcripts and audio events to the same time axis; a spoken instruction may explain an action that is visually ambiguous. For long recordings, retrieve relevant clips or use hierarchical summaries while retaining links to original segments.

Test event order explicitly: “the person opens the door before picking up the box” is different from the reverse sequence. Evaluate cross-shot identity, occlusion, repeated actions and events between sampled frames. Summaries can hallucinate continuity across an edit, so scene boundaries matter. Report the sampling rate and resolution used for each evaluation. A correct answer about a static object in a video does not establish motion understanding, and a configured long context does not guarantee coverage of every frame.
