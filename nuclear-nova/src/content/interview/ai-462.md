## Question

How would you evaluate answer quality for an answer engine, continuously and at scale, with both automated and human signals?

## Brief answer

Evaluate answer correctness, relevance, completeness, citation support, freshness, and user task success separately. Combine deterministic checks, calibrated model judges, and blinded human review. User clicks and session length are useful diagnostics but do not establish factual quality, especially when a convincing wrong answer ends the session.

## Detailed answer

Maintain a versioned query set spanning factual lookups, comparisons, ambiguous questions, recent events, and unanswerable requests. Score retrieval evidence independently from generation so the responsible stage is visible. Use human-reviewed claim labels to estimate automated-judge precision and recall; refresh calibration whenever prompts or models change.

In production, sample by traffic and by risk, including low-confidence cases and complaints. Track unsupported claims per assessable claim, complete-answer success per query, citation resolution, latency, and abandonment with clear denominators. Use randomized experiments for product changes and guard against exposure bias in feedback. Keep longitudinal panels for regressions and fresh panels for distribution shift. Red-team misleading sources, conflicting facts, and prompt injection. A dashboard should surface category failures and uncertainty, not hide them behind one aggregate quality score.

### Make the evaluation actionable

For each sampled answer, annotate its assessable factual claims, required query facets, and source spans. Claim support measures whether stated facts are justified; completeness measures whether important requested information was omitted. An answer that says only “it depends” can avoid false claims while failing the user's task, so neither measure should replace the other.

Use a two-tier sample: a representative traffic sample estimates overall quality, while an oversampled risk set finds rare consequential failures. Apply sampling weights when estimating population rates; otherwise oversampling difficult cases can make the dashboard appear to regress even when traffic quality is unchanged. Report the targeted risk results separately.

Before using an automated judge as a release gate, compare its labels with independent human judgments and inspect systematic errors, such as favoring longer prose or accepting a valid-looking citation without support. Maintain a fixed regression panel and a fresh panel for new events. A change ships only when the relevant quality dimensions improve or remain acceptable within uncertainty, alongside latency and cost guardrails.
