## Question

Design an LLM-powered customer-support assistant on top of an existing help centre, with escalation to humans.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: retrieve authorized, current help-center evidence, answer with citations, and escalate when the question is unsupported or requires human judgment. Separate informational answers from account-changing tools. The assistant's success metric is resolved customer need with accurate handling, not simply fewer transfers or longer conversations.

## Detailed answer

Index articles with product version, locale, effective dates, and audience permissions. Use hybrid retrieval and reranking, then verify whether the retrieved passages support the proposed answer. Ask a focused clarification for ambiguous product or account context. Avoid making promises about refunds, eligibility, or availability that the source does not establish.

For actions, use typed APIs with identity checks, idempotency, and confirmation where required. Escalate with a concise summary, relevant evidence, and actions already attempted so users do not repeat everything. Evaluate groundedness, resolution, unnecessary escalation, incorrect automation, and customer satisfaction. Test stale articles, contradictory policies, prompt injection, outages, and angry or multilingual customers. Keep article updates and model changes independently versioned.

### Walk through one support request

A user asks why a refund has not arrived. Resolve the product and policy version, retrieve the applicable refund timing, and distinguish a general explanation from an account-specific status check. The latter requires authenticated access to a constrained account tool; the model should not infer it from a generic article. Combine the evidence into a direct answer with the relevant next step.

If the tool reports an exception or sources conflict, transfer to a human with the user's issue, verified facts, and attempted actions. Keep “resolution” tied to a confirmed outcome or useful next step, not the assistant ending the chat. Use escalation thresholds based on unsupported evidence, repeated failed attempts, and consequential requests, then measure both missed escalations and unnecessary handoffs. Test policy updates independently of model updates so freshness problems are not mistaken for model regressions. Track user corrections and repeat contacts; an apparently low escalation rate may simply mean users abandoned a poor answer.
