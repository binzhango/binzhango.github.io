## Question

How does QLoRA achieve its memory reduction, and what are the quantization trade-offs?

## Brief answer

QLoRA stores the frozen base model in low precision and trains small LoRA adapters, reducing weight and optimizer-state memory. Computation dequantizes the needed weights; it is not ordinary full-model training in four-bit arithmetic.

## Detailed answer

The original recipe combines NF4 weight representation, double quantization and paged optimizers. Adapter gradients still backpropagate through the frozen network, so activations can remain a major memory cost. Quantization adds approximation and scale metadata; hardware support and dequantization affect speed. Validate rare behaviors and long contexts against a higher-precision baseline, and measure peak memory during optimizer steps.

Full fine-tuning pays for trainable base weights, their gradients and optimizer moments. QLoRA freezes the base, stores its weights in a compact representation, and pays training-state costs mainly for adapters. When a layer runs, needed weights are reconstructed into the compute format, allowing gradients to flow through the operation to the adapters without updating the quantized base values.

NF4 uses a nonuniform set of representable values intended to suit approximately normally distributed weights. Block scales preserve local magnitude, and double quantization compresses some scale metadata. Paged optimizers address transient optimizer-memory pressure through managed paging; they do not make activation memory disappear. Long sequences can therefore remain expensive even with a very small trainable-parameter count.

For an illustrative 7B base, raw four-bit storage is about 3.5 GB rather than 14 GB at two bytes per weight, before scales, adapters and runtime buffers. The full training peak must also include saved activations, temporary dequantization buffers and the optimizer implementation. Compare identical data and training budgets against a higher-precision adapter baseline. Check task-specific regressions and serving behavior after adapter merging or re-quantization; a training-memory saving does not guarantee equivalent latency or unchanged quality on every domain.
