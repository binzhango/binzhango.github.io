## Question

Design the IR and pass pipeline for a compiler targeting a spatial dataflow accelerator. Where does the memory-residency decision live, and why?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: use a high-level tensor IR for semantics and transformations, then lower to a spatial IR with explicit placement, buffers, transfers, and schedule dependencies. Memory residency belongs in a resource-aware planning stage that can feed back into partitioning and fusion. Deciding it only during final code emission is too late to make good global trade-offs.

## Detailed answer

The high-level IR records shapes, dtypes, layouts, and side effects. Apply constant folding, algebraic simplification, quantization, and legal fusion while preserving numerical requirements. Partition operations across processing units/chips, choose tiling, and allocate SRAM according to tensor lifetimes and reuse.

Lower communication into explicit send/receive or stream edges with capacity and timing constraints. Schedule operations and transfers jointly, then verify no use-before-ready, buffer alias conflict, or deadlock. When memory does not fit, reconsider fusion, recomputation, tiling, or placement. Keep source mappings and simulator hooks for debugging. Support dynamic cases through bounded shapes or multiple compiled variants with clear runtime selection rather than hiding them behind an unverified static assumption.

### Memory decisions feed back into scheduling

At a tensor IR level, matrix multiplication is an abstract operation with shapes and types. Tiling lowers it into pieces sized for compute units and SRAM. Placement assigns pieces to chips; liveness analysis identifies which intermediate values coexist. Buffer allocation then reveals whether a proposed fusion keeps too many tensors live, requiring smaller tiles, recomputation, or a different partition.

Represent communication explicitly in a lower IR with source/destination, layout, byte count, and dependency tokens. The scheduler assigns time/resource slots and verifies that receives complete before consumers run. Memory residency belongs in this middle-to-low-level planning loop because it changes both communication volume and legal schedules. It cannot be a last-minute allocation after instruction timing is fixed. Preserve numerical constraints through fusion and quantization, and retain mappings back to high-level operations for debugging. Test simulator parity, alias safety, shape boundaries, and schedule feasibility independently; a functionally correct graph can still be impossible to execute within the target memory and timing budget.
