## Question

You have an embedding model and a reranker. Why sell both? Design the two-stage retrieval pipeline and tell me when the reranker earns its latency.

## Brief answer

Embeddings cheaply retrieve a broad candidate set; a reranker spends more computation comparing the query and candidate together. The two stages are complementary when retrieval has good recall but imperfect ordering and reranking materially improves final task quality.

## Detailed answer

Index normalized document embeddings and retrieve a bounded top-k set, optionally fused with keyword search. Apply authorization and version filters consistently, then score candidates with the reranker and pass the best evidence to generation. Reranking cannot recover documents missing from the candidate pool. Sweep candidate count, passage length and batch size against latency and relevance judgments; skip the stage if its extra cost buys little. Cache only under compatible query, document, model and permission versions. Measure answer support and successful task completion in addition to ranking scores; selling both components is justified by complementary measured value, not by architectural fashion.

### The two models see different amounts of interaction

A bi-encoder embeds the query and each document independently, enabling approximate-nearest-neighbor search across a large corpus. A cross-encoder reranker reads a query and candidate together, allowing token-level interactions that can distinguish subtle negation or constraint matching. Running that expensive comparison against every document would be impractical, so retrieval first narrows the set.

For “laptops under 1 kg with 32 GB RAM,” embeddings may find broadly similar laptop passages while missing which constraints each item satisfies. A reranker can compare those conditions more precisely among the candidates, but cannot recover an eligible laptop omitted upstream. Measure recall at candidate depth before judging reranking. Sweep top-k and passage length under the end-to-end budget, and compare answer correctness or search success against the added latency. Skip reranking for simple exact-ID queries or cases where gains are negligible. Keep permission filtering and freshness consistent so a better relevance score never makes inaccessible evidence eligible.
