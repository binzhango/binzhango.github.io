## Question

You train natively in int8 rather than doing post-training quantization. Defend that.

## Brief answer

Defend int8-native training only if measured end-to-end savings outweigh accuracy, stability, and engineering costs. Specify which operations and states are int8: claiming all training is int8 often hides higher-precision accumulators, master weights, gradients, or optimizer state. Low-precision training and post-training quantization solve different parts of the lifecycle.

## Detailed answer

A proposed implementation might quantize selected matrix multiplies with per-channel or block scales, accumulate in higher precision, and keep sensitive reductions and optimizer updates in a stable format. Track activation outliers, clipping, scale updates, and gradient underflow. Calibrate across early and late training, not just a short stable interval.

Compare equal-quality time-to-train, peak memory, energy, and downstream evaluation with a mixed-precision baseline. Include kernel conversion overhead and hardware support; nominally cheaper arithmetic can still be slower. Native training may adapt representations to quantization noise, but it also expands the numerical failure surface. Avoid asserting a benefit without a reproducible experiment on the chosen model and accelerator, and keep a rollback path to higher precision for unstable layers.

### State the numerical contract

For a symmetric quantizer, a scaled integer approximation is `q = clip(round(x/s), -127, 127)`, with reconstructed value approximately `s*q`. Values smaller than the quantization step can disappear, while outliers force a larger scale and reduce resolution for ordinary values. Per-channel or block scaling limits that compromise but adds metadata and conversion work. Gradients and optimizer accumulators often need a different precision policy from forward activations.

During training, scales must adapt as distributions change. Track clipping frequency, gradient norms, loss spikes, and layerwise error against a higher-precision reference. A short run can miss instability appearing after a learning-rate transition or on an unusual batch. Use matched data order and seeds where practical, then repeat enough runs to distinguish precision effects from training variance.

The relevant comparison is equal-quality training cost, not nominal integer throughput. Include quantize/dequantize kernels, memory transfers, retained master state, and convergence steps. If an int8 path runs each step faster but needs substantially more steps or produces worse downstream quality, it has not established the desired benefit. Post-training quantization remains simpler when the only objective is serving efficiency.
