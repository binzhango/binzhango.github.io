## Question

Why this organization and team? Describe a time you pushed back on a customer request.

## Brief answer

Connect the organization's work and the team's specific responsibilities to your actual interests and evidence of fit, then use a real customer-pushback example to demonstrate judgment. Avoid generic enthusiasm or invented experience. Explain how you protected the customer's underlying goal while challenging a request that would have harmed it.

## Detailed answer

**Response framework.** Identify one concrete problem the team works on and why your skills or learning goals match it. Base that claim on verified role information and conversations, not assumptions about the employer. For the pushback story, state the requested solution, the underlying need, the risk you identified, and the evidence you brought.

Describe how you listened, offered alternatives, clarified decision authority, and reached or escalated a decision. Report the actual outcome and what you learned, including any mistake in your initial judgment. A strong answer shows respectful disagreement and accountability; it does not portray customers as obstacles or claim that refusing a request is inherently principled.

### Connect motivation and judgment with evidence

For motivation, name a concrete problem the team owns, explain why it interests you, and connect it to an actual skill or learning objective. “I want to work on reliable operational AI because I have built data integrations and want responsibility for their user outcomes” is a response pattern; replace it with your own truthful fit and verified role details. Avoid guessing the team's internal priorities.

For pushback, choose a real request where the proposed solution conflicted with the underlying need. **Illustrative scenario:** a customer asks for automatic ERP writes, but the available asset identifiers are ambiguous. The engineer shows wrong-target examples and proposes a draft-and-review stage while identity quality is improved. Explain how you listened, presented alternatives, agreed on decision authority, and followed through. Include the outcome and any evidence that changed your own view. The story should demonstrate protection of the customer's goal, not pride in saying no or portraying the customer as unreasonable.
