## Question

What is PEFT, why use it, and what are its advantages?

## Brief answer

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning trains a limited subset of parameters or small added modules while freezing most pretrained weights. It reduces gradient, optimizer-state, and task-checkpoint storage, but the base model and substantial activation computation remain necessary.

## Detailed answer

Keeping the backbone frozen avoids gradients and optimizer moments for those weights, though forward computation and many activations still consume resources. Small adapters also let several tasks share a base checkpoint. PEFT does not make the base model disappear from memory or guarantee full-tuning quality. QLoRA further reduces backbone storage through quantization; it is a particular method, not a property of every PEFT technique.

Examples include bottleneck adapters, LoRA, and learned prompts or prefixes. LoRA represents a matrix update as $\Delta W=BA$ with rank $r$, using $r(d_{\mathrm{in}}+d_{\mathrm{out}})$ parameters rather than $d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}$. Benefits include smaller task artifacts and lower optimizer memory. Quality, activation memory, and inference overhead depend on the method; LoRA updates can often be merged into a compatible dense weight matrix.

For a square $4096\times4096$ projection, full tuning has 16,777,216 trainable weights in that matrix. A rank-8 LoRA update uses $8(4096+4096)=65,536$ factor parameters, about 0.39% of that count. This is a calculation for one adapted matrix, not the percentage of the entire model. The actual total depends on which projections and layers receive adapters.

Freezing the backbone removes its gradient and optimizer-state storage, but training still runs through the backbone to compute activations and gradients needed by the added modules. Thus PEFT can greatly reduce persistent training-state memory while leaving significant activation and forward/backward cost. Quantizing the frozen base addresses another part of the memory budget.

Small task artifacts simplify storing and switching multiple adaptations, provided each is paired with the correct base revision and tokenizer. Different methods have different serving behavior: a linear LoRA update can often be merged, while nonlinear bottleneck adapters remain extra computation. Evaluate held-out quality and general regressions, because parameter efficiency is a resource property rather than a guarantee of equal full-tuning performance or protection against learning incorrect targets.
