## Question

Why construct hard negative samples?

## Brief answer

Hard negatives train a retriever to distinguish genuinely answer-supporting passages from plausible but irrelevant ones.

## Detailed answer

For a question about a product's refund deadline, a passage about delivery deadlines may be a useful hard negative. Mine candidates from a current retriever or reranker, then verify that they are actually negative. Unlabeled relevant documents are common false negatives. Mix difficulty levels and monitor held-out recall so training does not specialize only to one miner's mistakes.

A retriever needs to learn fine distinctions among related passages. If the question asks for a refund deadline, random text about gardening offers an easy separation. A delivery deadline for the same product shares many words and may score highly, but does not answer the request. Training against that verified hard negative encourages the encoder to represent the requested policy type rather than only the product name.

Mine candidates using a current retriever, then inspect or label them for true answer support. Removing only the known positive is insufficient when multiple passages contain the answer. A valid alternative source labeled negative would teach the model to suppress useful evidence.

Mix hard negatives with easier and diverse examples so training does not specialize to one retrieval model's quirks. Refresh the pool as the encoder changes, and hold out queries and sources for evaluation. Monitor both aggregate recall and difficult slices such as similar versions, near-identical entities, and opposite policy conditions. A lower contrastive loss is useful only if the resulting distinctions improve retrieval on new information needs.
