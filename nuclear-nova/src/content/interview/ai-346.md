## Question

How would you decide an LLM-powered assistant is ready to launch to millions of customers?

## Brief answer

Launch when representative evaluations, production readiness, and staged live evidence meet predefined thresholds for usefulness, reliability, and severe errors. The thresholds depend on the assistant's authority and consequences. A public benchmark score or persuasive demo is insufficient; failure handling, permission controls, and rollback must work before broad deployment.

## Detailed answer

Define supported tasks and out-of-scope behavior. Build a held-out suite from real user needs, including adversarial inputs, multilingual slices, inaccessible information, and tool failures. Score factual correctness, task completion, unauthorized actions, latency, and cost. Have domain experts review consequential failures and unresolved ambiguity.

Load-test realistic traffic and long-tail contexts, exercise dependency outages, and verify cancellation, quotas, privacy controls, and observability. Roll out internally, then to limited cohorts with clear stop conditions. Monitor corrections, human escalations, complaints, and outcome quality rather than engagement alone. Maintain versioned prompts, models, data sources, and evaluations so regressions can be reproduced and reverted. Expand scope only when evidence supports the additional tasks.

### Turn “ready” into scoped release gates

Define a supported task such as answering product questions from approved documentation, with explicit refusal and escalation behavior for account changes. A release gate can require acceptable factual accuracy on representative tasks, no observed unauthorized actions in a targeted adversarial suite, latency within the product budget, and a functioning rollback path. Passing a finite test suite never proves zero future failures, so monitor severity and uncertainty explicitly.

Run shadow traffic first when data policy permits, then a small canary with a stable control group. Check whether user corrections and repeated attempts increase even if engagement rises. Predefine who can stop rollout, which severe events trigger it, and how source/model versions are restored. Include dependency outages and overload because a model that works only with every tool healthy is not ready at scale. Expand languages and workflows separately; evidence for one task does not establish readiness for every assistant capability.
