## Question

Explain RoPE and how position interpolation / YaRN extend context beyond the trained length.

## Brief answer

RoPE rotates query and key coordinate pairs according to position. Position interpolation compresses longer positions into the trained range; YaRN adjusts rotary-frequency scaling and attention behavior to extend usable context with adaptation.

## Detailed answer

For a coordinate pair, apply a two-dimensional rotation with angle $p\theta_i$. Query–key dot products then encode relative position through angle differences. Naively increasing the window exposes rotations the model did not learn to interpret. Interpolation changes position spacing, trading resolution at short distances for longer coverage. YaRN uses dimension-dependent treatment and attention scaling rather than uniform interpolation alone. Verify retrieval across positions and short-context regressions after extension.

For one pair, the rotation is $R(\phi)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi\\\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{bmatrix}$. The identity $(R(m\theta)q)^T(R(n\theta)k)=q^TR((n-m)\theta)k$ explains how relative distance enters an otherwise content-based dot product. Different coordinate pairs use different frequencies, providing several distance scales.

Suppose a model was trained at length 4,096 and should handle 16,384 positions. Uniform position interpolation can map position $p$ to $p/4$, keeping the largest effective position close to the original range. But adjacent tokens now differ by only one quarter of the original phase increment. This compresses local distinctions as well as distant ones; merely changing the maximum-position setting would instead expose unadapted phases.

YaRN treats frequency bands differently and adjusts attention scaling, aiming to retain local resolution while extending longer-distance behavior. It should be understood as a specific extension recipe, not a guarantee of arbitrary context length. Adaptation and evaluation must test evidence at several positions and distances, conflicting facts, multi-hop reasoning and ordinary short prompts. Successful single-fact retrieval at the end of a long document does not prove that all of its content is being used reliably.
