## Question

Two days before a release decision, simulation shows a 15% increase in hard-braking events in one scenario cluster. Walk me through what you do.

## Brief answer

Pause the affected release decision, verify the metric and scenario cluster, and determine whether the 15% relative increase reflects meaningful risk or a measurement artifact. Inspect absolute counts, severity, exposure, and uncertainty. Do not dismiss a localized regression because the overall score improved or the release date is close.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Reproduce old and new builds on identical seeds, inspect representative traces, and check simulator/configuration changes. Determine whether braking is unnecessary harshness or a correct response to previously missed hazards. Analyze timing, perception, predicted actors, and planner constraints to identify the responsible stage.

Run targeted variants and independent validation, then fix, revert, or restrict the deployment scope according to predefined gates. Record the evidence and residual uncertainty for the accountable release owner. Monitor related clusters to avoid a narrow patch that shifts the problem. If the evidence cannot be resolved in two days, the schedule does not make the uncertainty disappear; use the existing release policy rather than inventing a favorable exception.

### Distinguish relative change from consequence

A rise from 20 to 23 hard-braking events and a rise from 2,000 to 2,300 are both 15%, but have different uncertainty and exposure. Normalize by scenario opportunities and examine severity, peak deceleration, jerk, and time-to-collision. More braking may indicate overreaction, or earlier detection of a real hazard that the old build missed.

Freeze the two builds, simulator version, seeds, and metrics. Review paired traces to locate the first divergence, then test neighboring conditions to see whether the cluster is a narrow artifact or a broader mechanism. Compare perception inputs, predicted trajectories, and planner constraints before changing a threshold. A patch that merely reduces braking can make collision avoidance worse. Present the release owner with evidence, options, and residual uncertainty: fix and revalidate, revert the responsible change, restrict scope, or delay. Preserve the cluster as a regression suite and verify adjacent scenarios so schedule pressure does not turn a local score repair into an unmeasured safety trade-off.
