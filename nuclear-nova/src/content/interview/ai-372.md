## Question

A customer's LLM chatbot on 8 GPUs is “too slow and too expensive.” You have one week with them. What do you do?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: Start with the customer's definition of slow and expensive, then collect representative traces and establish a reproducible baseline. In one week, prioritize the largest measured bottleneck and one or two safe changes with clear quality gates. Do not promise a hardware migration or model replacement before separating queueing, prefill, decode, retrieval, and unnecessary work.

## Detailed answer

On the first day, measure request mix, token lengths, concurrency, cache use, GPU memory, utilization, and p95 latency. Check retry loops, overlong prompts, oversized models, and idle replicas. On subsequent days, test targeted changes such as continuous batching, shorter retrieval context, prefix reuse, quantization, or better model routing.

Use paired task evaluations and load tests with identical traffic before accepting a saving. Include engineering effort, availability, and rollback costs. Deliver a reproducible benchmark, recommended configuration, capacity curve, and a staged rollout plan. If the real bottleneck is a slow tool or incorrect SLO, explain that evidence directly rather than forcing a GPU optimization because eight GPUs were mentioned.

### Turn the week into a measured experiment

Day one establishes a reproducible baseline: request-length distribution, success rubric, current SLOs, and traces separating tools, queueing, prefill, and decode. Days two and three test the largest observed bottlenecks individually. Days four and five combine compatible improvements, rerun quality and load tests, and prepare a canary with rollback. Keep configuration and hardware constant when comparing one change.

For example, eight GPUs may hold four mostly idle replicas because a request router pins sessions unevenly. Better placement can reduce cost without changing precision or model quality. Alternatively, a long output requirement may dominate latency even at good utilization; shorter task-appropriate output or a different workflow is needed. Present a capacity curve showing cost at the required p95 latency, not only peak tokens per second. Explain uncertainty in projected savings and include operational effort. A useful engagement can conclude that the current accelerator count is reasonable and the application layer needs repair.
