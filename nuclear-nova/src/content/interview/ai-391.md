## Question

Design a content recommendation system for a streaming catalogue, including cold-start for new titles and new users.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: combine collaborative and content-based retrieval, rank for the user's current viewing intent, and diversify the result within licensing and availability constraints. New titles rely initially on metadata and content embeddings; new users rely on explicit preferences, locale, contextual signals, and carefully chosen exploration. Popularity alone creates a cold-start trap.

## Detailed answer

Ingest genre, cast, language, age rating, synopsis, availability windows, and visual/audio features where justified. Use historical interactions carefully: completion, rewatching, and abandonment have different meanings by content length and user context. Retrieve from multiple sources and rank with calibrated objectives tied to satisfaction.

Use temporal holdouts and evaluate genuinely new titles and users separately. Measure successful starts, meaningful completion, diversity, and retention with experiments, while avoiding clickbait incentives. Respect household profiles and parental settings. Prevent expired or unavailable titles from appearing through stale caches. Provide a reliable editorial fallback and track whether exploration yields useful feedback without overwhelming users with irrelevant content.

### Cold start requires information besides clicks

A new title has no interaction history, so represent it with language, genre, cast, synopsis, age rating, and other legitimate content features. Retrieve it near related established titles and allocate limited exploration to learn audience response. A new user can provide preferences or receive diverse popular/editorial candidates, then adapt as meaningful interactions accumulate.

Distinguish a click from satisfaction: a short start followed by abandonment can mean a misleading recommendation, while completion depends on content length and viewing context. Train with multiple outcome signals and inspect household/profile mixing. A two-stage system retrieves candidates from collaborative and content sources, ranks them for the session, then enforces availability and diversity. Evaluate titles released after the training cutoff to measure real cold-start behavior rather than randomly hiding old interactions. Keep licensing windows and regional availability in the serving path; a relevant unavailable title is still a failed recommendation.
