## Question

Design a distributed search system for 1 billion documents at 1 million QPS.

## Brief answer

Partition the document index across shards and replicate each shard, with query brokers performing candidate retrieval and result merging. A million queries per second requires aggressive caching, selective routing and explicit fan-out budgets; sending every query to every shard can overwhelm the cluster. Establish latency, freshness and relevance requirements before sizing hardware.

## Detailed answer

Suggested design: ingest through a durable log, build lexical and optional vector indexes, and publish immutable index segments with versioned metadata. Queries pass through authentication, normalization and cache lookup, then route to relevant partitions. Shards return bounded candidates; a merger deduplicates, ranks and optionally reranks a small final set.

At 1 million QPS, fan-out to 100 shards means 100 million shard requests per second before replication. Use document/tenant routing, hierarchical retrieval, hot-query caches and replicas placed near demand. Avoid caching permission-sensitive results without including authorization scope in the key.

Capacity planning includes postings size, vector storage, replicas, updates and query skew—not just one billion raw documents. Test shard failures, hot terms, slow replicas and partial results. Expose freshness and degradation semantics; silently returning stale or incomplete results may be worse than a clear retry for some applications.

Capacity follows the work amplification. If every query contacts 100 shards, the broker creates 100 million shard operations per second at the stated global load. Caching common queries and routing by tenant, language or document partition can reduce that fan-out, but overly narrow routing can lose relevant results. Measure that relevance tradeoff explicitly.

Use replicas to absorb hot partitions and hedged requests cautiously for slow tails, with a strict duplicate-work budget. A merger needs stable document IDs, score calibration or rank fusion, and a defined policy for missing shard responses. Returning partial results may be acceptable for discovery but not for a completeness-critical compliance search.

Freshness and index rebuilds are separate capacity demands. Publish immutable segments, replicate them, and atomically change the active version while retaining rollback metadata. Test a hot term, skewed tenant, replica lag and a rolling rebuild under peak load. The design should state which guarantees degrade during failure—latency, recall or freshness—rather than assuming replication eliminates all tradeoffs.
