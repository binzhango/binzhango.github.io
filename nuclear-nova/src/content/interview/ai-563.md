## Question

Design the safety architecture for a learned whole-body policy operating near people.

## Brief answer

Place learned motion inside an independently enforced safety envelope with reliable sensing, limits, watchdogs, and emergency stopping. The policy can propose actions, but it must not control whether safety checks apply. Define the robot's permitted operating conditions and validate them with qualified robotics safety owners.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Separate high-level policy, real-time controller, and safety monitor. Enforce joint, velocity, torque, workspace, and contact limits at the appropriate control layer; detect stale commands, sensor faults, loss of balance, and unexpected proximity. Use hardware emergency stops and a tested safe-state strategy appropriate to the robot, since an abrupt power cut can itself cause a fall.

Validate worst-case stopping behavior, sensor blind spots, and fault combinations under controlled conditions. Stage deployment away from people before supervised collaboration. Log interventions and near misses, and prevent software updates from bypassing the independent monitor. Simulation and learned risk scores can support testing but cannot alone certify the physical system. Scope claims to the actual hardware, environment, and verified operating envelope.

### Analyze the complete stopping path

Draw the path from hazard sensing through detection, decision, command transport, actuator response, and the resulting physical motion. A monitor that detects proximity quickly can still be inadequate if actuation or braking is delayed. Validate the complete response under worst relevant payload, speed, posture, and surface conditions with qualified safety engineering.

Keep safety decisions independent of the learned policy's confidence. The policy should not be able to disable a limit, reinterpret a stale sensor as healthy, or choose whether an emergency stop applies. Use watchdogs for missing observations and commands, bounded action interfaces, and protected configuration for the validated operating envelope.

The safe state depends on the robot. Freezing joint targets, reducing torque, controlled deceleration, supported kneeling, and removing power have different consequences. A humanoid carrying a load may fall or drop it if power is simply cut. Define and test the appropriate response for each fault class rather than using one generic “stop” command.

Evaluate combinations such as sensor occlusion plus delayed commands, not only isolated component failures. Log interventions with enough context to improve the system, and review software or hardware changes for effects on the safety argument. A learned risk estimator can supply additional information, but it should not be the sole barrier between an unpredictable action and a person.
