## Question

Two partners at the same firm are on opposite sides of a deal. Design the data isolation for that, on top of normal multi-tenancy.

## Brief answer

Add matter-level ethical walls to tenant isolation and enforce them across ingestion, retrieval, generation, caches, logs, exports, and administration. Two users in the same firm must not automatically share access. Authorization belongs in trusted services using current identity and matter context, not in the model prompt.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Every document and derived artifact carries tenant, matter, and access-policy identifiers. Query execution resolves the user's effective permissions and filters before model context assembly. Cache keys include the relevant authorization scope and policy version; shared embeddings must not expose unauthorized text or membership. Recheck permissions at download and action time.

Use narrowly controlled break-glass access with reason, approval, and audit, and avoid revealing restricted matter names through autocomplete or counts. Propagate revocations and deletions to indexes and cached answers promptly. Test cross-matter references, shared templates, partner role changes, exports, and background jobs using stale credentials. Evaluate isolation through adversarial tests, not only positive access cases. The firm's professional rules and security owners determine the policy; the system must faithfully enforce it.

### Prevent indirect disclosure too

Matter isolation must govern both content and derived signals. A user should not learn that an opposing matter exists through autocomplete, a document count, a cached answer, or a ranking explanation saying a colleague accessed it. Filter the candidate universe before generating such metadata, and keep authorization scope in every cache and job identity.

For a query spanning a shared template and a restricted negotiation draft, the user may access the template but not the draft. A background summarization job must not combine them into a new artifact with only the template's broad permissions. Derived artifacts need a policy at least as restrictive as the evidence they contain, or separate outputs for different authorized audiences.

Revocation is a distributed invalidation problem. Update the authorization service, prevent new retrieval, expire cached answers and downloads, and reconcile long-running jobs before they publish. Test a partner moving between matters during an active session, not just access at login. Auditable exceptions must be narrowly scoped and time-bounded; an administrative support role should not automatically bypass every ethical wall.
