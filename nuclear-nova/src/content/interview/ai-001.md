## Question

Explain scaled dot-product attention and why the 1/sqrt(d_k) scaling factor matters.

## Brief answer

Attention computes a weighted sum of values using query–key similarity. Dividing dot products by the square root of the key dimension keeps their scale from growing with dimension and pushing softmax prematurely into saturation.

## Detailed answer

For $Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_k}$, $K\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_k}$ and $V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_v}$, compute $\operatorname{softmax}(QK^T/\sqrt{d_k}+M)V$, with softmax over keys. Under independent, zero-mean, unit-variance coordinates, a dot product has variance $d_k$; the divisor restores variance near one. The assumptions explain the heuristic, not a guarantee about learned vectors. A causal mask assigns $-\infty$ to future keys.

For a concrete row, suppose the unscaled similarities to three keys are $[2,0,-2]$ and $d_k=4$. Scaling produces $[1,0,-1]$, whose softmax weights are approximately $[0.665,0.245,0.090]$. If the corresponding scalar values are $[10,20,30]$, the output is about $14.25$. Similarity determines how much to read; the values determine what is read. Every query gets its own distribution, and the weights sum to one over its allowed keys.

Without scaling, increasing the dimension makes even initially random logits more extreme. A nearly one-hot softmax gives very small derivatives for most entries, making early learning harder. Scaling controls this tendency without forcing attention to stay diffuse: training can still learn a sharp match. Numerically, subtract the row maximum before exponentiation and compute sensitive reductions at sufficient precision. Apply padding and causal masks before softmax; an entirely masked row needs explicit handling because softmax of all negative infinity is undefined. Attention is differentiable content-based mixing, not a guarantee that a highly weighted token is the causal explanation for the final prediction.
