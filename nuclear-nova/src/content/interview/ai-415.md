## Question

Determinism is the headline claim. What does it actually buy at p99, and why does it matter especially for agentic workloads?

## Brief answer

Deterministic execution can reduce variance in the device portion of token generation, making latency budgets and capacity planning more predictable. It does not eliminate queueing, networking, tool delays, variable output lengths, or failures. Agentic tasks can amplify tail latency because many sequential model/tool steps must finish before the user sees the final outcome.

## Detailed answer

For a workflow with several dependent calls, occasional slow calls accumulate and may trigger deadlines or retries. A stable device schedule can make that portion easier to reason about, but end-to-end p99 still depends on load and orchestration. Do not multiply single-call p99 by step count as though it were an exact workflow quantile; measure the joint process and correlations.

Compare latency distributions at matched arrival patterns, utilization, model quality, and context lengths. Track first-token, inter-token, and complete-task latency, including admission control. Determinism is especially valuable when it allows tighter SLOs with less spare capacity, but a deterministic overloaded queue is still slow. Separate documented hardware guarantees from empirical service-level behavior.

### Predictable service time helps only one part of latency

End-to-end latency equals queue waiting, device execution, network transfers, and application/tool work. A deterministic device schedule narrows execution variance and can simplify capacity planning, but a queue with arrivals above service capacity still grows without bound. Admission control and headroom remain necessary.

In a hypothetical agent with twenty dependent calls, even independent 1% slow-call events produce about an 18.2% chance of at least one slow call: $1-0.99^{20}$. This illustrates why tails matter, without asserting that real call delays are independent. Correlated congestion can be worse. Measure complete workflows at realistic utilization and tool latency, not merely isolated token intervals. Use deadlines and bounded retries so one late step does not create cascading work. Determinism is valuable when it supports reliable SLOs or less overprovisioning, but claims about hardware timing should not be restated as guarantees for the entire service.
