## Question

Design a personalised news-feed ranking system / the “next post” logic for Facebook's feed.

## Brief answer

Retrieve eligible posts from followed accounts and discovery sources, score predicted user value, and rerank the feed with freshness, diversity and policy constraints. Keep feature updates timely enough to reflect recent interests without overreacting to one click.

## Detailed answer

A hypothetical feed design maintains candidate indexes and streaming aggregates for recent interactions. Rank with multiple targets such as meaningful engagement, hide/report risk and repeat exposure. Treat clicks as noisy observations affected by placement; train with exposure-aware data and evaluate chronological holdouts. Avoid feedback loops that repeatedly narrow the feed to the same topic. Use a fast fallback when personalized ranking is unavailable and exclude deleted or inaccessible posts at delivery time. Compare long-term retention and satisfaction through controlled experiments, with segment-level checks and explicit limits on objectionable exposure.

### “Next post” depends on the session state

The feed should account for which posts the user has already seen, recent skips or hides, and whether repeating an author or topic would be tiresome. Maintain a session-level exposure state and rerank a candidate pool rather than independently choosing the highest static score each time. Newly deleted or restricted posts must be removed even if they were eligible when the pool was first built.

Use separate offline pipelines for durable representations and streaming features for recent activity. Score several user-value signals, then apply product constraints such as diversity, freshness, and repetition limits. An explicit hide should not be treated as an ordinary non-click. Cold-start users need declared interests or broadly useful candidates, and new posts need limited exploration. Evaluate long-term satisfaction and objectionable exposure alongside engagement. The feedback loop matters: showing only one topic creates more data about that topic, which can falsely convince the model that the user wants nothing else. Track source diversity and controlled exploration to detect this narrowing.
