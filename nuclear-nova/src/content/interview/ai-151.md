## Question

An enterprise customer says “Claude hallucinates too much” in their RAG-based knowledge assistant. You're the applied engineer on the account. What happens in the first 48 hours?

## Brief answer

Translate “hallucinates too much” into reproducible failure categories before changing the model. In the first 48 hours, gather examples and traces, verify retrieval and permissions, compare generated claims with evidence, and establish a small adjudicated evaluation set. Apply a targeted containment measure while investigating; do not promise that adding more documents will solve unsupported answers.

## Detailed answer

During the first day, collect failing and successful queries with consent, expected answers, source versions, retrieved chunks, reranker scores, prompts and model settings. Separate missing evidence, retrieval misses, stale/conflicting evidence, incorrect synthesis and citation mismatch. Check whether the correct document was indexed and accessible to the user.

During the second day, run ablations: gold evidence versus retrieved evidence, lexical versus vector retrieval, different chunking and constrained answer prompts. Measure retrieval recall and claim-level support independently. If gold evidence still produces errors, generation is implicated; if it fixes them, focus upstream.

Contain high-risk cases with explicit uncertainty, source display or human review. Agree on acceptance metrics and an owner for the next experiment. Deliver a concise diagnosis with representative cases, not a broad provider/model comparison based on anecdotes.

Agree on a small set of concrete acceptance criteria with the customer before proposing fixes. “No unsupported refund-policy claims on these adjudicated cases” is actionable; “hallucinate less” is not. Include successful examples so the investigation does not optimize away useful behavior by refusing every question.

A useful diagnostic matrix has rows for failures and columns for source presence, authorization, retrieval rank, context inclusion, generated claim and citation support. If the authoritative passage is absent from the prompt, changing temperature is unlikely to solve the root problem. If it is present but a qualification is dropped, inspect generation and chunk boundaries. If sources conflict, the answer needs version policy and explicit uncertainty.

Within 48 hours, deliver the reproducible set, classified causes, a targeted containment change and the next controlled experiment. Avoid changing chunking, embeddings, prompts and model simultaneously, which makes any improvement hard to attribute. Preserve traces under the customer's data-handling rules and make clear which conclusions are supported by the sample and which remain hypotheses.
