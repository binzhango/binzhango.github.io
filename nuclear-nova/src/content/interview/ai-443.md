## Question

Design a private LLM benchmark and leaderboard (SEAL-style). How do you keep it trustworthy as labs optimise against it?

## Brief answer

Keep the benchmark private, periodically refreshed, and governed independently from participants. Use locked model submissions, reproducible execution, uncertainty intervals, and coarse feedback that does not expose individual hidden tasks. A leaderboard remains credible only if its measurement scope and susceptibility to optimization are explicit.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Draw tasks from a documented target distribution and hold out both a stable regression panel and a rotating confidential panel. Version datasets, harnesses, tool environments, and scoring rubrics. Limit submission frequency and detect suspicious overlap through canary items and provenance review. Require participants to identify model versions and permitted tools; rerun sampled submissions to assess variance.

Publish category results, sample sizes, confidence intervals, and execution budgets rather than a single unexplained score. Keep task-level diagnoses in a separate public development set. Have blinded specialists adjudicate ambiguous failures and provide an appeal process with an audit trail. Monitor ranking stability on fresh tasks: a widening gap between old and fresh panels is evidence of benchmark adaptation. Avoid using public visibility or a high score as proof of broad deployment readiness.

### Limit what a submission can learn

A hidden benchmark is still vulnerable when repeated aggregate scores reveal which changes work. Separate the public tuning interface from a less frequently evaluated final panel, restrict repeated submissions, and require immutable artifacts or version-pinned endpoints. Otherwise a participant can optimize against hidden cases indirectly or serve special behavior only while being evaluated.

For a paired comparison, run both systems on the same tasks and examine per-task differences. A nominal two-point lead can be inconclusive when the sample is small or outputs vary greatly across runs. Report confidence intervals and category breakdowns; preserve ties rather than forcing a total ranking unsupported by the evidence. A tool-using model also needs the same tool permissions and resource budget as its competitor.

Refresh tasks without silently changing what the leaderboard measures. Maintain an overlap panel to connect old and new scores, publish the distribution changes, and avoid claiming that a fresh score is directly comparable with a historical one when the task mixture changed. Confidentiality protects measurement; independent governance, reproducibility, and honest uncertainty make the result interpretable.
