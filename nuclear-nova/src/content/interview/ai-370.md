## Question

Design the parallelism strategy for serving a 405B-parameter dense model. TP, PP, EP: what goes where and why?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: For a dense 405B model, use tensor parallelism within fast interconnect domains and pipeline parallelism across domains when necessary, then replicate the resulting model group for throughput. Expert parallelism applies to MoE experts and is not a natural partition of a dense model. Choose the layout from memory, bandwidth, latency, and expected concurrency.

## Detailed answer

At two bytes per parameter, raw weights are about 810 GB, before cache and buffers. Quantization can reduce this but requires quality and kernel validation. Tensor parallel layers require frequent collectives, so crossing a slow network can make every token expensive. Pipeline stages move activations less frequently but introduce bubbles and stage-balancing concerns, especially for small batches.

Estimate per-stage weights and KV cache, reserve runtime headroom, and place layers to balance actual compute rather than only parameter count. Use continuous batching or microbatching to improve pipeline utilization while protecting latency. Measure prefill and decode separately. Failure handling must account for the entire model group; autoscaling and rolling upgrades operate at that granularity rather than one arbitrary GPU.

### A plausible placement calculation

At BF16, 405B weights require about 810 GB. Twelve 80 GB devices provide 960 GB raw capacity, but that leaves only 150 GB across the group for cache and runtime overhead and may not match a supported efficient topology. A larger group can provide headroom and better partitioning, at the cost of more communication and idle capacity.

Use tensor parallelism within a fast interconnect domain for large layer operations, and pipeline parallelism across domains when splitting layers reduces expensive cross-domain collectives. Balance stages by measured time, not equal layer count if embeddings or attention sizes differ. Expert parallelism applies to sparse experts; a dense 405B model has no expert axis to distribute unless its architecture changes. Continuous batches help fill pipeline stages but compete with per-request latency. Evaluate prefill and decode separately, include redundant model groups for availability, and benchmark a full deployment group because one-device measurements cannot capture collective and pipeline costs.
