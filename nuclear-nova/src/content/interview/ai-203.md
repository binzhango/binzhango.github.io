## Question

Design a personalised recommendation system for rental listings using demographics, property metadata, amenities, price, reviews and location.

## Brief answer

Retrieve a feasible set of rental listings, then rank for the user's stated needs and likely satisfaction while enforcing availability and policy constraints. Price, location and amenities often provide strong signals; demographics can introduce sensitive proxies and should not be used casually. Optimize useful contacts or successful matches rather than clicks alone.

## Detailed answer

Suggested pipeline: normalize listing metadata and geospatial features, filter unavailable or incompatible inventory, generate candidates from search preferences and behavioral similarity, then rank with calibrated relevance and utility features. Keep freshness near real time for availability and price. New users need explicit preferences and contextual defaults; new listings need content-based features.

Training labels should distinguish impressions, saves, inquiries and completed outcomes, accounting for position bias and delayed feedback. Evaluate ranking quality, coverage, geographic diversity and disparities in exposure. Reviews can be noisy or manipulated and should not automatically override current verified facts.

Use temporal holdouts and online experiments with guardrails for complaint rates and listing quality. For a real housing product, specialist review of applicable fairness and data-use constraints is essential. A technically predictive demographic feature is not automatically an appropriate product feature.

Candidate generation should first respect explicit feasible constraints such as budget, location, availability and required amenities. Ranking can then estimate satisfaction among eligible options. A high predicted click rate on an unaffordable or unavailable listing is not useful personalization. Keep verified inventory changes on a fast update path independent of slower model retraining.

Use temporal evaluation because listings expire and user preferences change. Training only on contacted listings ignores unseen alternatives and can reinforce exposure bias; log impressions and consider how ranking position affects observed engagement. Cold-start users can state preferences, while new listings rely on content and location features rather than nonexistent interaction history.

Demographic variables and correlated proxies require particular care in a housing context. Predictive usefulness alone does not establish appropriateness or compliance. Evaluate exposure and outcome disparities with authorized data and specialist review. Optimize successful, suitable matches and user trust, with guardrails for complaints and stale inventory, rather than treating additional clicks as a complete business objective.
