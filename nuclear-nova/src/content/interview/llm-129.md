## Question

How to match retrieval model output with LLM preferences?

## Brief answer

Select evidence that supports the answer the generator must produce, rather than optimizing semantic similarity alone.

## Detailed answer

Train or evaluate the retriever with answer-supporting relevance labels, and rerank query-passage pairs for answerability. Preserve necessary definitions, qualifiers, and surrounding context when packing evidence. End-to-end feedback can improve selection, but a generator's preference for a passage is not proof that it is accurate. Keep retrieval relevance, factual correctness, and answer usefulness as distinct evaluation targets.

A semantically related passage is not always useful evidence. For “Does the extended warranty cover accidental damage?”, a general warranty overview may rank highly while the needed exclusion is buried elsewhere. Relevance labels should reward passages that establish the requested answer, including conditions and exceptions, rather than passages that merely share vocabulary.

Reranking can assess the query and passage jointly, while context expansion restores neighboring material. For multi-part questions, choose complementary evidence instead of several redundant chunks with high individual scores. Preserve source dates and versions because the generator cannot resolve a policy conflict if that metadata is missing.

Evaluate with two views: retrieval metrics based on support judgments and final answer quality with the selected context. An apparent generator preference can be misleading if it favors fluent, simple passages over correct but qualified evidence. Use manual or verified support labels where possible and inspect counterexamples. The goal is to make the required reasoning possible from the prompt, not to select passages that make the generator most confident or produce the shortest answer.
