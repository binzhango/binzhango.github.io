## Question

Explain the prefill and decode phases. Why is prefill compute-bound and decode memory-bandwidth-bound?

## Brief answer

Prefill processes all prompt tokens and creates the KV cache; decode repeatedly adds one token per active sequence. Prefill often has high arithmetic intensity, whereas low-batch decoding often spends more time moving weights and KV data than multiplying them.

## Detailed answer

Matrix–matrix operations during prefill reuse weights across many tokens. Batch-one decoding resembles matrix–vector operations with much less reuse. These are tendencies, not fixed classifications: large decode batches improve reuse, long contexts increase attention traffic, and small prefills may be latency-bound. Profile FLOPs, memory bandwidth and queue time separately. Optimize time-to-first-token for prefill and inter-token latency for decode; speeding one stage does not automatically improve the other.

Arithmetic intensity is useful work per byte moved from memory. Reading a weight matrix once to multiply many prompt-token vectors amortizes its bandwidth cost. Reading it for a single decode token does much less arithmetic for approximately the same weight bytes. This is why a GPU with a high peak FLOP rating may be poorly utilized during batch-one generation.

Batching 32 decode requests lets the same weights process 32 new tokens in one iteration, improving reuse. However, each request also reads its own attention history, and larger batches consume more KV memory. At sufficiently long contexts, attention traffic may dominate even if dense projections are efficient. Decode is serial within a request: the next token depends on the previous sampled token, so the whole output cannot be processed like one prefill without a separate acceleration technique.

Use a timeline to distinguish CPU scheduling gaps, memory stalls and matrix computation. A long TTFT caused by queueing will not be fixed merely by optimizing attention. Conversely, reducing prefill time can improve admission but leave an overloaded decode pool unchanged. Capacity tests should sweep prompt length, output length and concurrency, reporting both device utilization and the fraction of requests meeting user-facing latency targets.
