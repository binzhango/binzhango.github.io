## Question

Tell me about a time you saw an opportunity to do something bigger than the initial scope. (Think Big)

## Brief answer

Response framework: explain how you discovered a larger user or business opportunity while working on a narrower task, then show how you tested it without losing delivery discipline. Distinguish a valuable expansion from uncontrolled scope growth. Use a real example with evidence of demand, an explicit trade-off, and a result you can defend.

## Detailed answer

Describe the original request and the repeated pain or broader pattern you noticed. Show how you checked that the opportunity existed through user conversations, usage data, or a small prototype. Identify what could be reused and what additional risks or dependencies the larger direction introduced.

Explain who agreed to the change, how you staged delivery, and what you deliberately deferred. Report adoption, time saved, reliability, revenue, or another relevant actual outcome; do not invent round-number impact. If the idea was rejected or failed, explain what the experiment taught you. The key is enlarging the value proposition while retaining a credible path to implementation.

### Make the larger idea testable

**Illustrative example:** a request for one team's reporting script reveals that several teams manually reconcile the same events. The broader opportunity is a shared event-quality service, but building a platform immediately could delay the original need. A staged response ships the first report, extracts the common validation layer, and tests adoption with a second team before investing further.

In your own answer, explain the evidence for repeated demand and the smallest experiment that could disprove it. Identify which requirements stayed specific and which were truly reusable. State how you obtained agreement on added scope, protected the deadline, and defined ownership for ongoing support. Report actual adoption or operational impact, including when the broader idea did not succeed. Thinking bigger demonstrates useful judgment when the wider outcome is credible; it is not simply adding more features or turning every task into a platform.
