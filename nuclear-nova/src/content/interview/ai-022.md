## Question

Compare FP16, BF16, FP8, INT8, INT4 and FP4 for serving. What breaks at each step down?

## Brief answer

Lower-precision formats reduce memory and can accelerate supported kernels, but represent range and precision differently. BF16 favors range over FP16 precision; integer formats use scales; FP8 and FP4 behavior depends on the exact format and hardware.

## Detailed answer

Weights, activations and KV cache need separate decisions. Weight-only INT4 may fit a model that BF16 cannot, yet dequantization or unsupported shapes can limit speed. Activation outliers can require per-channel scaling, mixed precision or calibration. Very low precision can damage rare-token, long-context or reasoning behavior even when average perplexity moves little. Compare a high-precision baseline on realistic tasks and benchmark the actual serving kernels. Never infer throughput directly from bit width.

FP16 and BF16 both use 16 bits but allocate them differently. BF16 has an eight-bit exponent, giving a wide dynamic range, while FP16 has more fraction bits and finer precision near a represented value but a smaller exponent range. FP8 formats further trade exponent range against precision. INT8 and INT4 usually represent values through quantization scales, for example $x\approx s(q-z)$, rather than storing a floating-point exponent per element.

Reducing bit width introduces rounding error; clipping introduces an additional error when values exceed the representable range. A single large outlier can set a coarse per-tensor scale that destroys small values, motivating per-channel or smaller-group scales. Those extra scales consume memory and can complicate kernels. FP4 likewise has very few representable values and often relies on block scaling; the name alone does not specify a complete quantization scheme.

Choose weight, activation and KV precision independently. Weight-only quantization may preserve high-precision activations and accumulation, whereas low-precision matrix computation requires supported hardware and scaling behavior. Calibrate on representative sequence lengths and domains, then inspect rare categories, numerical answers and long-context retrieval rather than relying on average perplexity. Measure actual latency: compression saves capacity even when conversion overhead prevents a proportional speedup.
