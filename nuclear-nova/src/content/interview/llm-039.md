## Question

Which activation functions do popular LLMs use?

## Brief answer

Examples include GELU in BERT and GPT-2, SwiGLU in Llama and PaLM, and a GELU-gated FFN in the original Gemma models.

## Detailed answer

The original Transformer used ReLU. Names such as SiLU in a configuration describe a pointwise activation; the surrounding multiplication and projections determine whether the full block is SwiGLU. Inspect a specific version rather than assuming all models in a family share the same FFN.

The useful distinction is between a pointwise activation and a complete FFN design. BERT's ordinary FFN projects up, applies GELU, and projects down. A SwiGLU FFN performs two input projections, applies SiLU to one, multiplies them, then projects down. Those are different computation graphs even if a configuration exposes just one activation string.

When loading a checkpoint, verify the number of projection matrices and their shapes. Two matrices generally indicate an ordinary FFN, while a gate/value/output layout indicates a gated one. Then inspect the exact activation convention, including GELU approximation or any scaling. Checkpoint tensor names can help, but implementation is the final authority.

This matters for both correctness and comparisons. Replacing GELU with SiLU in a pretrained ordinary FFN does not turn it into a pretrained SwiGLU network, and matching hidden width across gated and ungated blocks does not match parameter count. The model examples above refer to their published versions. Evaluate alternative activations through controlled training or adaptation rather than assuming a fashionable activation is a drop-in quality improvement.
