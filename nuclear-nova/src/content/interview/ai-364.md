## Question

Explain strategies to combat overfitting in tree-based classification models.

## Brief answer

Limit tree complexity and validate against deployment-like data. Depth, leaf count, minimum samples per leaf, feature subsampling, and ensemble size control different forms of overfitting. For boosted trees, learning rate and early stopping are especially important; for forests, decorrelating trees and increasing leaf support can improve generalization.

## Detailed answer

Begin by checking leakage, duplicated entities across splits, and labels derived from future information. A perfectly fitted deep tree may simply memorize IDs or rare combinations. Use temporal or grouped validation when the data requires it, and tune complexity on held-out performance rather than training accuracy.

For random forests, compare maximum depth and minimum leaf size, use feature subsampling, and inspect out-of-bag estimates only when their assumptions fit the sampling process. For boosting, tune learning rate jointly with number of rounds and stop on a valid holdout. Class weighting affects imbalance, not necessarily overfitting. Evaluate calibration and important subgroups, and prefer the simplest model within the acceptable performance range when latency or interpretability matters.

### Control how finely the model partitions data

A tree with a leaf for every training example can reproduce labels, including noise. Increasing minimum leaf size requires each decision region to be supported by more observations; limiting depth caps interaction complexity; pruning removes splits whose validation benefit does not justify their cost. These controls directly reduce the tree's ability to memorize rare patterns.

For a forest, averaging many decorrelated trees reduces variance, but adding trees does not undo leakage or biased labels. For boosting, a small learning rate spreads fitting across more rounds; it needs a suitable stopping rule because enough rounds can still overfit. Use validation curves for depth, leaf size, and rounds rather than changing all controls simultaneously. An illustrative warning sign is training accuracy rising to 100% while temporal validation stalls or declines. Inspect whether a customer ID or post-outcome feature creates shortcuts before merely shrinking the model. Report performance on future periods and rare classes, not only a random split.
