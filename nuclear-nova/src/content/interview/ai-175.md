## Question

Design a system to schedule jobs in a distributed environment.

## Brief answer

Store jobs durably, assign due jobs through leases, and execute them with idempotent task semantics. Separate scheduling time from execution ownership. A worker may die after completing an external effect but before acknowledging the job, so retries must tolerate duplicates; a distributed scheduler alone cannot guarantee exactly-once side effects.

## Detailed answer

Suggested components: job API, durable job table, due-time index, dispatch queue and workers. Claim a job with a compare-and-swap on state and lease version. Workers heartbeat during long tasks; expired leases permit reassignment. Fencing tokens prevent an old worker from writing after a newer owner takes over when the destination supports fencing.

Define cron timezone and daylight-saving behavior, missed-run policy, retry budget and fixed-rate versus fixed-delay recurrence. Use server time for scheduling decisions and retain execution history separately from the recurring job definition. Partition due-time scans to avoid one global hot index.

Test clock skew, duplicate dispatch, lease expiry during a slow task, scheduler restart and cancellation races. Monitor schedule lag, queue age, execution latency and retry volume. Backpressure should be visible; silently skipping late jobs is a policy decision, not an implementation detail.

A lease gives a worker temporary ownership, but it is not proof that the old worker stopped. If a pause exceeds the lease timeout, another worker may claim the job while the original resumes. A monotonically increasing fencing token lets a cooperating destination reject writes from an obsolete owner. Without that support, idempotency or reconciliation must handle duplicate execution.

Recurring schedules should generate stable execution IDs distinct from the schedule definition. This allows retries of Tuesday's run without accidentally creating a new Wednesday run. Define behavior for missed intervals, daylight-saving changes and schedule edits while a job is running. Server time and a consistent due-time policy reduce clock ambiguity.

Partition by tenant or time bucket to scale scanning, but account for hot schedules and fairness. A durable queue buffers work; it does not create capacity. Monitor schedule lag separately from execution duration and surface overload through quotas or explicit missed-run policy. Test cancellation after dispatch, heartbeat loss, slow workers and recovery from a scheduler restart with no duplicate untracked effects.
