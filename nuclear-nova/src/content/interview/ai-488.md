## Question

How would you evaluate an autonomous software engineering agent? Explain why SWE-bench pass rates mislead.

## Brief answer

Use repository-level tasks with hidden checks, controlled environments, repeated runs, and human review of the final diff. Report success together with cost, latency, unwanted changes, and reliability across task types. A benchmark pass rate alone can hide contamination, harness advantages, incomplete tests, and expensive retry policies.

## Detailed answer

SWE-bench-style tasks are useful reproducible samples of issue resolution, but they cover only selected repositories and testable outcomes. A patch can satisfy tests while violating maintainability, security, or the intended scope. Conversely, an environment failure can obscure a correct patch. Record those categories separately rather than silently changing the denominator.

Compare agents under equal tool access, runtime, attempts, and token budgets. Hold out newer repositories and tasks, and inspect patches for hard-coded answers or test manipulation. Add long-running tasks, ambiguous specifications, dependency changes, and required human escalation. In pilots, measure engineer review time, acceptance after review, downstream regressions, and intervention frequency. Preserve failed traces so improvements target mechanisms rather than merely optimizing an aggregate leaderboard score.

### Interpret benchmark success correctly

If an agent receives ten attempts and the best patch is selected using the test result, its reported task success is different from a single unattended attempt. Even under an oversimplified independence assumption, a 20% per-attempt success probability becomes about 89.3% chance of at least one success over ten attempts. Real attempts are correlated, but the example shows why retry and selection budgets must accompany a pass rate.

Classify failures into environment setup, incomplete implementation, incorrect behavior, scope violation, and evaluation ambiguity. This allows a harness improvement to be credited accurately without implying the model suddenly reasons better. Inspect successful patches too: hidden tests may not cover a security regression or an unnecessarily broad rewrite.

Before broader deployment, evaluate tasks sampled from the intended organization's repositories and workflows, including documentation, migrations, and work that needs clarification. Measure the combined engineer and agent effort through accepted delivery, not just time until a patch is emitted. Benchmark evidence is useful when its limits and operating conditions remain attached to the result.
