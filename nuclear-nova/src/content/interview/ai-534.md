## Question

When is intervening during decoding better than filtering the finished reply?

## Brief answer

Intervene during decoding when unsafe output must be prevented before it is displayed, when structured constraints can be enforced incrementally, or when streaming leaves no opportunity to retract. Finished-response filtering is simpler for whole-answer context but cannot undo content already spoken or shown. Choose the intervention point by the harm and the available signal.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Use grammar constraints for valid schemas and token-level restrictions only where they express a precise rule. For semantic risks, inspect buffered clauses or sentences and hold them until a classifier or verifier clears them. Keep a safe fallback for interrupted generation. Avoid assuming a banned-word list understands context or catches paraphrases.

Measure detection delay, false positives, latency, and quality loss. Early classifiers may lack enough context and can suppress benign quotations; final checks can assess meaning more accurately but require buffering. A hybrid approach uses deterministic decoding constraints plus segment verification and a final consistency pass. Test boundary-spanning phrases, multilingual content, evasive encoding, and a streaming client that disconnects before final validation.

### Choose the earliest reliable signal

For a JSON-only tool response, constrained decoding can prevent invalid syntax as tokens are generated. It cannot establish that the account ID belongs to the user or that the amount is permitted; those require validation against trusted state. For a semantic claim, a few initial tokens may be too ambiguous to classify, so buffering a complete clause provides a stronger decision point.

In a voice interface, words already played cannot be recalled. Segment-level checking before TTS may therefore be worth added latency. In a non-streaming draft editor, checking the finished response can use full context and avoid premature blocking. The intervention should follow the delivery contract rather than a blanket preference for earlier filtering.

Test content whose meaning changes at a boundary, such as a quotation followed by a rejection, and phrases split across network chunks. A network chunk is not necessarily a linguistic unit. Define what happens when a segment is blocked: discard unplayed audio, stop obsolete generation, and provide a coherent alternative. Measure false positives, missed violations, and added delay together so the chosen policy improves actual behavior rather than only a classifier score.
