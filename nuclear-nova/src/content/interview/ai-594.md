## Question

Design a system that lets multiple teams query a shared dataset without exposing raw data.

## Brief answer

Expose governed views or query services that enforce row, column, and purpose-based access, returning only approved results. If raw data must never be visible, arbitrary SQL over small groups may still leak it through aggregation. Define the threat model and use query restrictions, minimum group sizes, or differential privacy where appropriate.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Keep source data in a controlled environment and execute authenticated queries under per-team policies. Provide a semantic layer with approved metrics and joins, audit query history, and restrict exports. For repeated statistical queries, evaluate differencing attacks and privacy-budget accounting rather than relying on a single suppression threshold.

Separate operational authorization from statistical privacy: a masked view can still reveal sensitive correlations. Test joins, small cohorts, repeated queries, cached results, and permission revocation. Measure usefulness, latency, and leakage risk with the data owners. The correct mechanism depends on whether teams need exact authorized aggregates, exploratory analysis, or privacy-preserving population statistics; make that contract explicit before implementation.

### Aggregates can reveal individuals

If a team queries the total salary of a ten-person group and then the same group excluding one person, subtracting the two exact answers reveals that person's salary. A minimum group size on each query does not prevent this differencing attack. Define whether teams are trusted for exact authorized aggregates or require statistical privacy against adaptive analysis.

For governed exact analysis, expose approved metrics and joins, enforce row/column policies inside the execution service, and review exports and cached results under current permissions. For population statistics needing stronger privacy, constrain query families and use a calibrated differential-privacy mechanism with a privacy budget and utility evaluation. Noise must be accounted for across repeated queries; adding arbitrary independent noise without composition control is not a complete design. Keep the raw data in a controlled environment and audit accesses. Test overlapping cohorts, joins that reidentify masked data, permission revocation, and cached cross-team results. Choose the mechanism with data owners based on the actual threat and analytical need.
