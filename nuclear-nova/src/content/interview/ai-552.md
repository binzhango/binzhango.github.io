## Question

Clinicians will not sign what they cannot verify. How would you build span-level provenance from every line of the note back to the conversation?

## Brief answer

Carry stable audio timestamps and transcript span IDs through extraction, synthesis, and note editing, and link each factual note statement to its supporting evidence. Distinguish direct conversation evidence, chart-derived facts, and clinician additions. Provenance explains where a statement came from; it does not by itself prove the statement is correct.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Maintain versioned ASR segments with speaker and confidence, then structured facts referencing one or more segments. Generate note sections from those facts and store claim-to-source mappings. The reviewer can select a note line to inspect transcript context or a short audio clip. When edits change meaning, recompute or invalidate the old mapping rather than retaining a misleading link.

Handle merged claims, corrected speech, overlapping speakers, and facts requiring several turns. Keep access and retention rules consistent across notes, transcripts, and audio. Evaluate span precision, evidence coverage, and time to verify critical facts. FHIR Provenance can represent resource-level origin and activity; fine-grained audio-span links still need an application-level representation and workflow.

### Make provenance survive revisions

Assign immutable IDs to audio segments and transcript versions. A fact record should point to the versioned text span and its audio interval, with speaker and source type. When ASR revises a phrase, update or invalidate dependent facts instead of leaving offsets attached to different words. Character positions without a transcript version are not stable evidence.

For example, a provisional transcript says “takes fifteen,” then resolves to “takes fifty.” The associated dose candidate must be re-evaluated, and a note generated from the earlier candidate must be marked stale until reconciled. Preserve the history for debugging, while displaying the current evidence to the authorized reviewer.

Generated prose can combine several supported facts into one sentence, so allow one-to-many evidence links. Conversely, one utterance may support several facts. Chart-derived facts should point to the relevant resource and version rather than pretending to originate in an audio span. A FHIR provenance record and a transcript-span mapping serve related but different purposes.

In the review interface, selecting a claim should reveal the supporting text and, where permitted, the corresponding audio interval. Test the links after transcript corrections, note edits, and partial regeneration. Provenance is useful only if it remains accurate through the actual editing lifecycle; decorative evidence links that lead to stale or unrelated content can increase false confidence.
