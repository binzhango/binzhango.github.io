## Question

Design a customer-support agent that can take real actions, with escalation to humans.

## Brief answer

Use a support workflow that separates knowledge retrieval from authenticated business actions, with explicit escalation and an auditable action ledger. Give the agent narrow tools rather than unrestricted access to internal systems.

## Detailed answer

Resolve customer identity, retrieve applicable policy and collect required fields before proposing a refund or account change. Validate eligibility and authorization in deterministic services, use idempotency keys and confirm material consequences where required. Escalate ambiguous policy, disputed identity or repeated failures with a concise evidence packet for the human. Measure correct resolution, duplicate actions, customer effort and recovery, not just reduced human contacts.

Model a support case as a workflow with authenticated identity, issue classification, evidence, proposed resolution and execution status. Read-only retrieval supplies current policy and order information. Deterministic services decide eligibility and enforce limits; the model explains options and gathers missing information. Keep refunds, address changes and account closure as distinct narrow tools.

For a refund, verify the customer owns the order, calculate the allowed amount from authoritative records, present any required confirmation and execute with an idempotency key. If the provider times out after accepting the operation, reconcile its status before retrying. The assistant must not say “refunded” merely because it issued a request.

Escalation should transfer a concise packet: the customer's goal, verified identity context, relevant policy, attempted steps, confirmed effects and unresolved questions. This prevents the human from asking the customer to repeat everything and avoids duplicate actions. Trigger escalation for ambiguous policy, identity disputes, high-impact exceptions and repeated tool failures. Measure correct resolution, repeat contacts, customer effort, false completion claims and harmful actions. Reducing human involvement is useful only when the system preserves service quality and recognizes the cases it cannot safely resolve.
