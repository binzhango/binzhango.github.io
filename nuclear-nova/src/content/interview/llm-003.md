## Question

What are some typical problems addressed with unsupervised learning methods?

## Brief answer

Unsupervised learning commonly addresses clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation, and anomaly discovery without task labels.

## Detailed answer

Clustering can reveal customer segments, while principal component analysis compresses correlated measurements. Anomaly detection can flag unusual observations, but unusual does not automatically mean fraudulent or defective. Association-rule mining discovers co-occurring items. Because there are no target labels, validation often combines structural metrics, stability checks, and usefulness on a downstream task.

The absence of labels changes both the objective and the interpretation. K-means minimizes within-cluster squared distances, so it prefers roughly compact groups in the chosen feature space. If customer income is measured in dollars and visit count in single digits, unscaled income may dominate the result. Standardization and feature selection therefore express assumptions about what similarity should mean.

PCA instead finds orthogonal directions that retain the most variance. Compressing 100 correlated sensor measurements into 10 components can reduce storage and noise, but a low-variance direction might still be essential for detecting a rare fault. An anomaly detector asks whether a measurement is unusual relative to a reference distribution; it cannot conclude the cause without further evidence.

Validate by rerunning on different samples, checking whether groups remain stable, and testing a downstream use such as routing support requests. A pleasing cluster visualization is insufficient: the structure may reflect language, document length, or recording equipment rather than the phenomenon the application cares about.
