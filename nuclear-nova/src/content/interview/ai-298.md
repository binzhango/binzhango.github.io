## Question

Qwen ships both dense and MoE models (30B with ~3B active; 235B with ~22B active). When would you pick the 30B-A3B MoE over a 32B dense?

## Brief answer

Choose the 30B-A3B MoE when its task quality is adequate, all expert weights fit the deployment, and sparse kernels deliver better measured cost or latency. Choose the 32B dense model when simpler serving, predictable utilization, or stronger target-task quality wins. Three billion active parameters does not mean three billion parameters of storage.

## Detailed answer

At a simplified two bytes per weight, 30B weights need about 60 GB before cache and overhead; the active subset changes computation rather than this base storage requirement. Quantization changes the memory estimate and must be evaluated for quality.

Benchmark actual batch sizes, prompt lengths, and output lengths. Sparse routing can create small expert GEMMs and communication overhead, especially across machines or at low concurrency. Dense models often benefit from mature kernels and simpler placement. Compare dollars per correctly completed task, p95 latency, cache capacity, and operational complexity. The named sizes refer to the original Qwen3 family, whose release materials distinguish total from activated parameters.

### Two deployment scenarios

A throughput-oriented service with enough memory to keep all experts resident may benefit from the smaller active computation of the MoE. Continuous batches give each expert enough tokens to execute efficient matrix operations. A single-user deployment with little fast memory may prefer a dense model whose compute is higher but whose placement and kernels are simpler and predictable.

Measure memory after loading weights and reserve space for the largest supported live contexts. Then sweep concurrency while enforcing the same first-token and inter-token SLOs. If the MoE needs multiple devices and crosses slow links for every layer, routing communication may cancel its arithmetic advantage. Quality can also differ by language, tool use, and quantization sensitivity even when headline size looks comparable. Include failure recovery and replica cost in the comparison: the correct choice minimizes cost for the required quality and availability, not active parameters in isolation.
