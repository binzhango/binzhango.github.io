## Question

R1-Zero was trained with RL and essentially no SFT first. What did that show, and why did full R1 add SFT back?

## Brief answer

R1-Zero showed that outcome-driven RL on an already pretrained base model could induce stronger reasoning behavior without an initial SFT stage. It did not show that pretraining, reward design or all supervised information was unnecessary.

## Detailed answer

The DeepSeek-R1 paper reports readability and language-mixing problems in R1-Zero. R1 introduces cold-start examples and later supervised stages within a broader RL pipeline to improve usability and general behavior. Separate the experimental observation from a universal recipe: success depends on the base model, verifiable tasks, reward implementation and optimization setup. A reproduction should track held-out correctness, formatting, language consistency and resource cost, including verifier exploits. SFT is valuable when it supplies behavior the reward does not reliably teach; RL is valuable when exploration against outcomes improves beyond fixed demonstrations.

### Outcome reward can discover behavior that demonstrations omit

Start from an already pretrained model that has language and problem-solving foundations. Sample solutions to verifiable tasks, score outcomes, and update the policy toward successful trajectories. This can reinforce useful reasoning patterns without first supplying a supervised corpus of the desired reasoning style. It does not create competence from an untrained network or eliminate the need for careful task and reward design.

The reward may strongly favor correct final answers while weakly supervising readability, language consistency, and general assistant behavior. Cold-start SFT provides a useful behavioral format, and later supervised stages can consolidate successful solutions and broaden coverage. RL then explores improvements against outcomes. Evaluate each stage on independent correctness, formatting, language, and non-reasoning tasks rather than assuming one benchmark captures usability. Watch verifier exploits and response-length growth. The lesson is that supervision and exploration solve complementary problems; R1-Zero is evidence about one training path, not proof that SFT is unnecessary for every assistant or that more RL automatically improves all behavior.
