## Question

Explain post-training quantization versus quantization-aware training. What breaks when you push weights to 2–4 bits, and how do you recover quality?

## Brief answer

Post-training quantization converts an already trained model, often using calibration data to choose scales and preserve important weights. Quantization-aware training exposes the model to simulated or actual quantization during optimization so it can adapt. At two to four bits, outliers, limited representational levels, and unsupported kernels can damage quality or erase expected speedups.

## Detailed answer

Separate weight-only from weight-and-activation quantization and state the group size, scale precision, and excluded layers. Two four-bit formats can have different overhead and error. Diagnose quality by layer and task, especially long-context reasoning, rare tokens, and structured outputs.

Recover with representative calibration, per-channel or grouped scales, selective higher precision, clipping tuned to validation data, or QAT/adapters that compensate for quantization error. Distillation can help but does not guarantee recovery. Measure on-device latency and energy because dequantization overhead may dominate on unsupported hardware. Compare against an unquantized baseline using the same prompts and decoding.

### Quantization introduces a reconstruction error

A simple uniform quantizer maps a value to an integer using a scale and zero point, then reconstructs an approximation. With fewer levels, large outliers force a wider step size unless clipping or per-group scales are used, reducing resolution for common small values. Errors accumulate differently across layers and can disproportionately affect attention, normalization, or rare output choices.

PTQ chooses these parameters after training, often using representative calibration activations. QAT simulates quantization during training so weights adapt to the expected error; the deployed arithmetic still needs matching kernels. Weight-only four-bit quantization leaves activations at a higher precision and has different memory and speed behavior from four-bit activations too. Test calibration-domain mismatch: a model calibrated on short English prose may fail on long multilingual code prompts. Use selective precision and compare quality after each change. A low-bit file format is not automatically an efficient executable model on a particular phone backend.
