## Question

Design semantic search over a large product catalogue.

## Brief answer

Use hybrid candidate retrieval over normalized product attributes and text, then rerank against intent, availability and business constraints. Semantic similarity alone can return a related item with the wrong size, compatibility or price.

## Detailed answer

Ingest catalog revisions, variants, inventory and aliases with stable product IDs. Parse hard constraints separately from soft preferences; apply inventory and compatibility filters before final ranking. Train or tune ranking on judged relevance and debiased interaction data. Evaluate exact identifier queries, paraphrases and tail products, including zero-result behavior. Keep sponsored placement explicit and monitor whether gains reflect genuine relevance rather than position bias.

Separate hard constraints from ranking preferences. For “waterproof hiking shoes under $120, size 10,” price, size availability and perhaps waterproof certification may be mandatory; visual similarity or popularity is secondary. A dense match that violates a required size or device compatibility is not a good result, even if its description is semantically close.

Ingest product families and variants separately, normalizing units, brands, categories and aliases. Run lexical retrieval for identifiers and exact attributes alongside semantic retrieval for descriptive intent. Apply current inventory and hard filters, deduplicate variants where useful, then rerank using relevance, quality and authorized business rules. Keep sponsored placement distinguishable from organic relevance.

Use judged query-product pairs and interaction data corrected for exposure bias. Popular items receive more clicks partly because they are shown more often, so raw click counts can reinforce existing rankings. Evaluate tail products, typos, multilingual queries, zero-result cases and newly added items. For freshness, inventory and price updates may need a faster path than full embedding recomputation. Measure relevant-result recall, constraint violation rate, conversion quality and latency, while checking that revenue gains do not come from misleading substitutions or suppressing useful low-volume products.
