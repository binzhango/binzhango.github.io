## Question

A partner reports that Harvey missed a change-of-control clause in a contract it reviewed. Debug it.

## Brief answer

Trace the missed clause through source ingestion, extraction, segmentation, retrieval, interpretation, and output assembly. Preserve the exact document version and checklist run. A missing answer may originate from OCR or pagination, a synonym mismatch, an exception or amendment, or a model that saw the clause but misclassified it.

## Detailed answer

First confirm the reported clause and expected interpretation with the reviewer. Inspect extracted text and page coordinates, then replay retrieval with the original query and index. Check whether the clause was split, truncated, ranked out, or hidden in a schedule. If it reached the prompt, compare structured output and validation logic; an “absent” default may have overwritten an uncertain result.

Fix the responsible stage and add a regression example plus nearby variants, including indirect wording and amended provisions. Audit similar documents for the same failure pattern and communicate affected scope. Measure clause-level recall on an independently labeled sample before reprocessing. Do not solve one incident by blindly making every document answer “present,” which would merely replace false negatives with review burden.

### Use an evidence ladder to locate the failure

Ask whether the clause exists in the original source, whether it appears correctly in extracted text, whether the relevant chunk exists in the index, whether it was retrieved, whether it entered the exact model prompt, and whether the structured result preserved the model's finding. Stop at the first broken boundary. This avoids changing prompts to compensate for a parser that never extracted the page.

For a concrete failure, an appendix might describe termination after a transfer of majority ownership without using the phrase “change of control.” An exact-keyword query can miss it. Add concept-based retrieval and examples of indirect wording, but validate against unrelated ownership mentions to avoid turning every such passage into a positive finding.

If retrieval succeeded, test the model with only the complete evidence bundle and the checklist definition. Then reintroduce surrounding context to determine whether truncation or distraction caused the miss. Reprocess the affected slice identified by the root cause and track reviewer-confirmed outcomes. Preserve an unresolved state when extraction or interpretation remains uncertain instead of automatically reporting the clause absent.
