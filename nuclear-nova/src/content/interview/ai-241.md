## Question

You're training on tens of thousands of GPUs and hardware fails constantly. How do you keep goodput high?

## Brief answer

Optimize useful training progress per wall-clock time, including failures and recovery, rather than peak device utilization. Combine reliable checkpoints, fast failure detection, deterministic data progress and topology-aware replacement with enough spare capacity to recover promptly.

## Detailed answer

Measure failure frequency and checkpoint/restart cost before selecting intervals: overly frequent saves waste I/O, while infrequent saves repeat substantial compute. Asynchronous checkpointing still needs a consistent state boundary and integrity verification. Record optimizer, scheduler, RNG and data-loader state, and test restoration routinely. Use health checks to quarantine faulty accelerators or links, and distinguish transient network errors from repeatable bad batches. Sharded checkpoints need a manifest and atomic completion marker so partial writes cannot be mistaken for recoverable state. Evaluate elastic resizing against reproducibility and optimizer assumptions. Track recovered tokens and lost-step time, not just successful process restarts.

### Balance saving against repeated work

Let checkpoint cost be C seconds and average failure interval be M seconds for the whole job. Under a simplified periodic model, checkpoint overhead is roughly C/I and expected lost work is I/(2M) for interval I. Minimizing their sum gives $I\approx\sqrt{2CM}$. With an illustrative 30-second checkpoint and one-hour job-level failure interval, the estimate is about 465 seconds. Real asynchronous saves, correlated failures, and recovery cost require measurement rather than blind use of the formula.

At large scale, a job can fail often even if each device is individually reliable. Save a coherent optimizer-step boundary with all shards and data progress, verify completeness, then publish the checkpoint manifest. Test recovery onto spare hardware and confirm the next batch and schedule are valid. Use health telemetry to quarantine repeated bad devices or links instead of repeatedly restarting on them. Goodput counts useful accepted training progress after replay and recovery, so busy GPUs rewriting lost steps do not constitute success.
