## Question

What are the benefits of instruction fine-tuning?

## Brief answer

Instruction fine-tuning teaches a pretrained model how to respond to requests in the desired format, style, and task setting.

## Detailed answer

It can improve instruction following, multi-turn behavior, tool-call structure, and use of supplied context. It can also teach task-specific information, but success depends on demonstration quality and coverage. SFT imitates target responses; it does not inherently verify their truth or optimize every downstream preference. Evaluate generalization to new instructions and check for regressions outside the tuning distribution.

Pretraining teaches continuation of the text distribution; an instruction dataset concentrates probability on responses that satisfy explicit requests. For example, a base model might continue a document containing “Summarize this report” by inventing another instruction. SFT demonstrations instead show the requested summary, its expected length, and how to handle missing evidence. The same next-token machinery learns a more useful conditional behavior.

Benefits can include consistent output schemas, clearer response style, multi-turn coordination, and appropriate tool-call formats. Coverage matters: a dataset containing only successful tool results may not teach the model how to respond to timeouts or empty results. Include the decision boundaries where behavior should change, not only polished ideal answers.

SFT also inherits the targets' weaknesses. It can teach unsupported confidence or an overly rigid template just as effectively as a correct procedure. Evaluate unseen instructions and paraphrases, distinguish format compliance from factual correctness, and compare retained capabilities before and after tuning. A lower training loss shows imitation of demonstrations; it does not independently establish improved usefulness or reliable performance outside their distribution.
