## Question

Design an application to catalog and log species while exploring an unfamiliar environment.

## Brief answer

Build an offline-first field log with timestamped observations, optional location, photos/audio, taxonomy suggestions, uncertainty, and later expert review. Separate an observed organism from a confirmed species identification. Protect sensitive locations and avoid encouraging collection or handling of unfamiliar organisms merely to improve a model prediction.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Store observations locally with stable IDs and sync resumably when connectivity returns. Capture habitat and evidence quality, and let users choose coarse or private coordinates. A recognition model proposes ranked candidates with distinguishing features; users can record “unknown” and request review. Version taxonomy and retain the original label when names change.

Support duplicate detection without merging distinct sightings, and maintain conflict resolution for collaborative edits. Test offline capture, interrupted uploads, low-quality images, unfamiliar species, and GPS errors. Evaluate identification accuracy on region-held-out data, uncertainty calibration, and field usability. Sensitive-species locations should have restricted sharing, and the product should distinguish educational identification from safety guidance about edibility or contact.

### Keep observations separate from identifications

An observation record contains a stable ID, timestamp, optional coordinates, habitat notes, and media hashes. Identification is a separate versioned claim with candidate species, confidence, evidence, and reviewer identity. An expert can revise the species without losing the original field observation or falsely creating another sighting.

The device stores records and media locally, then syncs through a durable upload queue with resumable chunks and idempotent IDs. Collaborative edits need field-level conflict handling rather than replacing an entire record with the last upload. A recognition model can suggest candidates and distinguishing features, but an unfamiliar region may contain species absent from training; “unknown” must remain a first-class result. Evaluate on held-out regions and low-quality images, and make taxonomy updates traceable. Restrict precise locations for sensitive species and allow coarse/private coordinates. Optimize the capture flow for gloves, sunlight, weak connectivity, and battery use; a sophisticated classifier is of little value if observations are lost offline.
