## Question

Tell me about a time you had a conflict with someone. How did you resolve it and what did you learn?

## Brief answer

Choose a substantive disagreement and explain how you separated the underlying interests from the interpersonal tension. Show listening, evidence gathering and a concrete resolution or escalation. A successful answer need not end with everyone agreeing; it should show a fair process and a working relationship after the decision.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: describe the shared goal and the conflicting views without caricaturing the other person. Explain what you initially misunderstood, what you learned from their perspective and how you tested competing assumptions. If you used a prototype, customer evidence or an explicit decision owner, describe why it helped.

State the decision, who owned it and how you supported execution. If the conflict remained unresolved, explain the escalation and how you avoided undermining the team. Include a specific lesson about communication or decision-making.

Do not disclose private personal details or claim the other person was simply irrational. The interviewer is assessing whether you can disagree constructively under real constraints, including when your preferred option is not chosen.

An illustrative disagreement might involve one engineer favoring a rewrite and another favoring a targeted fix. Both can share the goal of reliability while weighing short-term delivery and long-term maintenance differently. A useful resolution is a bounded experiment or phased plan with evidence about defect rate and change cost, rather than deciding who argued most confidently.

For a real story, explain what you did to understand the other person's constraint and what changed in your own view. If a decision owner chose an option you opposed, describe how you supported execution while preserving an appropriate checkpoint for reassessment. Constructive disagreement does not require unanimous enthusiasm.

Include the outcome and a specific communication lesson, such as surfacing assumptions before debating solutions or separating a technical concern from frustration about timing. Avoid diagnosing the other person's personality. The strongest answer shows that you can advocate clearly, listen accurately and maintain a workable relationship after a difficult decision.
