## Question

How do you handle PII in prompts, logs and training data?

## Brief answer

Minimize personal data before it enters prompts, logs or training corpora, and preserve only what the task requires. Apply access controls, retention/deletion rules and provenance consistently across copies and derived datasets.

## Detailed answer

Replace identifiers with scoped surrogates where possible and keep reidentification mappings separately protected. Redaction must handle indirect identifiers and sensitive free text, not just regex matches. Avoid using production traces for training by default; establish an authorized data pipeline and deletion propagation. Log structured error metadata instead of full payloads when sufficient. Validate controls with representative leakage tests and involve the appropriate privacy/security owners for deployment requirements.

Map every copy of the data: original records, assembled prompts, provider requests, tracing systems, evaluation exports, vector indexes and training datasets. Removing a name from the visible response does not remove it from a trace or an embedding corpus. Define which fields are necessary for each stage and avoid transporting the rest.

For a support workflow, replace an account number with a scoped surrogate before generation when the model only needs to discuss the case. Keep the surrogate-to-account mapping in a protected service that executes authorized actions. Avoid global deterministic replacements when they would allow unrelated datasets to be linked. Free text can contain addresses, health details or combinations of indirect identifiers, so regex-only redaction is incomplete.

Set retention and deletion procedures for derived copies, with access logging and encryption appropriate to the deployment. Do not automatically reuse production conversations for training or evaluation; establish provenance and authorization for that separate purpose. Test redaction on realistic messy text and measure both missed identifiers and destruction of information needed for the task. For requirements that depend on jurisdiction, contract or organizational policy, obtain the applicable privacy review rather than treating a generic technical architecture as a legal compliance conclusion.
