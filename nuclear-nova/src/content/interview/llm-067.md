## Question

How do you handle large loss differences in multi-task learning?

## Brief answer

Normalize losses sensibly, then balance task sampling and gradient contributions rather than comparing raw loss magnitudes alone.

## Detailed answer

Use a weighted objective $L=\sum_i\lambda_iL_i$ and make each task's reduction explicit. One task may sum over thousands of tokens while another averages over examples, producing misleading scale differences. Inspect gradient norms and per-task validation metrics. Fixed weights are a useful baseline; adaptive methods such as uncertainty weighting or GradNorm can help, but require their own validation. Conflicting gradient directions are a separate issue from unequal scales.

First make units comparable. A reconstruction loss summed over 10,000 pixels can be thousands of times larger than an averaged classification loss without being more important. Convert each to a documented per-element or per-example reduction, then choose task weights according to desired behavior. A numerically small loss can still create a large gradient if its derivative is steep.

Suppose a shared encoder supports classification and regression. Log validation metrics for both tasks and inspect gradient norms on the shared parameters. If regression dominates the update, reduce its sampling rate or weight and test whether classification recovers without unacceptable regression loss. If gradients point in opposing directions, rescaling alone may not resolve the conflict; separate heads, partial parameter sharing, or gradient-conflict methods may be appropriate.

Sampling and weighting interact. Training classification on 90% of steps and regression on 10% already changes their effective influence before loss coefficients are applied. Use a fixed-weight baseline, then introduce adaptive weighting only if it improves the task tradeoff. Preserve a joint acceptance criterion so improving the aggregate loss cannot conceal failure of a rare but required task.
