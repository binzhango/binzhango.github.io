## Question

Why is RAG the right architecture for an enterprise assistant instead of fine-tuning on the company's data? Where does RAG break?

## Brief answer

RAG retrieves current, permissioned evidence at query time and gives users inspectable sources. Fine-tuning is better suited to behavior, format, or domain task patterns than to storing frequently changing confidential facts. RAG still fails when extraction, retrieval, permissions, freshness, or evidence synthesis fail; it is an architecture to evaluate, not an accuracy guarantee.

## Detailed answer

A company can revoke access or update a policy in an index without retraining the model. The answer can cite a specific document version and distinguish missing evidence from model knowledge. Fine-tuning on internal documents does not reliably preserve exact facts, provide citations, or implement per-user authorization.

RAG breaks on cross-document joins, exhaustive counting, poorly extracted tables, ambiguous terminology, and questions requiring evidence outside the retrieved set. Use structured queries for aggregates, dependency expansion for linked concepts, and carefully scoped full-context reading when appropriate. Measure retrieval recall and answer support separately. A hybrid system may fine-tune the assistant's tool use or style while retrieving enterprise facts; that separation keeps updates and security controls tractable.

### Separate knowledge access from learned behavior

Suppose a travel policy changes today and applies only to employees in one region. Retrieval can select the current policy under the user's permissions and effective date. Fine-tuning cannot reliably remove the previous policy from the model, identify which learned fact came from which version, or restrict that fact to one employee's access. The model's weights are therefore a poor authorization and record-versioning mechanism.

Fine-tuning can still teach how to ask clarification questions, call an approved expense tool, or format the final result. That behavioral learning complements retrieval of the current policy. Keep evaluation separate: one suite checks tool and interaction behavior, while another checks evidence freshness, retrieval completeness, and supported conclusions.

For a question such as “How many open orders are blocked?”, retrieval of several similar documents is not an exhaustive query over records. Route to a structured data service with a defined counting contract instead. RAG is useful when the answer depends on identifiable evidence, but its success depends on finding all necessary evidence and reasoning correctly over it. Missing retrieval, wrong ACLs, or corrupted extraction remain first-class failure modes.
