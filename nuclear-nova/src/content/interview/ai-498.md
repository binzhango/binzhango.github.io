## Question

After a foundation-model version upgrade, your production agent's escalation rate doubles overnight. Walk me through your response.

## Brief answer

Treat the doubled escalation rate as a release regression until evidence shows a beneficial change in appropriate escalation. Freeze rollout, compare matched traffic, and restore the prior model if customer impact warrants it. Preserve model, prompt, tool, and routing versions so the change can be reproduced.

## Detailed answer

Break escalation down by reason, intent, customer segment, and stage. The new model may interpret policy more strictly, fail a tool schema, lose context, or be genuinely better at recognizing uncertainty. Inspect resolved cases and missed escalations as well as the headline rate; reducing escalation blindly can worsen unsafe automation.

Replay a fixed trace set against both versions with identical tools and decoding budgets. Check structured-output compatibility, refusal behavior, token truncation, and prompt assumptions. Fix the responsible layer and rerun category-specific tests before a small canary. Monitor resolution quality, unauthorized actions, human workload, and latency jointly. Communicate the verified impact and mitigation to operators, and record the new failure mode in the release gate.

### Distinguish prudent escalation from broken automation

Sample cases escalated only by the new model and have reviewers label whether escalation was required, unnecessary, or uncertain. A model that now recognizes previously missed authorization boundaries can increase human workload while improving correctness. A model that emits the wrong tool schema can produce the same headline increase with no quality benefit.

Cross over the components: replay the same conversation and tool observations under both models using the same prompt, then test any revised prompt separately. Compare structured requests before execution, not just final prose. Look for a specific transition, such as treating a successful tool result as an error or forgetting the user's approval after context truncation.

Contain customer impact using version-pinned routing and a rollback switch. Preserve cases that expose both unnecessary and missed escalations in the regression suite. When restoring automation, require verified resolution and policy compliance rather than simply returning the escalation percentage to its old value. Report the staffing impact too, since a small percentage change at high volume can exceed the human team's capacity.
