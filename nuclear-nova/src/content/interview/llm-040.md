## Question

What are the differences between Adam and SGD optimizers?

## Brief answer

SGD updates parameters from gradients, optionally with momentum; Adam also estimates per-coordinate gradient scales to adapt update sizes.

## Detailed answer

Vanilla SGD uses $\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta g$. Adam maintains exponential moving averages of $g$ and $g^2$, bias-corrects them, and updates with $\eta\widehat m/(\sqrt{\widehat v}+\epsilon)$. These two moment buffers increase optimizer memory. AdamW additionally decouples weight decay and is a common Transformer training choice. Neither optimizer universally generalizes better; compare tuned schedules and equal training budgets.

Adam's first moment smooths the direction of recent gradients, while its second moment estimates their squared scale. Bias correction compensates for starting both averages at zero. Dividing by the square root of the second moment makes the step sensitive to each coordinate's history, so two parameters with different gradient scales can receive more comparable updates.

For vanilla SGD with learning rate 0.01 and gradient 2, the update is -0.02. For a first Adam step with zero-initialized moments, bias correction gives $\hat m=2$ and $\hat v=4$, so the update is approximately -0.01 when epsilon is negligible. This is a calculated illustration, not evidence that Adam's step is always smaller. Later steps depend on the entire moving-average history.

Momentum SGD usually stores one extra tensor; Adam stores two, often in higher precision. That matters for large-model memory planning. Tune learning rate, warmup, decay schedule, batch size, and weight decay for each optimizer rather than copying one configuration. Compare held-out quality and time to reach it, since a cheaper update can still need more updates overall.
