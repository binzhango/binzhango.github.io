## Question

Can SFT learn knowledge at all?

## Brief answer

Yes. SFT changes parameters and can teach new facts, but low training loss does not prove reliable knowledge acquisition.

## Detailed answer

A model may memorize the wording of demonstrations while failing on paraphrases, novel combinations, or conflicting evidence. Test on questions that isolate newly introduced information and prevent source leakage. For broad domain exposure, continued pretraining may be appropriate; for changing facts, RAG may be preferable. The slogan “SFT only teaches format” is too absolute, as is the claim that a few examples reliably install arbitrary knowledge.

To test whether SFT learned a new fact, introduce information that was not available in the base checkpoint and evaluate more than the exact training question. Suppose demonstrations teach a fictional device's calibration procedure. Test paraphrases, reordered descriptions, questions about individual steps, and cases requiring a consequence of the procedure. Keep those evaluation forms independently constructed rather than simple copies of the same template.

A model that reproduces the training answer but fails a different wording has learned a brittle association. A model that answers correctly only when the fact is supplied in the prompt may have improved contextual use without storing the fact reliably. Both are useful distinctions that training loss alone cannot reveal.

SFT examples also teach response behavior, and the two effects can interact. Overly narrow data can create confident answers for familiar-looking prompts even when information is missing. For many changing facts, retrieval offers easier update and provenance management. For stable domain concepts and procedures, adaptation can still be valuable. Evaluate the intended use of knowledge and its regressions, rather than deciding based on an absolute slogan about what SFT can or cannot learn.
