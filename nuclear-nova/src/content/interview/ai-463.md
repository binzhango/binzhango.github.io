## Question

Design the citation-verification system to reduce hallucinations in generated answers. How do you ensure every claim is actually supported by its cited source?

## Brief answer

Decompose answers into checkable claims, link each claim to exact source spans, and verify entailment and qualifications before treating the citation as support. A valid URL proves only that a source exists. The system must also check that the cited version says what the answer attributes to it.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Retrieval stores immutable passage IDs, document versions, timestamps, and offsets. Generation emits claims with those IDs; code rejects unknown IDs. A verifier checks numerical values, named entities, negation, time scope, and whether multiple premises actually support the conclusion. Use deterministic checks for structured facts and calibrated entailment models plus human audits for prose.

Rewrite, remove, or qualify unsupported claims; do not attach an unrelated citation merely to satisfy formatting. For streaming, buffer claim-sized segments until verification or explicitly avoid promising preverified content. Track unsupported-claim rate, missing-citation rate, verifier false negatives, and correction frequency. Audit primary-source quality separately from entailment: an answer can faithfully cite a false source. Test partial support, obsolete passages, contradiction, inaccessible URLs, and sources that quote misinformation while rejecting it.

### Test support at the claim level

A sentence saying “Plan A costs 20 per month and includes unlimited storage” contains at least two factual claims. A passage that supports only the price does not support the storage assertion. Split the sentence, match each claim to its exact evidence, and check scope such as region, billing period, currency, and product tier. Numerical and categorical comparisons can often be validated deterministically after extracting structured fields.

For an inferred claim, keep the premises and the inference distinct. Two source passages establishing separate prices can support a computed difference, provided the units and dates are compatible; neither passage alone states that difference. The verifier should record the operation rather than pretending the conclusion was quoted from a source.

Evaluation needs independently labeled supported, partially supported, contradicted, and unverifiable claims. Measure missed unsupported claims and unnecessary rejection of valid claims. Optimize thresholds according to application risk, and allow an explicit uncertainty outcome. No entailment classifier can guarantee every statement is true; its useful role is to reject identifiable support failures and make the remaining evidence trail inspectable.
