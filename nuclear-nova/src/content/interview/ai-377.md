## Question

How do you handle extreme class imbalance in rare-event detection (e.g. a child running into the road)?

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: Use targeted data collection, cost-sensitive training, and scenario-level evaluation rather than accuracy on a mostly negative dataset. Rare-event recall matters, but uncontrolled false alarms can also create unsafe behavior. Define the event and time horizon precisely, then evaluate detection lead time and downstream response under realistic prevalence and conditions.

## Detailed answer

Mine near-misses and hard negatives, add carefully validated simulation, and preserve diverse examples of small, occluded, and fast-moving children. Oversample rare positives only within training splits; keep validation prevalence realistic. Focal or weighted losses can help optimization, but do not solve missing coverage or poor labels.

Choose thresholds using the consequences of both misses and false positives, and evaluate trajectories rather than isolated frames. Test across distance, lighting, weather, clothing, and occlusion. Include uncertainty and conservative fallback behavior when evidence is insufficient. Safety evaluation should combine scenario tests, system-level analysis, and controlled operational evidence; Waymo's safety methodology illustrates that broader approach.

### Count opportunities, not just examples

If a dangerous event appears in one of a million frames, a classifier predicting “absent” everywhere reaches 99.9999% frame accuracy while providing no protection. Define the unit of evaluation as an event or trajectory with enough lead time to respond. Count misses, warning lead time, and false alerts per hour or distance under representative exposure.

Rare-event sampling should preserve context: a child hidden behind a vehicle, the preceding motion, and the eventual appearance. Hard negatives such as signs, reflections, and pedestrians safely on a sidewalk prevent a high-recall model from producing constant alarms. Weighting or focal loss changes which mistakes dominate training, but cannot create missing scenario coverage. Use simulation to vary conditions and validate that synthetic cues transfer to real data. Select an operating threshold jointly with downstream response behavior and human factors. A conservative fallback can be appropriate under uncertainty, but excessive false interventions also have consequences and need evaluation.
