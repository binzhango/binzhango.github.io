## Question

GLM has been bilingual Chinese/English since GLM-130B. What changes in tokenization, data and evaluation when a model must serve both languages well?

## Brief answer

A bilingual model needs efficient token coverage, balanced high-quality data, and evaluations written for both languages. Chinese and English differ in segmentation, idiom, typography, and knowledge distributions. Equal document counts or translated English tests do not establish equal capability, and vocabulary expansion trades fewer tokens against larger embedding and output matrices.

## Detailed answer

Measure tokens per character and per professionally segmented word on comparable corpora, including code-switching, names, numbers, and domain terminology. Avoid treating whitespace as a universal word boundary. Normalize text carefully without erasing meaningful script distinctions.

Balance by useful training signal rather than raw web volume. Deduplicate translations and preserve native Chinese writing rather than relying on translated English. Test native QA, writing, extraction, safety, tool use, and locale-specific formatting with fluent reviewers; report cross-language gaps and code-switch performance. Check that post-training does not erase pretraining capabilities in the smaller language. GLM-130B is a documented bilingual example, but the design principles apply independently of that release.

### Comparable data is not equal byte volume

Chinese and English differ in typical word boundaries, script coverage, and tokenization. A bilingual mixture with equal numbers of documents can still allocate very different numbers of training tokens to each language. Measure useful task coverage and token consumption after tokenization, then choose sampling weights deliberately. Otherwise a high-resource language may dominate gradients even when the corpus sounds “balanced.”

Use native questions for tasks involving idioms, legal or educational terminology, names, and locale conventions. For example, an extraction task should preserve the same monetary amount and date while producing the requested language's formatting. Test a Chinese question over an English document and an English question over Chinese evidence separately from monolingual QA. Shared representations can transfer skills, but can also cause unwanted language switching. Track that failure explicitly and retain bilingual instruction examples during post-training instead of assuming pretraining alone guarantees language control.
