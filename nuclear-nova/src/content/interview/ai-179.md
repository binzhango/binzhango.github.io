## Question

How do you approach GenAI safety in consumer products?

## Brief answer

Design consumer GenAI safety around likely users, content domains and actions, then measure both harmful behavior and excessive refusal. Layer model training with product controls, age-appropriate experiences, privacy protections and abuse response. Controls should match the actual harm pathway; a text classifier alone cannot secure an agent's external actions.

## Detailed answer

Suggested process: identify high-impact scenarios, build representative and adversarial evaluations, define escalation and refusal behavior, and test across languages and accessibility needs. Separate user intent from quoted or educational content so safety mechanisms do not indiscriminately block benign discussion.

Use restricted tool permissions, rate limits and human review for consequential workflows. Make uncertainty and source limitations visible where they affect user decisions. Provide reporting and appeal channels, and design retention/logging policies that support incident analysis without unnecessary exposure of sensitive conversations.

Monitor severity-weighted incidents, user exposure and false-positive rates. Roll out gradually with rollback capability and re-evaluate after adding modalities or tools. Safety is ongoing product operation: new abuse patterns and user populations can invalidate assumptions from prelaunch tests.

Map the product's harm pathways by feature. A text-only drafting assistant mainly affects what users read and reuse; an assistant with purchases, messaging or account tools can cause direct external effects. Adding images, voice or memory changes both abuse possibilities and privacy exposure, so the review must follow the product's actual capabilities.

Design useful safe alternatives for unsupported requests. A refusal should explain the relevant boundary without exposing internal policy or blocking legitimate educational context indiscriminately. For high-impact uncertainty, route to an appropriate human or authoritative resource rather than generating confident guesses. Test these paths with realistic users and supported languages.

Measure incidents with denominators and severity, alongside false refusals, successful assistance and appeal outcomes. A system can lower incident counts merely by reducing usage or refusing everything, so raw counts are insufficient. Maintain a feedback-to-evaluation loop, staged rollout and a way to disable a risky capability quickly. Consumer safety is sustained operation under changing behavior and traffic, not a fixed list of prohibited words.
