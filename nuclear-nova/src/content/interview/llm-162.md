## Question

What is fine-tuning, and how is it performed?

## Brief answer

Fine-tuning continues training from a pretrained checkpoint on a new objective or dataset to adapt its behavior or capabilities.

## Detailed answer

Prepare and split the data, preserve tokenizer and input conventions, choose trainable parameters, and optimize with conservative settings. Full fine-tuning updates all selected model weights; parameter-efficient methods train a smaller subset or added modules. Evaluate task gains and retained capabilities, and export the exact tokenizer, configuration, and weight or adapter artifacts required for inference.

Fine-tuning starts from learned weights rather than random initialization. The new objective can adapt classification, generation, domain language, or instruction behavior. The choice of dataset and supervised tokens determines what pressure the update applies. A model cannot infer the desired behavior simply because the dataset is labeled “domain” or “instruction.”

For a report-extraction task, collect input reports and verified structured outputs, split by source, and establish the base model's performance. Check serialization and loss masks, run a small sanity experiment, then tune update strength and duration on development data. Full tuning updates backbone parameters; PEFT restricts the trainable update through selected parameters or added modules. Both can overfit poor data.

Choose checkpoints by target utility plus retained-capability constraints, not training loss alone. Validate missing fields, unfamiliar report formats, and outputs requiring abstention. Export the exact tokenizer, configuration, and full weights or adapter dependencies needed for inference. Reevaluate after quantization or merging because deployment transformations can change outputs. Fine-tuning is successful when the adapted system generalizes the intended behavior under its operational constraints, not merely when optimization completes.
