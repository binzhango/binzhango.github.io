## Question

Explain the SVD and give two places it shows up in modern deep learning.

## Brief answer

The singular value decomposition writes a matrix as orthogonal left directions, nonnegative singular values and orthogonal right directions. It exposes rank and the strongest input-to-output directions. In deep learning it appears in low-rank compression and in analyzing representations or initialization, but a low-rank parameterization such as LoRA does not require computing an SVD during training.

## Detailed answer

For $A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, $A=U\Sigma V^\top$. Keeping the top r singular values gives the best rank-r approximation in Frobenius and spectral norm. The squared Frobenius reconstruction error is the sum of squared discarded singular values.

One application is compressing a dense weight matrix into two smaller factors, reducing parameters from mn to approximately $r(m+n)$. This is beneficial only when r is sufficiently small and the hardware executes the factorization efficiently; fine-tuning may be needed to recover task quality.

Another application is PCA of centered activation or embedding matrices: singular directions reveal dominant variation and can support dimensionality reduction or diagnostics. Dominant variance is not necessarily the most task-relevant information. State shapes and whether the matrix is centered before interpreting the decomposition, and avoid equating numerical rank with an exact count of semantic concepts.

For singular values `[5,2,1]`, keeping rank one discards squared energy $2^2+1^2=5$, so the Frobenius reconstruction error is $\sqrt5$. This is the best rank-one approximation under that matrix norm, but not necessarily the best compression for the downstream task: a small singular direction can still encode a critical feature.

For a square width-D weight, two rank-r factors use about $2Dr$ parameters rather than $D^2$. The saving requires $r<D/2$, and two smaller matrix multiplications may introduce overhead even when arithmetic decreases. Measure actual serving behavior after factorization and fine-tuning.

For PCA, center the observations before SVD if the goal is directions of variation around the mean. Without centering, a dominant mean offset can determine the first singular vector. Inspect explained variance and held-out task effects separately. LoRA resembles a low-rank update but learns factors directly; it does not need to decompose the full pretrained weight matrix with SVD at every training step.
