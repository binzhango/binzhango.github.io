## Question

What do you see as the most pressing unsolved problem in AI alignment?

## Brief answer

A defensible choice is scalable oversight: how to evaluate and guide systems whose reasoning or actions exceed what humans can reliably check. Explain the concrete failure mechanism, why existing feedback is insufficient and what evidence would change your view. There is no single agreed “most pressing” problem, so frame the answer as a reasoned priority.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: distinguish specification failure—rewarding the wrong objective—from generalization failure, where apparently aligned behavior changes outside training. Scalable oversight connects both: an evaluator may reward plausible answers or short-term success without detecting hidden errors and long-term consequences.

Propose tractable research such as decomposed verification, adversarial auditing, trusted tool checks and evaluations that compare stated reasoning with actual behavior. Name limitations: decomposition can hide global mistakes, model judges can share blind spots and interpretability evidence may be incomplete.

Ground the priority in the system's capabilities and deployment authority. A read-only assistant and an autonomous actor create different oversight burdens. Avoid declaring one technique a solved guarantee; instead identify measurable progress, such as detecting previously missed failures on held-out tasks without collapsing useful performance.

An illustrative answer could say: “I would prioritize scalable oversight for agents that can execute long workflows. A reviewer may approve a convincing plan without noticing a subtle data leak several steps later. I would test whether decomposed checks and independent outcome verification detect those failures better than a single model judge, while measuring the extra cost and false alarms.” This is a proposed position, not a claim about the author's personal work.

Strengthen the answer by naming a competing priority, such as robustness under distribution shift, and explaining why the deployment context changes the ranking. Specify a result that would change your mind: for example, strong evidence that tool-level constraints already bound the relevant harms might shift attention toward evaluation quality or specification errors. A useful interview response demonstrates reasoned prioritization and uncertainty, rather than treating an alignment slogan as a complete research agenda.
