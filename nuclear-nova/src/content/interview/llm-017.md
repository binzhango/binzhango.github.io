## Question

Explain maximum likelihood estimation for language modeling.

## Brief answer

Maximum likelihood estimation chooses parameters that assign high probability to observed training sequences.

## Detailed answer

For an autoregressive model, factor the probability using the chain rule and maximize its log:

$$
p_\theta(x_{1:T})=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}),\qquad
\theta^*=\arg\max_\theta\sum_{x\in D}\sum_t\log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t}).
$$

A beginning-of-sequence convention defines the initial context. Teacher forcing supplies observed prefixes during training. Taking logs turns products into sums; minimizing their negative gives the usual next-token cross-entropy objective. This trains a distribution over text, not a direct truth-verification objective.

For a toy two-token sequence, suppose the model assigns probability 0.2 to the first token and 0.5 to the second given the first. The sequence probability is $0.2\times0.5=0.1$, and its negative log-likelihood is $-\log0.1\approx2.303$ nats. Summing individual losses gives the same value: $-\log0.2-\log0.5$. Dividing by two gives the mean per-token loss.

During teacher-forced training, the second prediction receives the observed first token even if the model would have generated something else. Many positions can therefore be trained in parallel under a causal mask. At generation time, the model conditions on its own earlier outputs, so a mistake can alter later predictions.

Maximum likelihood rewards matching the distribution represented by the corpus. If that corpus contains repeated errors or biased sampling, learning those patterns can also improve likelihood. Data curation and separate evaluations are needed for factuality, usefulness, and safe behavior. Loss comparisons additionally require consistent tokenization, masking, and reduction; a smaller reported number is not meaningful when the denominator changes.
