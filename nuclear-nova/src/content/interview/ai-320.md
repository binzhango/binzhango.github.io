## Question

Estimate the annual serving cost of adding an LLM summary feature for 100 million weekly active users, and how you'd cut it by 10x.

## Brief answer

Estimate requests, tokens, and utilization explicitly; 100 million weekly active users alone is insufficient. Multiply annual feature invocations by input/output token prices or measured infrastructure cost, then add retrieval, storage, networking, and operations. A tenfold reduction must come from a measured combination of demand reduction, model selection, caching, and shorter outputs—not a claimed universal optimization.

## Detailed answer

Illustrative assumptions, not current vendor prices: 100M users, two summaries per week, 52 weeks, 2,000 input tokens and 200 output tokens each. That is 10.4B requests, 20.8T input tokens, and 2.08T output tokens annually. At assumed rates of $0.20 and $1.00 per million tokens, model charges are $4.16M + $2.08M = $6.24M, excluding other costs.

Vary adoption, frequency, document length, retries, and cache hit rate in sensitivity analysis. For self-hosting, price peak capacity and idle time rather than using peak tokens/sec. A possible tenfold path combines fewer duplicate summaries, validated smaller models, cached unchanged documents, and concise outputs. Verify each saving against quality and privacy constraints, and avoid multiplying improvements that affect the same cost twice.

### Show where a tenfold saving could come from

Using the illustrative $6.24M annual model charge, suppose half of summaries are duplicate requests for unchanged documents and can safely reuse a previously authorized result. Model demand falls to 50%. If a smaller validated model costs one-quarter as much per remaining request, the combined factor is 0.5 × 0.25 = 0.125, or an eightfold reduction. Reducing the remaining token cost by 20% then yields a factor of 0.1. Each assumption must be tested; these are not promised savings.

For a feature rollout, multiply weekly active users by actual adoption and invocation frequency, and model peak traffic separately from annual totals. Self-hosted capacity must survive peaks, maintenance, and failures, so average utilization determines whether hardware economics beat per-token billing. Include cache invalidation, storage, authorization checks, and quality regressions in the experiment. Report dollars per useful summary, not just dollars per token, because a cheaper answer that users regenerate repeatedly may cost more overall.
