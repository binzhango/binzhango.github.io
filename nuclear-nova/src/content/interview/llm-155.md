## Question

What should you check when relevant documents fail to reach the final context?

## Brief answer

When relevant documents do not reach the final context, inspect candidate recall, filters, rank cutoffs, reranking, and context packing separately.

## Detailed answer

If the document never appears among candidates, improve query interpretation or retrieval coverage. If it appears but is ranked poorly, assess reranking signals and relevance labels. If it ranks highly but disappears from the prompt, inspect deduplication and token-budget logic. Increasing k can improve coverage but also add noise and cost, so measure both retrieval and final answer changes.

Instrument the pipeline with stable IDs at each stage. Record whether the expected document appears in the initial retrieval pool, its rank after reranking, whether it survives deduplication, and whether its passage is included in the final prompt. This localizes the failure without relying on the final answer alone.

Suppose the relevant chunk ranks 15th initially and only ten candidates reach the reranker. Increasing reranker quality cannot help because it never sees that chunk; improve candidate recall or the first cutoff. If the chunk ranks first after reranking but is absent from the prompt, inspect token accounting and packing. A long irrelevant passage inserted earlier may exhaust the budget.

Check filter semantics and document versions as well. A wrong date filter can exclude the correct record, while deduplication can retain an obsolete near copy. Evaluate candidate recall, packed-context support coverage, and final answer quality separately. Increasing k is a useful experiment, but its extra distractors and latency must be measured. The correct fix depends on the stage where the evidence actually drops out.
