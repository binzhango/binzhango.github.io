## Question

What is a generative large model?

## Brief answer

A generative large model learns a distribution that can produce new content conditioned on an input or context.

## Detailed answer

Autoregressive LLMs generate text by repeatedly sampling or selecting the next token from $p(x_t\mid x_{<t})$. Other generative models use mechanisms such as diffusion and can produce images, audio, or video. “Large” has no universal parameter threshold, and generative capability does not imply factual correctness, reasoning reliability, or access to current information.

An autoregressive text model first tokenizes a prompt, computes a distribution over the next token, chooses one token, appends it, and repeats until a stopping condition. If the prompt is “The capital of,” the distribution represents plausible continuations learned from training, not a database lookup with guaranteed current truth. A model can produce new sentences by composing learned patterns even when the exact sentence never appeared in training.

Training and generation use the distribution differently. Training typically scores observed tokens and updates parameters to increase their likelihood. Generation keeps parameters fixed and uses a decoding rule, such as greedy selection or sampling, to choose an output path. Changing that rule can change diversity without adding knowledge.

Generative models are useful when outputs have flexible structure: explanations, code, translations, or images. A discriminative classifier may be simpler when only a fixed label is needed. Evaluate outputs for the task, including correctness, constraints, and failure handling. Fluency demonstrates that the model can generate plausible text; it does not establish source access, originality of every phrase, or reliable reasoning.
