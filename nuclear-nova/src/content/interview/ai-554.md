## Question

Clinic audio contains two or three speakers, background noise, accents, and many drug names. How would you build and improve the ASR?

## Brief answer

Optimize clinical ASR for speaker attribution and clinically important entities as well as overall word error rate. Use representative room audio, timestamps, domain vocabulary, and explicit uncertainty, then evaluate with qualified reviewers. Noise reduction and chart hints can help, but must not fabricate speech or erase negation.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Capture calibrated audio with consent and appropriate handling controls, detect speech segments, diarize speakers, and decode with domain-aware vocabulary. Preserve alternatives for uncertain drug names, doses, units, and corrections. Use chart context as a separately labeled hint rather than a transcript source.

Build evaluation slices for accents, overlapping speakers, room noise, specialty terms, and microphone positions. Report medication/entity error rate, negation errors, speaker confusion, and downstream note defects alongside word error rate. Collect targeted corrections for retraining without leaking test encounters into training. Review performance by subgroup and clinic. A lower average word error rate can still worsen safety if rare but important dosage errors increase.

### Use an error taxonomy aligned with downstream harm

Word error rate treats every substituted, inserted, or deleted word similarly. In a clinical transcript, confusing a dose, dropping negation, or assigning a statement to the wrong speaker can matter much more than omitting a filler word. Retain WER as a broad diagnostic metric, then add entity and assertion-level measures for medications, quantities, units, symptoms, temporality, and polarity.

Build test slices for accents, noisy rooms, overlapping speakers, abbreviations, and specialty terminology. Include absent-entity cases: measuring recognition only when a drug is spoken misses hallucinated insertions when it is not. Evaluate whether contextual biasing helps names without encouraging the recognizer to copy unrelated chart entries.

Inspect downstream effects on note generation. An ASR error that the review interface exposes as uncertain may be less damaging than the same error silently converted into a confident assertion. Conversely, a fluent transcript can still contain a subtle negation mistake. Preserve timestamps and alternatives where supported so reviewers can check the original evidence.

Compare systems on the same audio with qualified adjudication of disputed critical terms. Report both overall and subgroup results with uncertainty, and retain a fixed regression set for known high-severity failures. Select the system based on its ability to support a reliable clinical documentation workflow, rather than choosing whichever has the lowest single aggregate transcription score.
