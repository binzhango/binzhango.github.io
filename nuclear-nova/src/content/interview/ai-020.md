## Question

What is speculative decoding? Why is output quality preserved, and when does it not help?

## Brief answer

Speculative decoding lets a cheaper draft propose tokens and a target model verify them together. Exact rejection-sampling correction can preserve the target distribution; arbitrary accept-or-replace heuristics cannot claim that guarantee.

## Detailed answer

For a proposed token from draft distribution $q$, accept with probability $\min(1,p/q)$ using the target distribution $p$. On rejection, sample from the normalized positive part of $p-q$. This correction and the extra target sample are essential to exactness. Gains shrink when draft acceptance is poor, draft execution is expensive, or the target is already efficiently batched. Compare end-to-end latency including verification, communication and discarded work.

For one-step intuition, let the target distribution over two tokens be $p=[0.6,0.4]$ and the draft be $q=[0.8,0.2]$. Draft token A is accepted with probability $0.6/0.8=0.75$, while B is always accepted. Accepted mass is therefore $[0.6,0.2]$. The remaining 0.2 rejection mass is assigned to B through the positive residual $p-q$, yielding the exact target distribution $[0.6,0.4]$.

For multiple draft tokens, verification computes target conditionals at their positions in one target pass. Accept tokens sequentially until the first rejection; discard later draft tokens because their prefixes are no longer valid. If all proposals pass, the target can supply an additional token. Correct implementations must account for the same temperature, truncation and constraints in the distributions being compared.

The speed tradeoff is the number of accepted output tokens per expensive target pass, minus drafting and bookkeeping cost. A strong but slow draft can lose; a cheap draft with poor agreement wastes verification. High target batching may already exploit the hardware well, reducing the benefit. Report acceptance by task and prompt length, end-to-end latency and total accelerator time, since lower user latency need not mean lower infrastructure cost.
