## Question

What is your favourite product and why?

## Brief answer

Choose a product you genuinely use and explain the user problem, the key design decision and the evidence that it works well. Include a meaningful weakness and a specific improvement. The goal is to demonstrate product judgment, not to name a fashionable tool or list features you have never used.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: identify the target user and the task the product makes easier. Describe one concrete workflow, including what happens before and after the product is used. Explain why its interaction, reliability, distribution or business model is effective for that audience.

Then discuss a tradeoff. For example, a simplified interface may hide advanced controls, or real-time collaboration may complicate offline use. Propose an improvement with a measurable outcome and a plausible risk, rather than adding an AI feature without a user need.

Distinguish personal preference from market evidence. If you cite adoption or revenue, verify the figures; otherwise describe your observation as an observation. A balanced critique usually shows more insight than unconditional praise.

An illustrative product critique might choose a collaborative document editor and focus on one task: several people revising the same proposal without emailing competing versions. The core value is shared state and recoverable history, not merely a long feature list. A meaningful weakness might be unreliable offline behavior, with an improvement measured by successful synchronization and fewer conflict surprises.

Use a product you actually know and describe a real workflow you have observed. Separate your own preference from claims about all users. If you propose a change, explain who benefits, what becomes harder and what evidence would show it worked. Adding more controls may help experts while making first use confusing.

The interview response should reveal how you reason about users and tradeoffs. A favorite product need not be technically novel or related to AI. It is more convincing to analyze one ordinary interaction deeply than to praise an unfamiliar product using adoption statistics you cannot substantiate.
