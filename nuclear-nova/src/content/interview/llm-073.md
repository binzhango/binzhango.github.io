## Question

What is contrastive learning?

## Brief answer

Contrastive learning trains representations to score related examples above unrelated examples.

## Detailed answer

A common query-positive objective is:

$$
L=-\log\frac{\exp(s(q,d^+)/\tau)}{\exp(s(q,d^+)/\tau)+\sum_{d^-}\exp(s(q,d^-)/\tau)}.
$$

$s$ is a similarity function and $\tau>0$ a temperature. Positives and negatives may come from labels, paired modalities, or augmentations. The pairing policy defines what “similar” means; poorly chosen positives teach the wrong invariances. Some self-supervised approaches avoid explicit negatives using different objectives, so not all representation learning follows this formula.

The loss is a classification task over a candidate set: identify which document is the designated positive for a query. Raising the positive score increases its share of the softmax denominator and lowers loss; raising a negative score does the opposite. With one positive and three equally scored negatives, the positive probability is one quarter and the loss is $\log4\approx1.386$ nats.

Temperature controls score sharpness. A smaller value makes a given score difference more decisive and changes gradient concentration, so it interacts with embedding normalization and negative difficulty. It should be tuned or learned under the intended objective rather than copied without context.

For retrieval, a positive must support the query's information need. Two documents about the same product are not necessarily interchangeable positives if one discusses the wrong version. In image-text learning, paired captions teach another notion of compatibility. Augmentations likewise declare what differences should be ignored. Validate on independent examples, inspect false negatives, and test whether learned similarity supports the downstream task. Low contrastive loss on a narrow training pool can coexist with poor retrieval on unfamiliar language or topics.
