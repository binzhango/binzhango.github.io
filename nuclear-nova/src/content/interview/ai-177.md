## Question

A customer says “the model got worse” after you upgraded model versions in their deployment. How do you verify and respond?

## Brief answer

Reproduce the regression under controlled conditions before attributing it to the model. Pin prompts, tools, retrieval results and sampling settings, compare old and new versions on representative cases, and inspect task-level differences. If impact is material, roll back or constrain the upgrade while investigating rather than asking the customer to accept an unexplained aggregate benchmark gain.

## Detailed answer

Collect examples, expected behavior, model identifiers and deployment changes. Separate quality regressions from altered formatting, refusal behavior, latency and tool-call compatibility. Build a paired evaluation with blinded review and report performance by important slice, including cases where the new version improves.

Run ablations with identical retrieved evidence and tool responses. Check tokenizer/context changes, system-prompt assumptions and stricter schema requirements. A previously tolerated malformed request may now fail, which is an integration regression even if the underlying model is stronger.

Agree on acceptance thresholds, version pinning and a canary process. Communicate confirmed findings and remaining uncertainty with a concrete next update. Keep a migration test suite so the next upgrade is evaluated against the customer's actual workflow rather than a generic model leaderboard.

Use the customer's examples to define the regression, then build a balanced sample including ordinary successes and critical cases. A new model may be more accurate overall while breaking a required JSON field, refusing valid requests or becoming too verbose for the workflow. Those are real product regressions even if a general capability benchmark improved.

Freeze retrieved evidence and tool responses for one comparison, then run the live pipeline separately. This distinguishes model behavior from changes in retrieval, templates or provider defaults. Review outputs blind to version when practical, and record paired wins/losses rather than just independent averages.

If impact warrants rollback, preserve the failing bundle and traces for diagnosis. Communicate what is confirmed, what remains uncertain and the acceptance test for reintroducing the upgrade. Do not require the customer to supply a perfect technical explanation before taking the report seriously. The lasting fix is a versioned migration suite representing their actual tasks, plus a canary and rollback mechanism for future changes.
