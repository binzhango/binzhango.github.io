## Question

How does FLARE use active retrieval during generation?

## Brief answer

FLARE retrieves during generation when an anticipated next sentence contains low-confidence tokens, then regenerates that sentence using retrieved evidence.

## Detailed answer

The method predicts upcoming content, uses that content to form a retrieval query, and triggers retrieval based on token-confidence signals rather than only at the start. Query construction must avoid overcommitting to uncertain invented details. Reliable token probabilities may be unavailable through some APIs, and high confidence does not prove factual correctness. It adds retrieval and generation work, so evaluate the quality-latency tradeoff.

FLARE anticipates information needs while composing an answer. The system drafts a prospective next sentence, identifies low-confidence tokens, uses the anticipated content to form a retrieval query, and regenerates the sentence with evidence when retrieval is triggered. This differs from retrieving once before writing an entire long response, because later sentences can introduce needs that were not clear from the original question.

For a biography, an early sentence may be supported by the initial context while the next sentence requires a date or institution. Active retrieval can seek that evidence at the point it becomes relevant. Query construction should avoid treating the uncertain date itself as established; otherwise the system may search for support for its own guess.

Confidence is only a trigger heuristic. A model can be confidently wrong or uncertain about an unusual but correct name, and some generation APIs do not expose the required token probabilities. Evaluate retrieval frequency, added latency, factual support, and unnecessary searches. The method improves information gathering only when the retrieved sources and regenerated sentence actually resolve the uncertainty; it does not turn token probability into a truth detector.
