## Question

How would you evaluate a meeting-summarisation feature before shipping it to a hundred million users?

## Brief answer

Evaluate whether the summary accurately represents the meeting, preserves decisions and action owners, and omits unsupported claims. Use representative transcripts across meeting types, languages, audio quality, and speaker counts. Human review, structured factual checks, and staged online rollout are complementary; a single text-similarity score is not a release gate.

## Detailed answer

Build an adjudicated set of decisions, action items, dates, owners, unresolved questions, and sensitive exclusions. Score hallucinations separately from omissions, with higher severity for wrong commitments or attribution. Inspect whether ASR errors propagate into confident summaries. Compare against the current workflow or human notes using blinded reviewers.

Test long meetings, interruptions, jokes, negation, absent speakers, and malicious spoken instructions. Evaluate usefulness and time saved with consented users, then canary by cohort with rollback criteria. Monitor corrections, deleted summaries, opt-outs, and support complaints without assuming all engagement is positive. At large scale, rare severe errors matter: report slice-level rates and uncertainty, and ensure users can inspect the cited transcript evidence.

### Evaluate individual claims

For a meeting where someone says, “We might launch Friday, but legal has not approved it,” a summary stating “Launch is Friday” creates an unsupported commitment. Annotate the decision status, uncertainty, owner, and timing separately so a fluent paraphrase cannot receive full credit for changing the meaning. A summary can omit casual discussion while preserving every actual action item.

Use independent raters on a representative sample and adjudicate disagreements; automated judges can triage volume but need calibration against those judgments. Include transcripts with deliberately altered names, negation, and dates to catch attribution shortcuts. An online pilot should measure whether users correct action items and whether recipients understand the same commitments, alongside time saved. At very large scale, even a low severe-error rate can affect many meetings. Define severity-based release thresholds, confidence bounds, and a way to inspect the relevant transcript span before broad rollout.
