## Question

Text normalisation is where TTS quality actually dies in production. Walk me through it.

## Brief answer

Normalize text into a pronunciation-aware representation before synthesis, using language, locale, context, and entity type. Dates, currencies, abbreviations, addresses, units, and names are ambiguous without that context. Preserve the original text and normalization trace so incorrect speech can be diagnosed rather than blamed generically on the voice model.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Classify spans such as phone numbers, decimal quantities, URLs, and medication names, then apply deterministic rules where reliable and lexicons or learned disambiguation where needed. “03/04” needs locale or task context; a phone number should not be spoken as one large integer. Pronunciation overrides should be scoped to language and domain.

Test text-to-normalized-form separately from acoustic quality, with golden cases and native-speaker review. Track harmful entity substitutions, intelligibility, and user corrections. Keep punctuation and sentence boundaries useful for prosody rather than stripping them indiscriminately. When ambiguity matters, ask for clarification or speak an unambiguous rendering. Avoid injecting markup from untrusted text into a provider's synthesis controls.

### Preserve meaning through a typed normalization pipeline

Keep the original span, its entity type, the normalized spoken form, and the rule or model that produced it. For example, `$12.50` in an English-US payment confirmation can become “twelve dollars and fifty cents,” while `12050` in an account identifier should be read according to the identifier convention. The same characters can require different speech because their role differs.

Order transformations carefully. Detect protected entities before expanding abbreviations, and resolve locale before interpreting dates or decimal separators. Do not globally replace every period, slash, or letter sequence: that can corrupt version numbers, names, units, and URLs. A domain lexicon should override pronunciation without silently changing the displayed entity or numerical value.

When a date such as `03/04` is ambiguous and consequential, recover the structured date from the source system or ask which date was intended. Guessing a locale from a voice alone is fragile. Similarly, avoid guessing a medication name from a vaguely similar spelling; route uncertain clinical terms for appropriate confirmation.

Build a regression suite in layers: entity detection, spoken-form correctness, pronunciation, then complete audio intelligibility. Store the intermediate trace with suitable privacy controls so an incident can be assigned to normalization or synthesis. This makes a pronunciation fix narrow and testable instead of forcing a costly voice-model retraining cycle.
