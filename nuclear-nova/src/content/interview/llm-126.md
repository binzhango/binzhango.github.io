## Question

What is RAG?

## Brief answer

Retrieval-augmented generation retrieves external information and conditions a generative model on it when answering a request.

## Detailed answer

A retriever finds potentially relevant evidence, and a generator uses that evidence with the question. Contemporary applications often retrieve once and place excerpts in a prompt; others retrieve iteratively or use trained retrieval-generation architectures. The original RAG paper combined parametric generation with a non-parametric document index. Retrieval does not necessarily update model weights.

A basic system has two stores of information: the model's learned parameters and an external collection that can be searched. On a question about a maintenance procedure, the retriever selects relevant manual passages, and the generator conditions its answer on those passages plus the question. Updating the manual index can change the available answer without retraining the generator.

The retrieval unit matters. A paragraph may match the wording while the surrounding section contains the applicable product version or exception. Preserve provenance and restore that context before generation. The final answer should rely on retrieved evidence rather than treating any retrieved text as automatically authoritative.

A complete RAG application includes ingestion, authorization, ranking, token-budget management, and evaluation around the two central components. Test whether the answer exists in the corpus, whether it reaches the prompt, and whether the generator uses it correctly. Some systems retrieve once; others retrieve again after identifying a missing fact. More retrieval calls are justified only when they improve supported task completion enough to offset latency, cost, and query-drift risk.
