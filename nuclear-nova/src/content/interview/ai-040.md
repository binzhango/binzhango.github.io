## Question

Compare HNSW, IVF-PQ and flat indexes. How do you pick, and what does recall@k cost in latency?

## Brief answer

Flat search scores every vector and is exact for the selected metric. HNSW uses a navigable graph for fast approximate search, while IVF-PQ partitions the space and compresses vectors to reduce memory at an accuracy cost.

## Detailed answer

Choose flat search for small corpora or a correctness baseline. HNSW often trades substantial graph memory and construction cost for strong recall; increasing search breadth usually improves recall while increasing work. IVF-PQ saves memory but needs representative training data and careful probe/codebook settings. Compare recall against exact neighbors under actual filters, update patterns and concurrency. A global recall figure can hide poor behavior for rare tenants or heavily filtered subsets.

Flat search computes the chosen distance against every vector, giving an exact baseline at $O(Nd)$ work for $N$ vectors of dimension $d$. Its simplicity can be competitive on small datasets or GPUs. HNSW navigates a graph from coarse entry points toward nearby vectors; larger search breadth usually improves recall at the cost of more distance evaluations. Its links add memory beyond the original vectors.

IVF first assigns vectors to coarse clusters and searches only selected clusters. Product quantization further encodes subvectors using small codebooks, trading some distance fidelity for much smaller storage. Increase the number of probed clusters to recover recall, then optionally rerank candidates using original vectors. Poorly trained centroids or a shifted corpus can create imbalanced lists and unpredictable latency.

Choose using a measured recall-latency-memory curve under the actual workload. “Recall@10” for approximate search typically means overlap with exact top-ten neighbors, which is different from human relevance recall. Test both when the final application is search. Apply realistic tenant and metadata filters: a globally accurate index may underperform on small restricted subsets. Include index construction, updates, deletion behavior and recovery costs, because a fast static benchmark says little about a continuously changing service.
