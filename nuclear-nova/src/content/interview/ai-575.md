## Question

Disengagement rate is a weak safety proxy. How would you actually measure whether the Driver is safe enough to ship?

## Brief answer

Combine severity-weighted real-world outcomes, scenario-based testing, closed-loop simulation, fault tolerance, and a reviewed safety argument for the intended operating domain. Disengagements are influenced by driver behavior and test policy, so they are not a direct measure of collision risk. No single scalar establishes that a driver is safe enough to release.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Define exposure-normalized safety outcomes with uncertainty and appropriate comparison baselines, then assess coverage of rare but consequential scenarios. Track collisions, near-collision mechanisms, rule violations, recovery, and response to sensor/system faults. Validate simulation against observed behavior and keep independent test sets.

Review whether the evidence supports each claim about domain limits, fallback, and operational response. Waymo's deployment discussion explicitly describes combining multiple methodologies rather than relying on one metric. Set release gates and accountable review before seeing results. A relative improvement over the previous build can still be insufficient in absolute terms; the final decision concerns residual risk within a specified deployment scope.

### Build a safety argument with testable claims

Start with the intended operating domain: road types, speed range, weather, geography, and permitted maneuvers. For each hazard, state a claim, an acceptance criterion, and the evidence that supports it. A pedestrian-detection claim needs distance/occlusion scenarios and downstream stopping behavior; a compute-failure claim needs fault injection and a verified fallback. An aggregate fleet score cannot substitute for these mechanisms.

Use matched exposure when comparing real-world outcomes, with severity and uncertainty reported separately. Rare-event estimates need enough evidence; zero events in a small sample does not establish zero risk. Closed-loop simulation expands scenario coverage, controlled tests validate mechanisms, and operational monitoring detects gaps after limited deployment. Keep independent review and accountable release ownership, including the authority to restrict the domain or delay rollout. Distinguish “better than the previous build” from “meets the release criteria.” The final decision should explain residual risk, unsupported conditions, and how the service responds when it leaves the validated domain.
