## Question

Tell me about a time you had conflicting priorities with stakeholders and how you secured alignment.

## Brief answer

Make conflicting priorities explicit in terms of outcomes, deadlines, dependencies and opportunity cost. Establish a shared decision criterion and a clear decision owner, then document the tradeoff and communicate what will be delayed. Alignment means a workable commitment, not pretending every stakeholder can receive everything immediately.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: describe the competing requests and why each mattered. Show how you gathered evidence about urgency and impact, identified dependencies and proposed options such as sequencing, reduced scope or additional capacity. Include the consequences of each option rather than presenting your preference as inevitable.

Explain how the decision was reached and how you checked that stakeholders understood the implications. Track follow-up commitments and revisit the plan if assumptions change. If you escalated, frame it as clarifying ownership rather than avoiding responsibility.

Use a real outcome and distinguish agreement from satisfaction. A stakeholder may remain disappointed while accepting the rationale and next steps. End with what you learned about surfacing tradeoffs earlier.

An illustrative case is a customer launch competing with reliability work. Rather than treating one stakeholder as unreasonable, estimate the launch value, outage exposure, dependencies and the cost of postponement. Offer concrete choices: ship a narrower scope, delay a feature while fixing the critical risk, or add capacity if genuinely available. Each option should state what will not happen immediately.

In a real answer, show how the decision was documented and communicated to people outside the meeting. Agreement at the leadership level is insufficient if implementers still receive conflicting instructions. Assign owners and dates for deferred commitments, and define which new evidence would reopen the decision.

Use the actual result, including disappointment or a later change in priorities. Alignment means the team can execute a shared plan with understood tradeoffs, not that every stakeholder got their first choice. The learning should concern earlier visibility, decision criteria or ownership, rather than a generic claim that communication is important.
