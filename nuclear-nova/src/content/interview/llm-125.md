## Question

What are the limitations of base LLMs that RAG solves?

## Brief answer

RAG can reduce gaps in freshness, access to private documents, and source attribution, but does not eliminate hallucination or reasoning errors.

## Detailed answer

The generator can only use evidence that exists, is accessible, is retrieved, and fits its context. Stale indexes, missing documents, misleading passages, or poor synthesis can still produce wrong answers. Retrieval is most useful when errors stem from unavailable information; it may not fix weak arithmetic, poor instruction following, or bad task definitions. Compare results with and without retrieval on representative questions.

Classify the missing capability carefully. A model that has never seen an internal deployment guide cannot answer its exact rollback procedure from parameters alone. Retrieval can supply the guide. A model that knows an outdated procedure can be shown the current dated version. A model asked to identify which sentence supports an answer can cite the retrieved passage rather than rely on unverifiable memory.

However, every step can still fail. The guide may not have been indexed, access filters may exclude it correctly, the retriever may select a similar but obsolete version, or context packing may omit a warning. Even with perfect evidence, the generator can misread a negation or perform arithmetic incorrectly.

Use an oracle-context experiment: manually supply the correct evidence for a sample of questions. If quality improves sharply, retrieval or ingestion is a major bottleneck. If it remains poor, focus on evidence presentation, task specification, or generator capability. RAG is particularly effective for information-access gaps; it is not a universal repair for unreliable computation, poorly defined requirements, or unsupported actions.
