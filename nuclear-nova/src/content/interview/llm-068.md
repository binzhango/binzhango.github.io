## Question

Why is cross-entropy preferred over MSE for classification tasks?

## Brief answer

Cross-entropy matches categorical maximum likelihood and usually gives more effective gradients for softmax classification than MSE on class probabilities.

## Detailed answer

For one-hot targets, its gradient with respect to logits is $q-y$. MSE on softmax probabilities includes the softmax Jacobian, which can shrink gradients for confident incorrect predictions. MSE is not mathematically invalid: the Brier score is a proper probabilistic scoring rule. Choose according to the training objective and calibration needs; the common preference for cross-entropy is not a claim that regression losses can never classify.

Consider a binary target $y=1$ with predicted probability $p=0.001$. Binary cross-entropy with a sigmoid logit has gradient $p-y\approx-0.999$ with respect to that logit. For squared error $\tfrac12(p-y)^2$, the gradient is $(p-y)p(1-p)\approx-0.000998$. The extra sigmoid derivative makes the squared-error gradient tiny despite a confidently wrong prediction. This illustrates why cross-entropy often recovers more effectively from such mistakes.

The probabilistic interpretation also fits classification: maximizing the probability of the observed categorical outcome leads directly to negative log-likelihood. Cross-entropy distinguishes assigning 0.1 versus 0.001 to the true class strongly because the latter is a much more confident error.

That does not make squared error useless. The Brier score evaluates probabilistic predictions and can be useful for calibration-oriented applications. Cross-entropy is also sensitive to mislabeled examples that receive very low predicted probability, so annotation quality and regularization still matter. Choose metrics for decision quality separately from the training loss, and compare calibration and important error costs rather than assuming a preferred loss guarantees a better classifier.
