## Question

Cascaded ASR+LLM+TTS versus native speech-to-speech: argue both sides.

## Brief answer

A cascade of ASR, text LLM and TTS offers modular control, inspectable transcripts and independent component replacement. Native speech-to-speech can preserve prosody and reduce intermediate processing, but may be harder to inspect and control.

## Detailed answer

Cascades can lose emotion, timing and nonverbal cues at the text boundary and accumulate startup delays. Native models may better handle expressive interaction, yet tool use, factual auditing and text-based policies still need explicit design. Compare real conversational tasks, interruption quality, multilingual performance, cost and safety instrumentation. A hybrid architecture can retain transcripts for observability without forcing all model computation through them.

A cascade exposes clear interfaces: audio becomes text, the text model reasons or calls tools, and a synthesizer produces speech. This supports separate language customization, transcript search and component-level debugging. If a name is misheard, the error can be localized to ASR; if the response is correct but pronounced badly, TTS is responsible.

The text boundary discards information unless it is explicitly carried alongside the transcript. Tone, hesitation, laughter and timing may affect meaning, and a transcript alone can miss them. Cascades also accumulate buffering and startup delays, though streaming can overlap stages. Native speech-to-speech models can use richer acoustic context and generate more natural timing, but controlling exact wording and inspecting errors may require additional instrumentation.

Choose from product requirements. A regulated or highly transactional workflow may value a verified text record and deterministic action controls; a conversational companion may emphasize prosody and low-latency turn taking. Neither architecture automatically wins on factuality, safety or multilingual quality. Compare entity accuracy, tool-call correctness, interruptions, expressive behavior, cost and auditability on the same tasks. A hybrid can retain transcripts and structured actions while allowing the speech model to use acoustic information that a pure text cascade would discard.
