## Question

Design a connector framework that syncs content and permissions from 100+ SaaS apps into one index.

## Brief answer

Define a connector contract for identity, content enumeration, incremental changes, permissions, deletions, checkpoints, and health. Keep source-specific semantics inside adapters while central services handle scheduling, retries, normalization, indexing, and auditing. A hundred connectors require operational consistency more than a universal lowest-common-denominator parser.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Each adapter emits versioned records with source ID, tenant, revision, content, ACL references, and tombstones. Use source cursors or change feeds where available, plus periodic full reconciliation to catch missed events. Persist checkpoints only after durable ingestion and make replay idempotent. Handle permission updates with higher urgency than ordinary content refreshes.

Separate rate-limit budgets per tenant and source, quarantine malformed records, and expose lag and coverage dashboards. Preserve provenance through parsing and chunking. Test token expiry, pagination changes, partial failures, renamed resources, nested groups, and delete-then-recreate behavior. An adapter certification suite should verify these contracts before release. Fail closed when authorization information is unavailable, and clearly report incomplete coverage rather than presenting an apparently healthy but stale index.

### Specify the checkpoint protocol

A connector reads a source page, writes normalized records and tombstones durably, and only then advances its cursor. If the worker crashes before the cursor update, the page is replayed; immutable source IDs and revision-aware upserts make replay safe. Advancing the cursor first risks permanently losing records. A cursor therefore describes acknowledged ingestion, not merely the last page requested.

Separate content and ACL streams when the source supports them, but reconcile their ordering. A new document must not become visible with an uninitialized broad ACL while its permission event is still queued. If an adapter cannot determine current permissions, quarantine that record from serving until the contract is resolved.

For 100 connectors, publish an operational contract: maximum supported lag, how deletions are detected, whether group membership is expanded or resolved dynamically, and what a full resync costs. Certification should include duplicate pages, cursor expiry, permission revocation, and delete-then-recreate with the same human-readable name. Shared infrastructure handles these patterns consistently while adapters preserve the source semantics that cannot be generalized away.
