## Question

A customer executive says “the AI keeps getting things wrong” and wants to cancel the pilot. Walk me through your next 48 hours.

## Brief answer

Acknowledge the impact, pause harmful behavior, and turn the complaint into a small set of reproducible failures with agreed business consequences. The next forty-eight hours should establish facts, containment, and a credible recovery or stop decision. Do not respond with aggregate benchmark scores that ignore the executive's actual experience.

## Detailed answer

**Response framework.** In the first hours, meet the operational owner, collect affected examples, identify scope, and preserve relevant versions and traces. Classify errors into data, retrieval, reasoning, permissions, and workflow design. Put a safe fallback in place for consequential failures and state clearly what is known.

By the next day, reproduce priority cases, estimate affected users, and demonstrate a focused fix or revised scope on independent examples. Agree on measurable acceptance criteria, an owner, and a short validation window. Report remaining limitations and whether the pilot still has a defensible path to value. If the use case or data cannot meet the required quality, recommend narrowing or stopping it rather than disguising the gap with a polished demo.

### Make the forty-eight hours produce a decision

In the first four hours, acknowledge concrete harm, identify the operational owner, and contain consequential failures. By the first day, reproduce a prioritized set with frozen model, prompt, data, and tool versions. Classify whether the issue is missing evidence, wrong reasoning, stale data, unauthorized actions, or a workflow that encourages overtrust. Give the executive a concise evidence-based update rather than a defensive benchmark presentation.

By the second day, demonstrate the proposed repair on both the reported failures and independent cases, define measurable acceptance criteria, and name an owner and validation period. **Illustrative example:** if the assistant invents refund status, restrict it to authenticated status-tool results and escalate unsupported cases; then test stale records and tool outages. This is a scenario, not personal history. If the use case cannot meet the required quality, recommend narrowing or ending the pilot. A credible recovery includes known limits and a stop condition, not an unverified promise that more prompting will solve everything.
