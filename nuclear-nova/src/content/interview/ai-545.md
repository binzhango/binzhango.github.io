## Question

The note should be ready before the clinician leaves the room. Build me the latency budget, and tell me where the money goes.

## Brief answer

Process audio incrementally, maintain a provisional structured note, and finalize only after the visit ends and required consistency checks complete. The deadline is note readiness for clinician review, not automatic signature. Separate the latency of ASR, extraction, note synthesis, validation, and EHR write-back to find the actual bottleneck.

## Detailed answer

**Suggested approach.** Stream transcription and section-level fact extraction during the encounter, preserving timestamps and uncertainty. At the end, reconcile corrections, speaker attribution, medications, and unresolved contradictions before producing the draft. An illustrative finalization budget might be 2 seconds ASR settling, 4 seconds synthesis, 2 seconds validation, and 2 seconds write-back, to be tested on real visit distributions.

Cost comes from audio processing, long-context generation, repeated revisions, storage, and integration operations. Reuse stable extracted facts and regenerate changed sections rather than the entire note after every utterance. Measure time from encounter end to usable draft, critical omissions, unsupported additions, and clinician review time. A faster draft is not beneficial if it increases verification burden or arrives in the wrong chart.

### Reuse stable work without freezing provisional facts

Maintain an encounter state containing extracted facts, sources, uncertainty, and note-section assignments. During the visit, update this state when a speaker corrects an earlier statement. If “ten milligrams” becomes “actually, five,” the correction should invalidate affected medication text rather than leave two inconsistent doses in different sections.

At encounter end, synthesize from the reconciled state and run targeted checks for patient identity, attribution, unsupported claims, contradictions, and required sections. The illustrative 2 + 4 + 2 + 2 second finalization budget totals ten seconds; its usefulness depends on whether a usable reviewed draft actually arrives within that interval under concurrent load. A long-tail tool call or retry can dominate the whole deadline.

Repeated whole-note generation can multiply cost. If a visit has twenty incremental updates, regenerating an unchanged history each time wastes tokens and can introduce new wording errors. Regenerate affected sections and perform a final cross-section consistency pass, while periodically rebuilding from authoritative facts to detect accumulated state errors.

Instrument cost by audio minute, fact-extraction call, finalization, and integration operation. Compare total clinician time as well as machine latency: a draft delivered two seconds sooner but requiring several extra minutes of verification is a regression. Keep signature and final acceptance within the clinician's established workflow.
