## Question

Your offline metric improved but the online A/B did not. Enumerate the reasons this happens and how you would tell them apart.

## Brief answer

Check whether the offline gain measured the right objective on representative data and whether the online system actually served the intended change. Common causes include leakage, biased labels, proxy mismatch, insufficient experiment power, feature skew, latency regressions, and user or marketplace feedback. Diagnose them with evidence rather than immediately discarding either result.

## Detailed answer

Verify assignment, exposure, model version, feature values, and sample-ratio balance. Compare offline replay predictions with logged online predictions for the same inputs. Inspect latency and fallback rates: a better ranker cannot help if it times out and the old system serves most requests.

Then examine the metric. Better click prediction may not improve satisfaction, and a static dataset reflects the previous ranking policy. Look for cohort differences, novelty effects, saturation, and interference between users. Estimate confidence intervals and whether the test could detect the expected gain. Use a small controlled intervention or targeted experiment to isolate the likely mechanism. Keep both negative and positive findings in the model's evaluation record.

### Follow the causal chain

First establish that the new model actually served the treatment traffic with the intended features. A better offline ranker that times out frequently may produce an online experience dominated by fallback results. Compare a sample of online inputs and outputs with offline replay to isolate implementation or feature-parity errors.

Then ask whether the offline target influences the desired outcome. Better click prediction can favor provocative content without improving retention or satisfaction. A historical dataset also reflects the old policy's exposures, so unseen candidates and position effects limit replay validity. Finally check experimental power, novelty effects, and interference. A small true gain may be undetectable, while a short-lived gain may fade. Use a targeted experiment to test the leading hypothesis—for example, equalize latency or randomize a small candidate subset. Do not keep tuning on the same failed online comparison until a noisy positive result appears; update the evaluation model and run a properly specified test.
