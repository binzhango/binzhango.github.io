## Question

When should an agent retrieve the full conversation history?

## Brief answer

Loading the full conversation is simple and preserves detail, but only works when the useful history fits the model's context and cost budget.

## Detailed answer

Retrieve messages in order, preserve system instructions and tool-call/result pairs, and reserve space for new evidence and the answer. Long transcripts increase prefill cost and can distract the model. Never concatenate unrelated users' histories. Full-history storage can still be useful for auditing even when model calls use a smaller, selected context.

Full-history context is appropriate for a short conversation where earlier turns contain tightly related constraints and the transcript fits comfortably after reserving answer space. It avoids summarization loss and can simplify early prototypes. For example, a five-turn editing discussion may be best represented exactly, including the user's corrections and the latest draft.

Before sending it, preserve chronological order and complete tool-call/result pairs. Do not replay a tool result without its identifying call or accidentally duplicate messages on reload. Keep trusted current instructions separate from quoted historical content, and ensure the context belongs to the right user and task.

As history grows, costs and failure risks change. Large tool outputs can consume most of the window, older corrections can conflict with newer ones, and relevant details can be buried among unrelated material. Switch to selected recent turns plus structured task state, summaries, or retrieval when needed. Keep the complete transcript in durable storage if required for audit, but do not confuse storage retention with context selection. Measure whether full history actually improves delayed-reference correctness relative to a smaller carefully constructed context.
