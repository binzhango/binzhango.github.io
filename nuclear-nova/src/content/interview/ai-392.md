## Question

Design “people you may know” / job-recommendation ranking at a professional network's scale.

## Brief answer

Suggested approach: use graph and semantic candidate generation, then rank for useful reciprocal connections or relevant jobs under privacy and eligibility constraints. “People you may know” and job recommendations share infrastructure but have different objectives and harms. A connection score should not expose sensitive inferred relationships, and a job score should not merely reproduce historical hiring patterns.

## Detailed answer

For connections, retrieve mutual-network and shared-context candidates while honoring blocks, visibility, and contact-import permissions. Rank for likely accepted, meaningful connections and limit repeated unwanted suggestions. For jobs, combine skills, experience, location, preferences, availability, and semantic matching; distinguish eligibility filters from relevance signals.

Log exposure and delayed outcomes, and use temporal splits to avoid graph leakage. Evaluate acceptance quality, successful applications, relevance, and distribution of opportunities rather than raw clicks. Handle new members and new postings through content features and declared preferences. Review privacy-sensitive inferences and fairness with appropriate domain expertise. Provide explanations based on legitimate visible evidence, not hidden sensitive attributes.

### Similarity alone is not the objective

For people suggestions, graph proximity supplies candidates, but repeated unwanted invitations or revealing a hidden relationship can harm users. Respect blocks and visibility before scoring. Rank for reciprocal, meaningful acceptance and include frequency caps; a high click rate on a profile is not proof that a connection should be suggested.

For jobs, first filter explicit constraints such as location eligibility and posting status, then score skills, experience, stated interests, and semantic fit. Do not conflate a historical hiring pattern with a legitimate relevance criterion. Labels arrive at different times—views, applications, interviews, and hires—so choose an objective and observation window deliberately. Temporal graph splits avoid using future connections to predict past ones. New members and postings need content-based candidates and controlled exploration. Evaluate opportunity distribution and downstream outcomes with appropriate review, while explaining recommendations using visible legitimate evidence such as shared skills rather than private inferred attributes.
