## Question

Do you have experience working with APIs? Are you used to working with C-suite executives?

## Brief answer

Answer the two capabilities separately, using real examples: technical API integration and communication with senior decision-makers. For APIs, discuss contracts, authentication, retries and failure handling. For executives, show how you translate technical uncertainty into options, business impact and a clear decision without hiding important limitations.

## Detailed answer

Response framework: choose an API project where you owned more than the happy-path call. Explain versioning, idempotency, rate limits, observability or an incident you resolved. Describe your role precisely; using an SDK is useful experience but different from designing a public API.

For senior stakeholders, describe the decision they needed to make, the tradeoffs you presented and how you handled disagreement or missing information. Show that you can lead with the consequence and supply technical detail when needed. Do not equate executive exposure with agreement or project success.

If you have not worked directly with C-suite leaders, say so and use the closest relevant stakeholder experience. A credible answer identifies transferable skills and a learning plan rather than inventing access to senior leadership.

An illustrative API example could discuss integrating a billing service: validating schemas, handling throttling, preserving idempotency across retries and monitoring reconciliation failures. The useful evidence is the engineering responsibility and a concrete failure handled, not simply naming an SDK. Use an actual project and distinguish implementation from architecture ownership.

An illustrative executive conversation might present three delivery options with cost, timeline and residual risk, then ask for a decision on scope. Explain how technical uncertainty was made legible without hiding it. For instance, “We can launch the read-only workflow now; automated writes need another validation stage” gives a decision-maker a concrete tradeoff.

If direct C-suite exposure is absent, say so and describe analogous work with product leaders, customers or cross-functional owners. Do not inflate seniority of the audience. A strong response demonstrates transferable habits: lead with the decision, support it with evidence, answer technical follow-ups clearly and document commitments afterward.
