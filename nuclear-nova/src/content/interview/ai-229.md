## Question

Design a language translation model / service.

## Brief answer

Choose the language pairs, domain and latency target first, then compare a translation-specialized sequence model with a general multilingual LLM. Preserve numbers, names, formatting and terminology, and evaluate meaning with qualified bilingual review.

## Detailed answer

Train or adapt on aligned, provenance-checked parallel data, with domain terminology and document context where relevant. Deduplicate across splits and separate language-pair performance; a good aggregate score can hide weak low-resource coverage. Use automatic metrics to screen changes but inspect adequacy, fluency and critical factual errors. The service needs language identification, document segmentation, glossary controls, batching and bounded retry behavior. Streaming translation has a delay-versus-revision tradeoff because later words may change the interpretation. Provide a low-confidence review path for consequential documents, and test code-switching and unsupported language pairs explicitly.

### Preserve the invariants of meaning

The service receives source language, target language, text, and optional domain/glossary context. Segment documents without losing cross-sentence references, protect placeholders and markup, translate, then validate required entities, numbers, and formatting. For “do not exceed 5 mg,” negation, amount, and unit are more important than stylistic fluency. Automatic metrics can miss such decisive errors.

An encoder–decoder translation model is efficient for supported language pairs; a general multilingual LLM may handle instructions and context better but can add commentary or paraphrase beyond the task. Compare on native bilingual judgments and domain-specific terminology. For streaming speech, later words can change syntax or meaning, so choose an explicit delay and revision policy rather than promising immediate final translation. Batch independent segments where it reduces cost, with document-level context for consistency. Test names, code-switching, ambiguous terms, low-resource pairs, and unsupported inputs. Return a clear review or unsupported path when evidence does not justify a confident translation.
