## Question

How to prevent forgetting general abilities after domain tuning?

## Brief answer

Reduce forgetting with balanced data mixtures, conservative updates, and repeated evaluation of retained capabilities.

## Detailed answer

Replay suitable general or instruction-following examples alongside domain data. Tune learning rate, training duration, and trainable parameter scope; regularization or distillation can constrain harmful drift. PEFT preserves the original checkpoint for switching back, but an enabled adapter can still degrade general behavior. Track both domain gains and regressions in language, reasoning, formatting, and safety rather than relying on one domain score.

Forgetting is interference from optimizing a new distribution: parameter changes that improve domain examples may degrade representations or behaviors needed elsewhere. A replay mixture keeps some of those other behaviors in the objective. For instance, interleave domain extraction with general instruction-following and reasoning examples rather than training exclusively on one repetitive report template.

Conservative learning rates and earlier stopping limit drift, but the right amount depends on data and objective. Distillation can encourage agreement with the original model on selected inputs, though it can also preserve original mistakes. PEFT makes task updates smaller and separable; disabling an adapter recovers the base model, but the adapted mode still needs regression evaluation.

Measure a tradeoff curve over checkpoints: domain accuracy on one axis and retained capabilities on the other. Select according to explicit acceptance thresholds, not a single averaged score that hides a severe regression. Include prompts outside the domain, changed wording, longer contexts, and structured-output tasks. Keep data and checkpoint versions, and prefer rollback or selective routing when one adapted model cannot satisfy all required behaviors.
