## Question

Design the harmful-content detection system for Facebook and Instagram uploads.

## Brief answer

Use staged multimodal detection with policy-specific thresholds, human escalation and appeals. Combine fast high-recall screening with more expensive contextual review, and separate the model's evidence from the final enforcement decision.

## Detailed answer

Text, images, video and audio require different preprocessing and failure tests. Sample frames and transcript segments carefully so brief harmful events are not missed; preserve evidence timestamps. Train with realistic context and hard negatives, and evaluate false positives across languages and benign communities. Queue reviewer work by severity and uncertainty, keeping an urgent pathway for high-risk material. Track action accuracy and appeal reversals in addition to detector metrics. Version both policy and models, and run shadow/canary tests before changing enforcement thresholds. This is a proposed platform design, not a claim about current Facebook or Instagram internals.

### Detection and enforcement need different evidence

An upload pipeline can run inexpensive hashes and classifiers first, then analyze uncertain or context-sensitive material with larger multimodal models or human review. Video sampling should include temporal coverage and scene changes; one harmful frame or brief spoken statement can be missed by a few evenly spaced thumbnails. Store evidence timestamps and model/policy versions for review.

A classifier score is a noisy signal, while enforcement applies a policy with severity, context, and appeal requirements. A documentary discussing violence is different from glorification, even when similar visual features occur. Set policy-specific thresholds and review queues rather than one universal harmfulness cutoff. Measure false positives on benign communities and languages, false negatives on targeted severe scenarios, reviewer agreement, and appeal reversals. Attackers may alter media or context to evade detection, so refresh adversarial tests and retain random audits. A rollout should distinguish a model change from a policy change, making both outcomes and reversals traceable.
