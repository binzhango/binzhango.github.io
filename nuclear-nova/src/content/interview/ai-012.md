## Question

Compare greedy, beam search, top-k, top-p and temperature sampling. When does each fail?

## Brief answer

Greedy decoding chooses the largest next-token probability; beam search retains several high-scoring sequences. Temperature rescales logits, top-k limits the candidate count, and top-p retains a cumulative-probability nucleus before sampling.

## Detailed answer

Greedy can be repetitive and locally shortsighted. Beam search suits some constrained sequence tasks but often produces bland open-ended text and depends strongly on length normalization. Low temperature concentrates probability; high temperature increases variation and mistakes. Fixed k behaves poorly when uncertainty varies, while top-p adapts candidate count but can still discard useful rare tokens. Sampling controls are applied after masking invalid tokens; define their order and seed when comparing implementations.

Take next-token probabilities $[0.55,0.25,0.15,0.05]$. Greedy always chooses the first token. Top-k with $k=2$ samples only the first two after renormalization. Top-p with threshold 0.9 keeps the first three because their cumulative mass is 0.95. The token that crosses the threshold must be retained. Temperature transforms logits as $z_i/T$ before softmax; it changes relative probabilities rather than directly limiting the number of candidates.

Beam search carries several partial sequences and compares their cumulative log-probabilities. It can avoid a locally attractive prefix that leads to a poor sequence, but raw sums favor short outputs because each additional log-probability is nonpositive. Length penalties alter this preference and must match the task. High-likelihood sequences can still be dull or repetitive because likelihood is not the same objective as usefulness.

For an extraction task, constrained decoding plus low variability is often sensible; for brainstorming, moderate sampling can provide diversity. Neither setting guarantees truth or valid business decisions. Token-level randomness compounds over long outputs, and different kernels can prevent reproducibility even with a fixed seed. Evaluate task success, diversity and failure severity together; tune one parameter at a time before combining truncation strategies.
